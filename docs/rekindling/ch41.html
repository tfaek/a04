Chapter 41: Ghosts: Part Two

It was eerie, hearing the crunch of snow under her boots. The wind had died as Shepard marched across the desolate surface of Alchera. She couldn’t be exactly sure how long the journey had taken, following in the footsteps of her synthetic guide. The prints it left in the cold powder could’ve easily been mistaken for a quarian. But the toes were wrong, a design quirk to differentiate them from their flesh and blood masters, perhaps? The geth had been silent the whole journey, aside from a request to link to Shepard’s hard-suit. The most she got was text updates, or some new notification on her HUD. An observation program had opened on her vitals, keeping a permanent eye. She wasn’t sure if it was flattering or patronising. Mostly it was just surreal.

“So…” She broke the silence. “You got a name?”

“Geth.” The response was instant. She took it in stride.

“Anything more specific, like a unit designation or something?” She tried again.

“No.” The flat answer made her brow furrow.

“Of course not…” She said to herself, shaking her helmet. She’d have to think something up later.

More walking, more silence. But soon metal beams and debris broke up the stark natural landscape. Shepard felt the clench in her gut as they ascended to the edge of the overlooking cliff. The skies had cleared, and a strange calm blanketed the crash site below. Stars flickered, and even the battle she’d fought down amongst the wreck seemed to join in the stillness. Some of the larger pieces still bore the scars of the Collector attack; scorch marks and gouges left by particle beams.

An invisible shudder ran through the commander, hidden beneath armour. All of this, it was still a recent memory. Standing there, looking down on it, she felt… late, for lack of a better word. Something important had happened here, something she knew but couldn’t quite believe. Now the flames were extinguished, and the dust had settled. The old girl’s mangled corpse could’ve rested here for a thousand years, and no one could tell the difference.

“Shepard-Commander, we have arrived.” The geth mentioned it like she hadn’t noticed. “What is the objective?”

“We’re uh…” She swallowed. How would she even explain something like this to a machine? It would be pointless to even try. “We’re looking for dog tags, you know what those are?”

“Yes, we have scanned yours for physical reference.” It didn’t notice the prickling in her stance. “Shall we scan for potential matches at SSV-Normandy crash site?”

“…Yeah, go ahead.” She waved a hand towards the wreck.

“Acknowledged.” One of the geth’s “ear” flaps extended, and its optic widened. For a few seconds the machine’s head slowly tracked along the vista, before coming to a stop. “Scan complete. Twenty possible matches found. Uploading data to your HUD.”

Sure enough, twenty little diamonds appeared in Shepard’s vision. Strewn across the area, some of them still clustered around the larger debris. How many of them had lived to feel the ship shudder as it entered the atmosphere? Had the deafening roar of entry been the last thing they heard, clutching to beams and bolted furniture, praying they would survive? She could almost see it, chunks of hull streaking through the clouds like meteors. Sweat slicked between her skin and the fabric of the under-suit.

“Here’s the plan. We’ll take half each, then do a final sweep, understood?” Shepard’s unsteady tone betrayed her.

“Acknowledged.”

“Alright…” She took a breath. “Let’s get down there.”

A puff and a crunch accompanied the feeling of solid ground. Shepard glanced up fifty meters of cliff; her biotics had certainly gotten stronger: Enough to carry her down without injury. A tremor followed as the geth crashed down beside her, not even scratched by the plummet. Without waiting, it marched off in the direction of “its half” of the objective. She bristled at the idea of a geth disturbing her friends’ remains, but Garrus had been right in his assumption. It would’ve taken hours without help.

She set off, passing the smouldering Kodiak. A quick look inside revealed her fear; not a single signalling rocket had survived. Much to her dismay, comms hadn’t returned either. Despite the clear sky, it was still static. She warily eyed the geth, or heretic corpses as she went. Unconsciously, she touched the silver repair patch on her helmet.

Wind whistled through the battered remains of the old crew deck. So much had been warped and crushed, but enough was recognisable to weigh down Shepard’s steps. The tags of five friends clinked together in her gloved hand. Chase, Barret, Crosby, the Draven sisters. She still remembered confusing them during those first weeks on board. Identical twins, both women gifted enough for roles as flight officers.

Poker Nights back then had been filled with Chase’s “adventures in rural America”, or another near-death scenario in Barret’s short twenty-two year lifespan. Shepard couldn’t remember laughing as much as she did during the Saren mission, bonding with subordinates like that. It hadn’t been long before Garrus and Tali joined them, fitting in at the table like they were born in Alliance blues.

The edges of the small metal tags bit into her squeezing palm, fingers curling. Her breathing hitched, standing there in the lifeless remains of her home. In front of her, pinned down by twisted metal and bent furniture, was Tanaka. So far, mercifully, the tags she’d found were buried in snow without a body to their name. But she could still make out the details of Raymond’s frozen face. Pain… terror, some small hope that the legendary “Commander Shepard” might have saved him, maybe?

Sinking down beside the body, a woman carrying that name reached out to collect the dog tag. Tears drew lines down her cheeks, unbidden. Try as she might, Shepard’s hands wouldn’t stop shaking. The necklace came slowly, catching on stiff uniform and frozen hair. Finally it was free, dangling from her fingers like she’d stolen it. In the silence of the dead, only the woman’s breathing could be heard. Quiet, controlled, like it had been that day.

The day she’d failed all of them…

Heavy footsteps broke the moment. Shepard didn’t turn around, recognising the geth’s presence. She readied for interruption, but it never came. The machine stopped dead at the entrance, simply watching. She didn’t know why it did, but she was grateful.

“All done?” She asked, voice thick.

“We have completed our assigned collection. We wished to assist you with yours.” The geth paused, head tilting past her kneeling form. “We detected an abnormality in your vital signs, are you damaged?”

Hidden from view, Shepard’s lips curled into a grim smile. It was there and gone, replaced by tightness in her chest. She screwed her eyes shut, forcing the last tears out and down her face. Tali should have been here, Garrus too. What right did she have to deny them? They’d had friends amongst her crew, and they deserved to be here with them. Perhaps more than she did. Letting the tension fall from her shoulders, she stood up. At least there was some form of company.

“I’m fine.” She answered, turning to see a sheepish geth waiting for her. It held out its hand, five sets of dog tags nestled within. Not even the slightest sign of mishandling. It was a strange feeling, being grateful as she checked over the names she’d come to know so well. That glowing optic never wavered in its study of her, but not a single prying question came. It waited patiently until she was satisfied. “Let’s keep going.”

She led them towards the others, making idle conversation where she could. The silence ate at her here. The geth confirmed her suspicions about the radio situation. Apparently the heretics had set up a jammer, designed to mimic sensor readings from the surrounding environment. Geth technology put to bad work. She kept her opinions on that to herself. It was a little frustrating when her new companion admitted it hadn’t found the thing.

She tried another personal question, gleaning some interesting knowledge. This chatty geth supposedly ran about ten times more programs within its system than the average trooper. An “independent network” had been its phrase. Any topics actually related to the geth were noticeably avoided. Limited answers, wise on the machine’s part.

One after another, the last five tags were collected. It had taken the two of them less than an hour, and spanned most of the crash site. It never got any easier. The final tag had found itself resting in the old CIC, and a grim process of elimination meant Shepard already knew who’s it was. Of all the crew, Charles Pressly had been the oldest. A man of his time, the old Normandy’s navigator and XO had been more resistant to non-humans than the others. Casting sideways glances at Garrus, or shifting uncomfortably around Tali. It had bothered the commander, more than a little. But Charles had never been a cruel man, or a belligerent one. Slowly but surely, he’d warmed to the newcomers, as he had to Shepard herself.

He’d had kids… waiting for him.

There was no sign of the body, a morbidly welcome thing. Instead the icy remains of the CIC were almost devoid of clutter. Shepard’s boots clanked against the floor, gravity tethering them where magnets had during her last walk here. A flicker of light caught her eye, coming from the far wall opposite the dead galaxy map. Not the subtle glint of metal tags, but the dim orange glow of a data-pad. She reached down to retrieve it, finding Pressly’s tags resting beneath it.

The screen was cracked and a quick link up to her hard-suit showed that hibernation mode was nearly depleted. From what precious little Shepard could make out, it was her old XO’s personal log. The man’s thoughts were garbled by data corruption, but they plotted his course toward growth. It was nice, seeing Charles’ words again. Heading back to the opening, the commander caught the geth staring at her old Mako, half-buried in the snow.

“Looks like we’re done here.” Shepard announced herself to the machine, its optic focusing on the data-pad.

“Corrupted data detected.” It looked up at her. “Shall we attempt file recovery?”

A part of her balked at the idea of a geth reading Pressly’s private thoughts. Not for the first time today, she cursed not having Tali there. Though what little remained of the log certainly didn’t paint Tali in a favourable light, at least in the early entries. Maybe it was the fatigue, or the loneliness of this place, but in the end she relented.

“Sure, go ahead.” She held out the damaged tablet. Without a word, the geth accessed it, lines of code zipping by on the cracked screen. Unaware of exactly how the process worked, she kept her arm outstretched, feeling a little awkward.

“Complete, all files recovered.” Shocked at the speed, Shepard opened the copy sent to her Omni-tool. Scrolling through it, she could see the full context now. None of it seemed forged, or even unlike Pressly. Some of it was lip curling, but most simply made her miss the man. Pocketing the damaged tablet, she looked at the geth, that same strange sensation gripping her again.

“Thanks.” She managed a smile beneath the helmet. Its head-flaps moved slightly, but no response came. “…Let’s get out of here.” She made her way past the geth, toward the centre of the site.

“Shepard-Commander.” Its voice made her stop in her tracks.

“Yeah?” The pause made her tense up as she caught its gaze.

“…We have something to show you.”

No waypoints appeared on the HUD, no little diamonds. Instead the geth walked off, Shepard following close behind. Stepping over debris and past familiar sights, a bit of honest curiosity pushed her on. With the innate sense of threat having waned, now the strange machine was more of a mystery. So far most of their interaction had been giving and receiving orders. It never hesitated, or voiced an opinion, just carried them out with no hesitation. When Shepard asked a question, the geth answered it, no wordplay or mind games.

In those ways, it felt like a machine: Something only pretending to be intelligent. But there were a few moments, this one among them, where things shifted. Where the clockwork stopped and things became unpredictable. She could sense the cunning there, unsaid motivations behind the flashlight eye. A part of her felt insane for thinking it, but she wanted to know more.

When the geth stopped, Shepard looked past it to see their destination. The blood froze in her veins.

In a small cave of bent and twisted hull, was an undisturbed patch of snow. She itched all over, blue eyes resting on the stain of black that vaguely resembled the shape of a woman. Scraps of material were scattered about, with heavier chunks of ceramic and metal resting near the frozen blood. Her stomach churned at the realisation. She recognised a greave, or a piece of boot. It was Alliance gear, the kind worn by N7s.

Since the day she’d first learned of her death, the thought had always haunted her. What had happened before those first moments aboard Lazarus Station, where had her battered, disfigured corpse been resting? Pushing through her light-headedness, now the answer was staring right back at her. Clenching her teeth, she forced her mind to clear, to calm again.

“Why… why’d you bring me here?” She asked, focusing on the tags in her hand. The geth carefully stepped around the stain, not disturbing anything. Shepard followed its movements until she realised something was sitting in the back. Her helmet; propped up like it had been placed there. Gently the machine’s hands lifted it, bringing it back to her.

“This was… yours.” It said looking down at the scratched, blackened thing. After a moment, it handed the helmet over, strangely careful. A tremor shot through her, holding it again.

“That armour you’re wearing.” She looked into the visor. “…You found it here?”

“Yes. This platform was discovered on several occasions, during our search. Damage to chassis necessitated repairs. We… recovered this debris from your hard-suit.” The geth looked back at the dark snow. “When you were not found, we judged Collectors had recovered your body.” It paused for a moment. “Current outcome was… not accounted for.”

“Let’s go.” Shepard said heavily, glancing the stain a final time. “I’ve seen enough.”

The journey back to the scout ship was a silent one. No small talk anymore. With her back to that awful place, Shepard could feel its shadow looming over her. Even with their last memories clutched in her hand, she heard them calling: A chorus that carried on the stiff wind, one that her own wailing soul had been stolen from. The bent, insect-like form of the geth vessel was a welcome one.

She took a seat while her escort propped its physical form against the wall. Under the command of a powerful and alien drive core, the ship disobeyed gravity and took to the sky. Unlike the Kodiak, there was no buffeting of wind here. Shepard sat peacefully, thoughts bottlenecking at the decision that drew near.

She’d kept a close eye, animal instinct demanding her to focus on the geth. It had killed its brothers, escorted her back to the crash site and helped her find every last tag. No signs of suspicion or plot. The potential in having this machine as an ally, the implications… and all she could think about was Tali. There would be no quick explanation, no easy appeal to the mission. She still remembered Haestrom vividly, and she’d only seen the aftermath. She looked down at her old helmet, and then over to the plate bearing an N7 on the geth’s chest. She couldn’t shake the feeling…

“Shepard-Commander!”

The whole ship lurched the side, before jolting violently. Before she could react the geth was there, pinning her down to the seat. Saving her from being flung across the cabin. Her mind caught up, realising they’d been hit by a glancing shot. The smaller, nimble scout ship had barely survived. Only one ship’s guns could be responsible.

“Hail the Normandy, now!” Shepard yelled into her helmet. “Patch me through!”

“Acknowledged! Taking evasive manoeuvres!” It gripped her tight as momentum dampeners struggled to keep them on the floor. The hull screeched as a shot passed through. “Channel open!”

“Normandy, this is Shepard! Cease fire on the geth ship! I repeat, do not fire at the geth ship! I am on board!” She braced, staring the machine in the eye.

Second after second ticked by, but another jolt didn’t come. The silence was deafening.

“Voice pattern analysis complete.” EDI’s soft, even voice came through. “Apologies, Shepard.”

“She’s gonna kill us…” Joker eked out through the radio.

“God damned good to hear your voices.” Shepard chuckled with relief. “Prep the cargo bay and fortify our local-net, we’ve got a friendly geth coming aboard.”

“Uh… a what?”

As the scout ship approached the Normandy’s cargo ramp, the commander did her best to explain. Bearing the brunt of well-intentioned warnings from EDI, and nervous wisecracks from her bearded pilot, eventually the situation was made clear. Not wanting to cause a panic, the order was given to clear the cargo bay. All non-essential staff were headed to the Crew Deck, at least for now.

After that, Shepard decided on exactly who should be present for her arrival. Garrus and Miranda were contacted, along with Mordin and Samara. Cool heads and deadly warriors, things were unlikely to get out of hand. Then came the last, and hardest choice. The Normandy’s… hell, the galaxy’s foremost expert on the geth. No matter what was decided, she knew Tali had to be there. Even beyond Tali’s vast knowledge and technical brilliance, she trusted no one more than her.

The ship settled on the bay floor, weapons and sensors disabled. Gathering up her weapons and the dead’s tags, she clipped the old helmet to her belt. Though she couldn’t see outside due to the geth vessel’s lack of windows, Tali’s locator put her just a few meters away. Already she could feel the tension building inside her. As for the geth itself, her orders were simple. Stay out of sight, and wait until signalled. While the choice of its acceptance would need to be discussed, Shepard was intent on having this whole encounter run smoothly. The moment called for calm, and she would have calm.

Tali stomped off towards the elevator. Her long legs carried her effortlessly across the near-empty cargo bay. Behind her lay the wretched, misshapen form of a geth troop ship, cunningly fitted with a compartment for organics. Icicles still pricked up her spine, just being so close to it. She’d hated it, but could bear it. Seeing Jane emerge from its belly had been sickening, the thought that Jane had been trapped inside for even a moment. It was an awful thought.

Her joy and relief had been cut short when that thing had followed behind. Her forehead still throbbed, the animal deep inside her still calming, returning to the depths. Everything Jane had said, so much of it beyond what she could believe, and it all evaporated in the presence of that monster. It even wore Jane’s armour, like a fucking battle trophy.

Then came the “discussion”. Mordin, Samara, that Cerberus bitch… she couldn’t believe what she’d heard out of them: Tactical advantages, mission priorities, technology interests. Only Garrus had voiced his disapproval, if just once. Then his support had dwindled to an understanding look. Hearing Jane talk about the geth standing beside her like it was some new pet… it’d been almost too much for her. But all of it was made so much worse, by the machine’s constant staring. The moment it had noticed her, the soulless glowing optic hadn’t left. She’d felt her setae stand on end, having it be so close.

“Tali!” She heard Jane’s voice close behind, boots heavy against the metal.

Teeth bared, she opened the elevator doors with a thought, effortlessly hacking in. She was through them in seconds, already sending the close command. She heard them hiss, then whine and clunk. Low grunts accompanied the straining mechanism, and the glow of biotic blue filled the compartment. Then the boots were behind her, doors finally closing. They’d barely moved an inch before everything went dark, and she saw her own hack overridden by EDI, on the captain’s authority. She stood there, glaring holes into the wall. Her face burned.

“We’re not even going to talk about this?” Jane asked, her voice calm. A spike of rage shot through Tali.

“Talk?” She spun around, looking Jane dead in the eye. “You brought a geth onto the Normandy, Our home! And you want to talk about it?”

“I know what I did, Tali.” Jane’s tone sharpened. “You’re not gonna consider why I did it?”

“Because it can mimic speech.” She answered dismissively, hearing the machine’s grating buzz again. “It’s an infiltrator, Jane! Trying to gain your trust so it can-”

“What, kill me?” Jane finished the gruesome thought. “Tali it saved my life down there; fought other geth to do so.”

“There are no other geth!” She roared. “They’re a hive mind, just drones following orders! If you saw this thing fight other units it’s because they wanted you to see it! To convince you that you could trust it!”

“For what purpose? So I’d bring it here? EDI controls every single transmission that goes in or out. It’d be sharing this place with some of the most dangerous people in the galaxy.”

“None of that matters if you trust it.” She said, anger bleeding into concern. “All it needs is one opportunity, for you to lower your guard. And we could all pay the price for it.”

Standing there in the low light with Jane, she finally took in the details of the commander’s injuries. A silver streak curved over the brow of Jane’s helmet, it made her throat tighten. She’d seen Garrus glance at it more than once, not quite hiding his discomfort. Jane hadn’t said anything, but the idea of a sniper’s bullet coming so close… it forced a shaky breath from her lungs. There were spots of silver all over, geth repairs.

She’d recognised the material immediately, seen it countless times. The machine had fixed the damage, but hadn’t cleaned away the blood. The left side of Jane’s face was red, burned by the freezing cold. Jane had endured hell down there, while she’d been trapped here, helpless. All so that these fucking things could play puppet show with Jane…

“What if you’re wrong?” Jane asked, as their eyes met. Tali blinked in surprise.

“What?”

“Back on Alchera, it told me about a… schism, that happened. It said that the geth we’d been fighting are a rogue faction, “heretics” that chose to follow Sovereign. But the rest of the geth, most of the geth, wanted no part of it.”

“It’s lying.” She said simply. “Keelah, geth can’t even share information like that! They don’t have the ability to differ in opinion!”

“You also said their trooper hardware couldn’t handle running more than a hundred programs at once.” Jane countered. “You saw it yourself, over a thousand, running perfectly.”

“That doesn’t… it’s been three hundred years! It’s just an advanced platform!”

“You’re right, it’s been three hundred years.” Jane agreed. “Which means all the both of us have to go on is second-hand information. Information that could be out of date, or not even accurate in the first place.” She stepped closer to Tali. “But right now, we have a live geth, one that wants to talk. We could finally learn something about them.”

“Or it could give the geth access to the Normandy’s stealth drive!” She pointed out, fighting back a cough. “It could scan one of our Omni-tools, or the ship’s components, and the next thing we know there’s an entire geth fleet that we can’t see on sensors anymore. You have no idea what its intentions are!”

“You weren’t there.” Jane huffed, frustrated. “You didn’t see what it-”

“No!” She cut Jane off. “I wasn’t there!”

The fight in Jane’s eyes flickered and dwindled. Tali watched the woman deflate, shoulders sinking. Jane’s lips pressed thin, giving her a small nod. Silence took the intimate space, pressing in on them from all sides, thickening the air. After a few moments, she couldn’t bear it any longer. She wanted to close the distance, to embrace the woman she cared for so much. The woman whose curiosity, who’s open mind had made all that they shared possible.

But now those qualities she loved were being used against Jane… against all of them. Before she could force the first step, Jane pulled something from a belt clip. Her eyes widened in realisation.

“While we were down there, the geth took me to see something.” Jane’s breathing shortened. “There was… blood, and scraps of my old suit…” Her expression tightened. She looked down at the old helmet gripped in her hand. “I hadn’t mentioned anything about wanting to see it, but it took me there anyway. Handed me my old helmet. It was… reverent, almost.”

She held out the scorched piece of armour. Tali’s throat went dry as she took it. Seeing it like this, it brought back a flood of memories. Burning halls, and screams: The sting of tears against her sensitive cheeks, day after day of agony. Now a piece of that past was in her hands, tangible.

“Why would it do that?” Jane asked, almost lost in thought.

At first, the question was only a distant sound, slowly encroaching from the corners of her mind. The closer it got, the more the truth balked against it. This geth was trying to manipulate Jane, to gain her trust. It was a machine, no spontaneity, only intention. There were factors it was accounting for, variables at play that just… weren’t obvious.

“Jane… I know you want to believe it.” She kept her voice steady. “But it’s not true. This geth, it doesn’t see a person when it looks at you. It sees a logic puzzle.” Jane let out a long breath.

“Maybe you’re right.” She said, voice low. “But if you’re not, then we could be losing the one chance to truly understand them. If what this geth says is true, it could be an opportunity to change how they see us. It told me the heretics were just five percent of the total geth population…”

She saw her own horror reflected in Jane’s eyes. “…Tali, we can’t fight something like that. It’d be catastrophic. Our best chance to avoid war is down there in the cargo bay right now, waiting for my decision. I can’t just ignore it.”

Tali’s fingers squeezed around Jane’s old helmet. She looked down at it, gently rubbing a thumb across the dirty visor glass. Her face tightened, visions of death haunting her every thought. She saw Riah, those cold metal claws sinking into her. She swallowed.

“Jane…” She blinked away the wetness in her eyes. “You know I trust you. That I…” The word danced on the tip of her tongue, just beyond reach. “...That I would follow you, anywhere.”

She stood tall, meeting Jane’s gaze. “But I’m asking you, please don’t do this. I can’t spend every minute worrying when this thing will turn on us. Because it will, Jane, just like Cerberus will. I… can’t go through this again.”

Tali could see it, clear as day on Jane’s face. Jane knew what she meant, the tragedy she feared the most. It felt wrong, using it against her, using Jane’s feelings for her as a cudgel. Shame wriggled in her gut, nearly breaking her composure. But she held firm. Jane was more important than Tali’s feelings. For a moment, she saw that familiar shine in Jane’s eyes. That connection they always shared together, two halves of one whole. Hope soared, before sparking and burning. Those warm eyes became glassy, and her shoulders straightened. Tali’s heart sank as Jane spoke.

“I’m sorry.” Jane said quietly, looking back at her. “I can’t let fear, anyone’s fear, destroy this chance. We'll take every precaution, any suggestion you make, I'll implement. But until then, the geth stays.”

Rage, pain, and terror roiled up inside her. Tali grit her pointed teeth, resisting every screaming urge to lash out. She wanted to yell and curse, to tear apart the entire compartment with her bare hands. The thought of that cold monster sharing the same ship as the people she treasured, having to sit politely with the thing that robbed her entire people of their lives. In the end, the only tension visible was in her hands, squeezing the old helmet between them.

“Right.” She nodded mechanically. “Well, that’s that then.” A moment of silence passed between them, utterly devoid of comfort or ease. It was an absence she’d never felt before. “I should get back to my shift.” She handed the helmet back to Jane, before retreating back a step. “I’ll need to overhaul our system security, it’s going to take a few hours.”

“Tali-”

“Please, Jane just… let me work.” She saw whatever protests Jane had vanish under her inflection.

The order was given, and EDI released the elevator. Seconds of silence ticked by like lifetimes in that cramped little box. When the doors finally opened, it was a mercy. She was out and around the corner before the doors even finished closing. All without a backwards look. She couldn’t, not after what she’d just heard. Having to stand there, and listen to all this insanity, it felt like she was drowning.

The hum of the drive core called to her beyond, promising to be the refuge it always was. Even now she still felt shaky on her feet. A first stumbling step quickly became a strong, confident lope as she passed the sub-deck stairwell and entered Engineering. Scottish and American accents mixed together in energetic bickering; it was a welcome relief: A little space station of normalcy, amidst a void of madness.

“Hey Chief.” Fluttered over from the two humans, before returning to their dogged discussion.

Tali allowed herself a momentary smile as she brought up the core readouts. She tabbed over to the Normandy’s internal wireless network, seeing EDI’s icon already present and at work. An AI keeping tabs on an AI… Keelah. Syncing up her neural interface, three-fingered hands danced across the holograms. She brought up every piece of geth data she’d ever collected, running them through simulation after simulation.

She knew the geth’s propagation tactics, what to look for during a system siege. Soon even EDI took a backseat, chiming in with text suggestions as the furious quarian worked. Ken and Gabby were still in the dark like the rest of the crew. As much as Tali would’ve delighted in complaining to them, until Jane gave the announcement, her lips were sealed.

Instead, she pulled up every piece of malware she’d ever written, already preparing new ones. Systematically, she rigged every node, piece of wireless hardware, and vulnerability she could find with traps. Logic bombs set to deliver a veritable hydra of viruses for the machine to deal with. She knew full well it could, geth were masters of hacking themselves. And whatever this model was, no doubt a thousand programs would prove difficult.

But she didn’t need to kill its software. No, she just needed to slow it down, lock it in place for a few seconds while every alarm and communicator sounded at once. Someone, somewhere would be close enough to finish it off. She only hoped it would be her.

Until then, she would work. Work until the tar in her belly relented; work until the red left her vision. Something had turned, deep inside her, and she knew it. She had to keep it focused on the real enemy… and not let even a piece of it find the woman she loved.
