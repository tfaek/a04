Chapter 27: Old Friends

It had taken another two days to reach the Tasale system. Multiple Relay transfers had shaken the Normandy, straining her hull and engines. When she emerged from the jump, another batch of systems had failed. Her dutiful engineers had been on their feet until the frigate was within sub-light distance of Illium. By the time Joker was verifying the ship’s identity and intentions, Shepard and her team were already on their way to the Kodiak. As damaged as she was, the Normandy couldn’t hope to make an atmospheric transfer to the planet’s surface, let alone dock. Instead, the plan was to rendezvous with a private shipyard in orbit. Cerberus had already secured the loan, filling the place with their engineers and resources. The crew were offered accommodation there, along with a rather disheartened Joker. As for the others, their lodgings would be planet-side, in the city of Nos Astra.

Bidding her ship farewell, Shepard looked over the dossier reports she had received as the shuttle began its descent. These new files were nearly as anaemic as Jack’s had been. The first was an asari warrior, a matriarch and powerful biotic. Finding a single asari on a world full of them could prove difficult, but another heavy-hitter would certainly be useful. The other was an assassin. A Drell she’d never heard of. By all accounts a master assassin, with a history of successful kills and not a single botched mission.

The idea couldn’t find a way to sit comfortably with the commander. On the one hand, a skillset like that could be invaluable, while not putting Kasumi in unnecessary danger again. But on the other, mercenaries were only ever one payment away from betrayal. She pursed her lips, focusing her attention on the orange screen of her Omni-tool. The black outside clawed at the corners of her vision, still waiting for her. The feeling hadn’t gone away, she would have to confront it eventually. But soon the buffeting of clouds jolted the cabin, and daylight filled the window.

Breathing easy again, she turned off the screen. From what she knew, the details would be explained by their contact: A local to Illium. Intel had been oddly tight-lipped about the whole thing, “compartmentalisation”, Miranda had called it. More than it annoyed her; Shepard wondered if it was a symptom of lost faith. Horizon had been a mess, enough to completely derail the main plan of the mission. Was the Illusive Man making a point? Was it punishment for failure? He’d been cordial during their conversation, but the old terrorist didn’t quite have the poker face he thought he did.

Shepard could tell the costs and delays were getting to him, maybe even enough to re-negotiate the terms of their agreement. The thought was enough to make her skin itch. She rubbed it away, shutting out the noise from her mind. Instead she glanced the friends at her side. Garrus was serene as usual, silently taking in the sights below. Tali kept close, rubbing shoulders while she gazed out. Those shining eyes were pressed up against her helmet, a look Shepard hadn’t seen since her pilgrim days: The commander’s lips quirked into a smile, readying the pry the young woman for conversation when a voice sounded from behind.

“Quit starin’ at me you big, scaly fuck!” Jack’s husky voice bellowed. All three of them turned to see the commotion. Taking up the centre of the cabin were the two newest additions. Jack was sat in one of the seats, her sights set on Grunt. The krogan stood across from her, his reptilian eyes unblinking as he stared. Seemingly unaware of the woman’s problem, his wide face pulled into a confused frown. For a moment the two sat in silence, glaring at one another. Jack’s temper was slipping as she snarled back at him.

Feeling a sigh brewing, Shepard readied to diffuse the moment when Grunt simply snorted, shifting his view to Jacob instead. The Cerberus agent grimaced a little, settling in while the krogan glared at him. Why he was simply looking at people, the commander couldn’t even begin to figure out. But the yelling had stopped, so the confused trio turned back to the window. By now Nos Astra was in full view, a gleaming example of asari architecture and culture: A huge, sprawling metropolis with towering white skyscrapers. Each building had sweeping curves, tapering upward like mountain peaks. Gardens and parks covered the open platforms and rooftops, the greenery breaking up the artificial landscape. Brilliant neon lights completed the vista, hinting at a vibrant nightlife.

“Keelah…” Tali said quietly, taking it all in.

“You ever been here before?” Shepard asked, smile returning.

“Once…” Her tone became sombre for a moment. “I didn’t get much time to enjoy it.” Shepard could sense there was more, but she wouldn’t press her.

“Got into my first bar fight here.” Garrus interjected, lifting the mood. “Found out the hard way how fast salarians are.”

“You’ve been to Illium?” Shepard cocked an eyebrow.

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Garrus feigned hurt at the question. After a cheeky flare of his mandibles, he looked out the window. “Back before I joined C-Sec. Our patrol frigate put down here for a few days, supposedly the safest world in the cluster…” The turian paused, his gaze hardening on the city below. “…We should be careful, Shepard. Underneath the glitz and glamour, it’s no safer here than Omega.”

Shepard gave the turian a nod. Though she’d never been, stories about Illium were common. One of the galaxy’s tax havens, a place where enterprise had flourished unimpeded. Where Noveria had been little more than a planetary black-site for corporations, this place was something truly special: The most successful colony in the Terminus Systems. However, beyond the reach of most galactic authority this world was still wild. Greed made conflict inevitable, and a jewel like this was built on it. A quick check of her Omni-tool confirmed her armour and weapons were functional. Behind the three of them, every member of the ground team was armed and ready. The commander’s orders were clear; there was to be absolutely no trouble with the law. Civilised worlds required finesse, something not all members of her growing squad sported.

The shuttle touched down on the flight terminal’s landing pad. The commander led her crew of ten out and across to the authorities: A squad of fifteen LOKI robots, marching in lockstep with heavy weapons at the ready. They were led by a single asari, who approached with a welcoming smile on her face. A quick gesture over Shepard’s shoulder kept the squad calm, while Garrus and Tali both readied themselves, hands resting near their guns. Miranda slotted in between the commander and her turian friend. She traded a quick, knowing look with Shepard just as the mechanical soldiers stomped to a halt.

“Commander Shepard. It’s an honour to meet you.” The asari bowed her head. “My name is Careena, I’m here on behalf of the Illium Trade Bureau to welcome you to Nos Astra.” Her greeting was punctuated by the LOKIs’ head displays flashing neutral white.

“That’s a lot of firepower for a friendly welcome.” Shepard noted, eyeing the pistol slung on the hip of Careena’s dress.

“Here on Illium we respect the right of all people to defend themselves.” She replied cheerily. “Vigilance is the price of freedom, one we’re quite happy to pay. To that end, you and your companions don’t have to worry about your weapons and armour. As long as you follow the law, you’re as safe here as you would be on any asari world.”

“Thank you Careena.” Miranda returned the bowing gesture. “We have no intentions of breaking the Bureau’s laws.” The asari beamed at the reassurance.

“I assume there’s some documents I need to sign? Customs to go through?” Shepard posed the question; unsure exactly what Careena was here for, other than as a walking threat. She was surprised when Careena shook her head.

“Not at all, Commander. Customs requires no active participation, and all your documents have been signed and cleared. We have your rooms ready and waiting.”

“Our… rooms?” Shepard exchanged glances with the other three.

“At the Ascension Hotel, all paid for in advance. Your sponsor requested suites for you and your team. It’s a generous offer, normally the place is completely booked out.”

“Who is this sponsor?” Shepard felt exasperation take hold when a mischievous glint crossed the asari’s eyes.

“I’m afraid I’m not at liberty to say.” She answered, her smile widening. “However, she has requested you meet with her. I can take you whenever you’re ready.”

The commander felt that familiar sensation on the back of her neck. Pieces on the board were being moved, while she was kept in the dark. Setting her and her team up in a high-class hotel, just to wait out a few days of repairs? It felt far too convenient. She wasn’t sure if it was Cerberus, or some third party having caught wind of her return. The kind of enemies she had possessed very indirect means, with a machine’s patience to match. Without knowing the play, the best she could do was to make her own certainty. With a disarming smile, she gave a nod of approval.

“I’m ready now.” She answered. “However, These two will be joining me.” She gestured to Garrus and Tali, dampening Careena’s glee.

“Uh, I’m not sure if that’s-”

“No doubt your sponsor wants me to feel comfortable?” Shepard interrupted the asari. “You’re looking at my comfort, right here.”

“O-of course…” She gave an uneasy nod, before returning to her natural pleasance. “Not a problem ma’am.”

“Great to hear.” Shepard exchanged a quick look with Miranda before stepping forward with her friends. Suddenly Careena became animated, as though she’d just remembered something.

“Oh, Commander.” She played off the moment. “My apologies, but since Illium is a free-trade world, I will have to see your acquisition papers.” Her tone was expectant, with a hint of something else.

“My… what papers?” Shepard asked. Careena seemed puzzled for a moment.

“Excuse me.” Careena said bashfully. “I mean the contract for service. Just the outline is all right. Terms of agreement, length of service, health concerns, allergies, things like that.”

“Health concerns, allergies… I didn’t bring a pet with me.” Shepard answered, completely stumped.

“Oh no, no ma’am. We don’t use… verbiage like that here. I’m referring to your servant.” Careena looked hopeful, but the commander’s eyes widened.

“My what?”

“Your… servant.” The asari gave a polite gesture to Tali. Utterly lost for words, Shepard exchanged a look with the astonished quarian. In that moment when their eyes met, she saw an absolute maelstrom of rage roiling behind her faceplate.

“What did you call me?” Tali roared, her shining eyes narrowing to slits.

She stalked forward, fists clenched before the LOKIs came to life, pointing their guns. In a flash Shepard caught her by the shoulder, keeping her from crossing into the robots’ kill zone. Careena held up her arms defensively, completely shocked.

“I-I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to… I wasn’t given the details of your arrangements, Commander.” She gave Shepard a pleading look.

“Well let’s clear that up right now.” Shepard softened her grip on Tali, her hand running down the quarian’s arm as she moved forward. “Tali’Zorah is not a servant. She’s a free woman, and if she’s shown another ounce of disrespect, I’ll be taking this to the Bureau.” Her threatening tone got a hurried nod.

“Y-yes, of course.” Careena agreed. “Goddess, I am so sorry ma’am. Please accept this discount, citywide for the duration of your stay here. The Illium Trade Bureau abhors all forms of racism, and we endeavour to make all species comfortable and welcome.”

“Just, take us to this sponsor.” Shepard got the word in, burying the conversation.

Dismissing the robots, Careena led the three of them toward a waiting sky-car. A larger shuttle pulled in behind it, ready to transport the others. Garrus took the front seat, while the embarrassed asari got behind the wheel. Offering a hand, Shepard helped Tali into the back. She’d taken it silently, her eyes softening for a split second. The vehicle’s canopy door clicked shut, cutting off the whipping of wind outside. With a muffled whine, the landing platform began to fall away, quickly becoming another part of the scenery. The cabin filled with soft music, enough to mitigate the silence.

It was another planet, another culture. Shepard had to remind herself, she had to stave off the urge to dress her concierge down any further. Time spent in Alliance and Council space had lulled her into a false understanding of the galaxy. Slavery was the tool of criminals and despots, like the Hegemony. It was branding and neural clamps, done only to helpless victims: A horrible, completely illegal practice. But this wasn’t Council space; hell it wasn’t even really asari space. A “free-trade world”, it shouldn’t have surprised her.

Shepard looked over at Tali. She was looking out the window, her body atypically still and rigid. Shepard could see her hands clenched in her lap, pushing through the anger. Shepard felt a pit in her stomach. Had she said enough? Why didn’t she realise it earlier? She should’ve stopped Careena before she ever got to those words. Damn it, she knew what the wider galaxy thought of quarians. She had vowed, since those first days together, that she wouldn’t let that poison touch Tali. That there would be respect aboard her ship, and wherever she went with her. But ten minutes on this planet, and she’d already stumbled right into it.

The awkward flight was mercifully brief. Before long Careena’s forced bubbliness piped up to point out their destination. Another high-rise tower, impeccably clean and accented by green balcony gardens. A ring of landing pads circled the building about halfway up, with another ring of them at the very top. Shepard felt the car bank upward, the orange afternoon sun dancing across the windows of every floor. The momentum dissipated as they cleared the roof, and the car settled into a gentle hover as it descended. When the canopy opened, all four occupants were treated to a small forest of trees. The concierge was quick on her heeled feet, leading the trio into the rooftop park.

Dappled light poured in through the emerald leaves, catching the brightly coloured alien flora around them. Reds and blues stood out against the greenery, with strange shapes transporting them far from the throng of the city. Walking briskly along the path, Shepard could hear piping and peeping overhead. Hoping to spot something, she noticed Tali’s absence from her side. Turning back she saw her, stopped a few meters behind. Tali was watching a pair of colourful, four-winged birds. The tiny creatures wheeled and flittered after each other, locked in an aerial dance. The quarian was mesmerised, completely entranced by the sight. For a moment Shepard simply watched her, seeing how the light caught the fabric of her realk: A splash of dazzling violet. By the time she took a step toward Tali, the birds vanished into the branches. The moment was over, but Tali’s loping gait was looser now. She caught up quickly, a familiar bounce in her step.

The trio followed their guide through the rooftop park, into a lavish elevator. Clearly there was no expense spared for a place like this. Garrus resumed his station as bulwark, while Tali leaned against the wall, farthest from Careena. Shepard kept close, distracting herself with the intricate asari script etched into the railings. Things were starting to heat up again, with everyone in such close proximity. Just before the beginnings of a lecture could take root in the commander’s mind, the soft whirring stopped, punctuated by a chime. Released again into a generous hallway, they strode quickly across the designer metal floor toward one of the offices. Just outside, another asari worked at a desk, seemingly aware of their presence without even a glance up. Shepard caught a wry smile cross her face.

“Ma’am, they’re here.” She spoke softly into her terminal. A moment later she emerged from the desk, waving them toward the open door with a bow. Careena followed suit, mirroring her.

Without a word, they entered. The door slid shut behind them, while the large window beckoned. Slowly emerging out from the entrance, the sheer size of the office was impressive. The style was familiar, but enough details were different to denote an asari taste. The large main desk was adorned with a number of strange artefacts, something about them poked at Shepard’s mind. On the far side, a small staircase led up to a second floor. This place almost seemed more like an apartment than an office. There was a small kitchen, and even a few soft chairs. The whole place balanced perfectly between professional chic, and somewhere lived in. On the bench was a bottle, with a pair of glasses sitting empty. It seemed their host was planning something friendly.

Just then a sound from upstairs caught her ear. Footsteps carried from the staircase, and soon a figure came down. She was an asari; her azure skin sparkled in the light. The woman was dressed in a white ballistic coat, accented with blue. A mixture of fashion and function, it could clearly take a few bullets. She was tense, tapping down the steps before turning to face them. Then Shepard saw it: The unmistakeable markings on her brow-line, the dusting of freckles across her cheeks. Eyes like twin sapphires found Shepard’s, and her slender hands covered her mouth. The commander felt Garrus and Tali stiffen up in surprise.

“Shepard…” Liara’s soft voice nearly whispered Shepard’s name. Tears were already welling in her eyes as Shepard approached.

She felt Liara’s arms wrap tightly around her, a few soft gasping sobs breathed into her shoulder. Shepard returned the embrace, overjoyed to see another old friend: After a long week of pain and failure, this meeting finally felt like a reprieve. Away from Cerberus, away from the Normandy and the mission, she could just be here, surrounded by the people she trusted with her life. For a brief and fleeting moment, it was like nothing had changed. It was a longing that tugged at her heart more and more these days, one that drove the air from her lungs, and wetted her eyes. She squeezed Liara back, letting the veneer of the commander go. Soon enough Liara’s composure returned, and she broke away with a smile.

“My sources said you were alive… but I…” She struggled to focus her words. “…It’s wonderful to see you.”

“Wonderful, she says.” Garrus gave the asari a chuckle, pulling her attention from Shepard.

“Goddess… Garrus, Tali?” She fled Shepard’s arms to embrace them both. “I… it’s been far too long.”

“Two years.” Garrus noted, awkwardly patting her back. “You look good.”

“I missed you.” Tali said quietly, matching Liara’s enthusiastic squeeze.

Taking the opportunity for a breath, Shepard enjoyed the scene. Ignoring her turian friend’s pleading look, she left him trapped in the hug. It hadn’t really occurred to her that there would be a reunion like this, part of her wasn’t sure if they’d ever cross paths again. But here they were, alive and together. Her serenity was intruded upon by memories of Kaidan, the words they’d exchanged only a few days ago. Not for the first time, she kicked herself for them. She could’ve handled things better, she should have. Instead she would have to hope things could be repaired in the future, if fate was kind enough to grant another chance. Perhaps she’d played that card already.

“Please. Come, sit.” Liara waved her hand, biotic fields jostling the chairs into a rough circle. Feeling suddenly quite obliged, Shepard led the others over to them. She saw the asari look over to the bottle and glasses, undecided. With a subtle shake of her crested head, she left them there, instead claiming the seat across from Shepard. The leather felt soft beneath her armour, a welcome change from fighting and running.

“So…” Liara started, her hands clasped together. “I imagine you probably have some questions.”

“I uh, I’m not sure where to begin.” Shepard admitted. “This isn’t exactly where I expected to find you.” She looked around the impressive office again.

“Yes…” Liara’s cheeks flushed slightly. “A consequence of my mother’s estate, mostly. As her only child, I’ve inherited a great deal of her wealth, along with a few close contacts. I’ve been honing my skills as an information broker. So far it’s granted me much more access and utility than my previous…”

She trailed off, looking down at her feet. Her fingers interlocked, knuckles growing pale.

“I’m sorry…” Her gaze drifted to Garrus and Tali. “I… I meant to keep in contact. After what happened…” For a moment she seemed to blank out. “Goddess… has it really been two years?”

Nobody knew what to say. Shepard’s instincts pushed her to absolve Liara, to brush past the whole thing like nothing happened at all. But she knew this apology wasn’t even really for her. The tone of Liara’s voice, the way she avoided eye contact. Shepard knew the feeling well, even now it gnawed in the back of her head.

“No point in dredging up the past.” Garrus finally spoke. “We all muddled through it, best we could.”

“You’re right.” Liara almost gasped the words. “We shouldn’t dwell on it now.” She took a breath, forcing a smile. “How have you been? I didn’t know Shepard had anyone with her from before.”

“She couldn’t tie her boots without us.” The turian scoffed, glancing at Shepard. “…Besides, it’s the least we could do.”

“…Oh?” Liara saw the scars on his plated face, her question obvious.

“She saved our lives.” Tali answered it, giving the commander an invisible smile.

“Tactically, I might add.” Garrus joked. “Gave herself the edge in the looks department.” Any solemnity the asari still clung too evaporated in a giggle.

“Well, it’s good to see some things haven’t changed.” Liara’s expression shifted from happy to quizzical. “Though I wasn’t expecting to be contacted by Cerberus.”

For just a second, something pricked up in the back of Shepard’s mind. She took in the gentle warmth of her old friend’s face. Liara had always been a little different, since the day they’d met. She was quiet and reserved, tactless from years spent indulging a solitary hobby. Ash had noticed her stunted social skills, eager to prod the asari for laughs. Shepard hadn’t been sure what to make of it then, but that forthrightness had certainly come up on the topic of certain feelings. It could be taxing, and no doubt embarrassing for Liara, but it was honest.

Though Shepard’s feelings had been… elsewhere, Liara’s honesty made her a close friend, even after only a few weeks together. But now, at this very moment, Shepard felt it. Something was off, or maybe… she just thought it was. Shepard cast the thought away. It was stupid to assume nothing would change with Liara, no different than Tali or Garrus. It had been two years. Not to mention that between the two of them, she was the one with questionable alliances.

“Yeah.” She rubbed the back of her neck. “It’s a long story, to say the least. They’re helping me hunt down the Collectors.”

“The colony attacks.” Liara nodded, her brow furrowed. “I’d only heard rumours. You think that…” She glanced to the doorway, her voice lowered. “…Are the Reapers involved?”

“Definitely.” Shepard answered, her face grim. “Tali found something on a pirate raid a few months ago: Another Reaper artefact, controlling the slavers. The footage showed the Collectors taking the prisoners, just humans. Combine that with these attacks and… this thing that attacked us on Horizon. I’m certain of it.”

“A thing?”

“One of the Collectors, but… different. It was glowing, with these bright, golden eyes. That voice… when it spoke to me, it sounded like Sovereign.” Shepard suppressed a shudder at the memory.

“Wait, it spoke to you?” Garrus asked.

“Yeah, back when we fought the praetorian. You didn’t hear it?” Shepard could see the confusion on Garrus’ face, but something else shone behind Tali’s mask: Realisation perhaps, or just horror. She noticed Liara looking at her intently.

“We fought them off on Horizon, but not without losing a lot of colonists. My team just scraped through.” She sighed in frustration. “We weren’t ready for them. That’s why I’m here, I’m hoping to change that.”

“Of course.” Liara nodded. “I’ve been looking into the dossiers your associates sent me.”

“The matriarch and the drell.”

“Yes. I’ve already made contact with Samara, the matriarch in your dossier. She came here looking for a fugitive, though she has been rather reserved with her information. What I do know is that circumstances prevent her from simply pursuing this person alone. I was able to offer help, in exchange for a meeting with you when you arrived.” Liara looked exasperated while recounting the experience.

“Something wrong?”

“There were some details I discovered for myself. The most troublesome being… that she is a justicar.”

“Is that some kind of law enforcement?” Shepard’s guess got a chuckle out of her.

“Not exactly. The Justicars of Tevura are a monastic order. They swear an oath to the goddess of law, one that compels them to punish the guilty and protect the innocent. Their code supersedes legal complexities and popular interpretations of morality; they are very… black and white in their thinking. Most are older matrons or matriarchs, and they are all extremely dangerous.”

“If she’s so dedicated, doesn’t that make her a dead end then?”

“Normally yes. However, she has agreed to offer her aid in exchange for help locating this fugitive she’s hunting. Whoever they are, she seems determined to find them.”

“Well, that makes things a little simpler.” Shepard nodded in approval. “Anything on the drell?”

“Thane Krios. “Master assassin” is a fitting title. He’s been operating for over twenty years, with a perfect kill record. Weapons expert, biotic, and frustratingly difficult to locate.” Liara huffed. “So far my sources have him here on Illium, likely in this city. His latest target will be here soon, but he’ll be impossible to find before then.”

“Naturally.” Garrus groaned. “Who’s the target?”

“An old friend of ours. Nassana Dantius.”

“That diplomat from the Citadel? The one who had us kill her sister?” Tali’s question got a nod from Liara.

“Exactly, though her status as a diplomat has been revoked. It seems she couldn’t resist from further “ensuring” her position.”

“More killings…” Shepard surmised. Liara nodded.

“Nassana returned here to run her mother’s company, Dantius Holdings: One of the largest mega-corporations in the Asari Republics. She’s been CEO for the past two years, expanding the company’s interests in the Terminus Systems, far from the prying eyes of the law. Have you heard of the Eclipse mercenary company?”

“You could say that.” Garrus answered, sharing a look with Shepard.

“Dantius Holdings is their benefactor. Here on Illium, Eclipse is a legal and registered LLC, with protection from Nassana. From what my sources could find, she’s been using their criminal enterprises to fund her own schemes, along with crushing any competition. Several journalists and public detractors of hers have disappeared.”

“If you know about this, is there anything you could do?” Shepard asked. Liara frowned.

“Unfortunately, her mother is a well-respected and influential matriarch. Melassania Dantius. She’s a war veteran, with close ties to asari High Command, and the owner of Dantius Holdings. She’s been a key player on Thessia for centuries. Any threats to Nassana would be a threat to her directly, I don’t have the resources to withstand such a reprisal.” Her eyes fell. “…That must sound cowardly, considering your mission.”

“Better to live and fight another day.” Shepard said, giving her a reassuring smile.

“I have managed to find a connection between your dossiers, however.” Liara perked up a little. “Over the past few months, several high-profile rivals of Nassana were found dead. Samara mentioned her fugitive was a serial killer. Nassana was also recently seen with an unknown associate, the two have been inseparable. If she is our justicar’s fugitive, then she would need someone like Miss Dantius for protection.”

“Hiring a serial killer for hostile takeovers… why doesn’t that surprise me.” Shepard said with disgust. “So what are you thinking, two birds with one stone?”

“Yes. Nassana is scheduled to return to Nos Astra tomorrow, no doubt with her assassin. She has a penthouse at the Dantius Towers. They’re well defended, but tower one is still under construction. Every night there is a change in worker shifts, if Thane intends to kill her, he will do it then.”

“Security?”

“Eclipse, a full company of them. However if you can convince Samara to help you, they shouldn’t be a problem.” Liara said confidently. “Justicars are legendary warriors.”

“Alright, then we’ve got a solid plan for these dossiers.” Shepard jotted down the details into her Omni-tool, sending the outline to Miranda.

“So, T’Soni. How have the past couple years treated you?” Garrus asked. “You never did explain the fancy office.”

“That’s… A long story.” She ran hand over her scalp crest. “It would make for better table conversation anyway.” Her answer piqued the trio’s interest.

“Got something planned for us?” Shepard asked. Liara gave her a distinctly sinister smirk.

“I do, in fact.” Her eyes brightened. “The Ascension Hotel has a deluxe restaurant on its higher levels. Usually it requires booking months in advance, but I was able to reserve some tables for tonight. All expenses paid, of course.” The reveal shocked the trio.

“Wha… hang on.” Shepard held up her hands. “You set my team up in a high-class hotel, dug up the info on my dossiers well before I got here, and you’ve got a fine-dining soiree planned for us tonight. Liara…”

“Please, Shepard.” She held up a hand to quash Shepard’s objections. “The cost is no issue, and knowing about dangerous people is half my business anyway. Besides, this isn’t just a fun evening. It will give you a chance to meet Samara in person, and work out the terms of your deal together.”

“But-”

“Shepard.” She fixed the commander with her gaze. “I haven’t seen you in two years, any of you. I want a night for us, where we can just enjoy ourselves.”

“Alright.” Shepard backed off, not wanting to ruin the mood. “What’s the itinerary?”

“Dinner will be in a few hours. Your team is invited, if they want to come.” She sent the time to their Omni-tools. “Now, since I have put such considerable effort into all of this, I expect you to show up in something nice.”

Garrus and Shepard looked at each other, before their eyes travelled to their armour.

“So… not fatigues then?” The turian asked.

“No.” She said sternly, before a smile betrayed her. “Here, these stores are on your hotel’s trading floor. You should find formalwear for humans and turians there.”

“Well, I can safely say I wasn’t expecting to go suit-shopping today.” Shepard chuckled.

“Liara?” Tali’s voice caught their attention. “I hate to ask, but does this restaurant have…” The asari realised immediately what she meant.

“Yes Tali, of course.” She assured her friend. “There’s a wide range of dextro food, all with quarian-safe options.” She smiled to herself. “I didn’t know for sure, but part of me suspected you would all be together again.”

A small beep emanated from Liara’s wrist, prompting a quick check. She scanned over the message for a moment, before getting up from her chair.

“Sorry, something I have to chase up for tomorrow.” Shepard and the others followed suit, following their friend over to her desk. They’d been chatting for almost an hour, though none of them had realised. Collecting something from the desk, she turned back to them. “I’ve got a sky-car waiting on the roof. It will take you straight to the hotel. Remember, something nice.”

“Okay, okay. Something nice, I promise.” Shepard put her hand on her heart as she took the access key.

“See you tonight.” Liara said warmly, giving Shepard a parting hug.
