Chapter 28: Illium Blues

“We’re late…”

“We’re not late.”

“We are, actually. By five minutes.”

“Shit…”

Shepard checked her Omni-tool, frustrated at the time staring back at her. Three hours of getting ready, only to trip and fall at the finish line. Clearly her feelings were showing, she saw Tali and Garrus exchange a look. She swore there had been more time; everything seemed to be going smoothly. Just a quick trip up to their rooms, squaring things away for the few days they’d be staying. Then back in the elevator, before bouncing between a couple different clothing stores. Her and Garrus, they were discerning people, decisive people: People who kept their heads and always had things under control. If either of them had even the slightest faltering in their composure, then Tali was their ace in the hole: A technical woman, running like a fine-tuned watch. She never missed a single detail; her brilliant mind and perfect memory would have them all on schedule and well in hand.

Jesus Christ…

Now the three of them were squeezed into the hotel elevator, watching helplessly as the floor counter ticked over again, and again, and again. How the hell had they lost track of time? There had been barely a hitch with checking in; the staff had clearly been given an earful regarding Tali, because none of them gave her anything less than a smile. The trip up to their rooms hadn’t been that long, just a few minutes appreciating the amenities. They had certainly been luxurious, with soft carpet and wall-length windows looking out on the spectacular Nos Astra skyline: Side-by-side, with even species-specific minibars. She remembered the comment Tali had made upon coming out, something in there had been very authentically quarian.

Still, the three of them had done little more than a quick inspection before shedding their armour and setting off downstairs. Shepard’s ire focused in on shopping, the real culprit. The service had been deceptively good, velvet hands stroking egos long stunted by military life. Waistlines had been measured; colour swatches for hair and crests had been brought out. What was too old fashioned, what was out of her age range? What style of shoes, bare talons or gloves. Options, options, options. In the end, the two of them had been as dull as dishwater, standing side-by-side in matching black and white.

“You’re doing it again.” Garrus leaned closer to Shepard.

“Damn it.” She forced her hands to her sides, leaving her bowtie unmolested. The tuxedo was a perfect fit, yet it still felt stifling.

“I don’t know what you’re even fussing about?” Garrus looked her up and down. “Your collar’s tiny anyway, that’s why you have that little flower on it.” The turian’s formal wear was little more than black military regalia, the wide collar of his garment hiding the slimmer fit to his neckline. They matched in monochrome, if nothing else.

“It’s not about the collar.” Shepard tried to explain. “It’s about balance, getting every piece just right…” She dug her fingers into her palms. “…I know this god damn tie is crooked.”

“Here.” Tali’s gloved hand brushed Shepard’s shoulder. “Let me.” Turning to the quarian, she held still as her nimble fingers set Shepard’s bowtie into pinpoint symmetry. “There, now it’s perfect.” Her friendly tone grew an edge. “So don’t touch it again.

“Thanks.” Shepard’s hands remained still, grateful for the help. Tali gave a satisfied nod before looking up at the floor counter. Shepard felt a pang of guilt, having brought her along the whole time. Hours spent screwing around with Garrus for the best ball gown, while Tali hadn’t been offered a single option. Not that she thought Tali needed it; her suit was a work of art. She hadn’t said anything of course. Hell, it wasn’t in her nature. But Shepard still wished she could’ve joined in as more than just an observer.

The chime of the elevator sounded, and the doors opened into the restaurant’s lobby. She felt genuine awe at the gorgeous scene, taking in the sweeping hallway columns and rich asari murals painted along the walls. Some of it reminded her of ancient Greece, though seamlessly mixed with technology and a sheer feminine essence. That awe quickly turned to discomfort when she caught sight of the woman standing nearby.

“You’re seven minutes late.” Miranda’s brows arched, her lips curled into an annoyed sneer. Her arms were crossed, bare and pale in a strapless, crimson dinner gown.

“That falls within the range of fashionable.” Shepard said lightly, striding toward her.

“Come on.” Miranda rolled her eyes. She slotted in between Shepard and Tali, something that didn’t go unnoticed by either of them. As they passed the wait-staff, she looked the commander up and down. “Hmm. What is that, a legacy brand?” She asked, clearly knowing the answer. “Didn’t take you for old-fashioned, Shepard.”

“Is that a compliment, Lawson?” Shepard shot back, only getting a smirk in return. Fine, if that was the game she wanted to play. “Gotta be honest, never thought I’d see you out of black and white.” She held back a grin, watching Miranda’s brows rise.

“Yes, well…” She was cool as always a moment later. “A girl’s got to have some secrets.”

“Does that include some secret decency?” Garrus jabbed, glaring down at her from his natural height.

“I don’t know, Vakarian.” She pondered, her tone decidedly hostile. “Any secret sense of style under that travesty you’re wearing?”

“You know…” Shepard slipped out of the little line-up in a display of N7 agility. Switching her position with Miranda, she left the two arguers shoulder-to-shoulder, almost touching. “I’m sensing some tension here. I’m thinking a little forced face time might do the trick.”

With a generous crook of her arm, she offered the quarian an invitation. “Tali, what do you say we give the love birds a moment to kiss and make up? So that they do not bring this attitude to the table.”

“Definitely.” Tali matched Shepard’s feigned haughtiness, looping their arms together. Shepard could see that cheeky confidence rising in those shining eyes. Her orders were clear, and the two of them quickened pace through the palatial dining area. The grumbles behind her were a good sign, before they mixed into the soft music and idle chatter. After a safe distance had been created, their footsteps slowed again, back to an enjoyable stroll.

“What do you think that was all about?” Shepard asked her friend, giving away only a little irony to her question.

“Keelah, I think they’re both “secret” idiots…” She said dryly, shaking her helmet. Shepard felt a chuckle come out of her, warm and honest. Soon enough, Tali’s chirpy giggle joined in, all but banishing the surrounding noise from her mind. She’d forgotten this, just sharing a laugh with Tali. After Horizon, everything had felt hopeless. But like always, she’d been there. Now that they had a new direction, and a moment of calm in the endless storm, it was like returning to a half-remembered dream. So much had changed; they had changed. But this, this little spark the two had always shared? She could still feel it.

“Shepard, over here!” Jacob waved to them, sporting a more modern, white tux. A woman was with him, her black dress trailing behind like a shadow. At first Shepard didn’t recognise her, but that all-knowing glint in her eyes betrayed her just as she spoke.

“Hey Shep.” Kasumi’s smoky voice was laced with both glee and mischief. “Well, look at you two.”

Suddenly, Shepard felt very much back in the world around her. A sympathetic shiver ran through Tali, and their arms disentangled: The thief’s bright smile only widened, devilish demeanour blatant. What had seemed like a silly display before quickly grew friendly again, the two of them readying their own words.

“Glad to see you came.” Shepard gave Jacob a smile. “You shine up nicely.”

“Not too bad yourself, Shepard.” He returned an impressed nod. “Classic look, straight outta the vids.”

“You look beautiful, Kasumi.” Tali’s praise was earnest, her eyes squishing to friendly crescents. The thief gave a little bow.

“Had to wear something floor length, but thank you.” Keen eyes glanced them both over. “Not that it comes close to that lovely suit of yours.” Her comment made the quarian clam up.

“Oh, well I…” Shepard watched her race to play it off. “…I’m glad someone noticed!” She put her hands on her hips. “I wore my two sexiest belts tonight!”

Kasumi’s laughter was infectious, and the four of them quickly mingled around the table. A few other faces had come to join them. Mordin was there, dressed in a salarian fashion that seemed all too close to his typical doctor’s coat: Quick talking and energetic as always, he was retelling an anecdote from his days on Omega: Nothing too grizzly, something about bottle-feeding baby batarians. Apparently their needle teeth came in early. Karin was squaring up for a medical match, clad in a conservative dress with a glass of shiny alcohol in hand. Her presence was a nice surprise, but Shepard was barely able to get a word in between them.

The two doctors had seemingly found quite the rapport over the past couple weeks, the thought darkened things, but only for a moment. What wasn’t a surprise however, were the absentees. Jack was nowhere to be seen, neither was Grunt. Shepard had a sneaking suspicion someone had given them an order in her stead. On the other hand, they would be held responsible for any damages.

Zaeed’s ugly suit did little to hide his presence, giving a growling snort for greeting. Suddenly, a streak of red clacked past. Miranda didn’t say a word, but instead gave her the most chilling glare Shepard had ever seen. In a flash she was in high spirits, chatting with Jacob and Kasumi. Her haunting display of mood shifts had little time to sink in, before a certain voice rumbled behind Shepard.

“You’re a bitch…” Garrus said flatly, walking up beside her. “You owe me a drink for that.”

“Sorry Garrus.” Shepard gave him a look of remorse. “I misread the room. I thought you and Lawson were…”

“Were what?” The turian’s eyes widened a little.

“Y’know… flirting.” She watched those brow-plates shoot up.

“W-what?” Garrus exclaimed; mandibles flared in shock. The situation wasn’t helped by an audible snort of laughter exploding from Tali’s vocaliser.

“…Grow up.” The turian grunted, perfectly composed. Shepard saw him glance to the still recovering quarian, before meeting her eyes again. There was a second of anticipation, and those reptilian eyes conveyed all they needed to. Then the friendly light was back, and he levelled a childish insult at Tali. Shepard smiled, before a gentle hand touched her arm.

“You look dashing, Shepard.”

She turned, following Liara’s voice. Standing in front of the three of them, the asari was a sight to behold. There had been rumours, perhaps for centuries that her species held some kind of mystical sway over all life in the galaxy. That something deep and primal awakened in their presence, something universal. Looking upon her old friend, Liara’s flowing white dress contrasting against her cerulean skin, Shepard could understand where such rumours came from.

The white silk was distinctly asari in design, evoking an ancient, long-lived sense of style. It was both elegant and simple, like something Shepard might have seen adorning a marble statue. Her neck was adorned with a silver torque necklace. The ring of sparkling metal was engraved with an intricate carving, with white stones inlaid. Shepard took a moment to adjust, racking her brain for any other time she’d seen Liara dressed like this. This awkward young scientist, whose company had been dusty ruins for half a century? Now she wouldn’t have looked out of place in a ballroom on Thessia.

“I, uh…” Awareness returned. “I did promise.” She said confidently.

“Samara’s waiting on the balcony.” She beckoned to Shepard. “I’ve arranged for us all to be seated together. Five minutes?” Liara’s gaze turned to Garrus and Tali.

Following her well-dressed sponsor, Shepard politely slipped past other guests and patrons. Soon the two of them had made it to the far side. A large outdoor balcony complimented the indoor dining area. Arranged with lights, chairs and small tables for a more intimate setting. None of them were occupied. Stepping through the glass doors, Shepard was surprised at the sense of privacy that seemed to hang in the night air.

Keeping pace with Liara, it wasn’t long before Shepard spotted the woman they were meeting: She stood alone, tall and proud. Easily a head over Liara, Shepard figured this asari could look her dead in the eye. She turned as they approached, long before either of them could announce themselves. Her oddly bulky physique was revealed to be a suit of armour, curved plates of ceramic and metal. The centre of her chest plate was adorned with gold, rising up under her jaw. The juxtaposition of the precious metal against the blood-red colour of her suit was striking.

“Lady Samara.” Liara bowed low in her presence. “This is the human I told you about, Commander Shepard.”

Shepard felt the justicar’s eyes on her. They were bone white, holding a strange serenity. Like all asari, Samara’s face was beautiful, but nothing like Liara. Where Liara still retained the fullness of youth, soft and approachable, this warrior was almost… regal. Her features were stark and angular, with a proud nose and jaw. Her face bore no markings or tattoos, instead framed with a red headpiece that sat just below her crest. She silently observed Shepard, her face impossible to read.

“Thank you, Doctor T’Soni.” Samara’s voice was low and patient, like a mother speaking to her child. “I would like to speak to her alone, if that is all right?”

Liara gave another bow, nodding in agreement before giving Shepard a parting smile. Soon the sounds of her footsteps disappeared beyond the door. They were alone together. Samara’s eyes were back on Shepard, appraising and dissecting for a final time. Then, with a graceful wave of her hand, she beckoned Shepard over to the railing. She was there before realising what happened, as if compelled. Her assumptions had been right, and Samara met her eye-to-eye.

“Your friend has told me much about you, Commander Shepard.” Her voice was arresting. “She told me of your deeds on Feros, how you saved innocent people from enthrallment to a monster. She spoke of her rescue, how she would have died on Therum if not for you. You disobeyed the orders of your superiors to pursue Saren, and as I understand it, we would not be having this conversation if you had not.”

“That was… a long time ago.” Shepard admitted, leaning against the railing.

“And now you face a new threat. One that troubles your heart.” She gave Shepard a knowing look. “Your path has been difficult of late, hasn’t it?”

“It… has.” Shepard wasn’t sure if she felt forced to tell the truth, or if there was some strange kinship she felt with the justicar. But either way, she couldn’t bring herself to lie. “This threat, the Collectors, they’ve taken thousands of innocent people.” Her voice grew steely. “I intend to stop them, and I could use your help.” A faint smile graced Samara’s statuesque features.

“You are a brave one.” She paused for a moment. “Your goal is honourable, and your enemy is worthy.” She looked out at the city, scanning it’s twinkling horizon.

“It has been many years since I faced battle with allies at my side…” She looked back at Shepard. “However, I am currently hunting a dangerous criminal.”

“Nassana’s hired killer.” Shepard finished her thought.

“Yes. My Code compels me to pursue her wherever she flees to. That pursuit has brought me to this world, and Miss Dantius.”

“If you know where she is, then why meet with me?” Shepard saw her eyes fall for a second.

“I am no fool, Shepard. Nassana Dantius is a powerful woman; she commands both mercenaries and the law on this world. Working alone, I would not be fast enough to locate her before law enforcement arrived. I would be forced to kill them, despite their innocence."

“There’s no other way?”

“Until tonight, there was not.” Samara said grimly. “But if your friend’s tales of you are true, we could work together to reach her.”

“I’d be willing to, but… missions like these require tactical flexibility. Situations can change rapidly, and I have to be sure my orders are followed. A rouge element could endanger the mission, and as I understand it your code supersedes everything else.” Shepard watched the justicar consider her statement silently mulling it over.

“This is true.” Samara conceded. “However, there is a way to circumvent this problem: The Third Oath of Subsumation. I would swear myself to your service, granting absolution from the Code’s prescriptions.”

“You’d… swear yourself to my service?” Shepard was taken aback.

“I would be placing my trust in you, Shepard. This is not something I do lightly, nor without repercussions if I am forced into dishonourable acts.” Shepard’s shock received a smile. “But you are right. Both our missions require… flexibility.”

Getting down on one knee, Samara bowed her head. She pulled the gauntlet from her right hand, offering it to Shepard.

“Are you ready?”

Her question required no words, and Shepard took Samara’s hand in her own. For a moment, she simply felt the justicar’s skin, and listened to the wind whistle overhead. Then everything changed. In an instant, the reality around her fell away, like slipping beneath a perfectly clear lake. If not for the water line crossing her view, it would seem like nothing had changed at all. Then that indescribable oneness came, and she felt hundreds of years of wisdom and confidence flow through her veins.

Her eyes beheld a child, brave and plucky, but filled with resentment. Other feelings had crested into view. Regret… anger. Then they were gone. Samara’s mind was disciplined, and took nary a single step beyond Shepard’s bounds of comfort. She heard the justicar’s words, her oath to serve Shepard. Her morals would be Samara’s morals, and her choices Samara’s choices. Then it was over, like hours had passed in the blink of an eye. She was standing on the balcony again.

As Shepard walked back to the restaurant’s doors, she felt calmer than she had in a long time. She’d bid the justicar farewell, as Samara had wished to remain outside. The conversation was like a blur, no doubt a side effect of their joining. But this time had been so very different. When she’d joined with Liara, the experience had been terrifying. Liara’s mind had pressed upon hers, overpowering her will and forcibly exposing her thoughts. Both of them had felt fatigued afterward, with a headache plaguing her for nearly an hour.

But understanding just what the practice meant to the asari, Shepard realised that Liara’s attempts had merely been clumsy. The eagerness of a first-timer, trying her best to navigate a new experience. With Samara, there was no clumsiness. She had handled their joining with a deftness and skill that made the whole experience… oddly pleasant. Soon Shepard spotted her old friends, sitting together at one end of the table. Careful with her suit, she sat down between Tali and Liara, the latter sitting at the head of the table. Garrus was across from her, clearly anticipating her return.

“How’d it go?” The turian asked, with his usual bluntness.

“Yes.” Tali chimed in on Shepard’s shoulder. “What was she like?”

“Samara? She was…” Shepard shook her head. “Not like any asari I’ve ever met.” She could see that her description hadn’t sated them. “You’ll see for yourselves tomorrow. Now, I’m pretty sure there was a dinner part of this dinner, right?”

Dinner there was, a range of options befitting a galaxy-class restaurant: Examples of cuisine from all over the galaxy, on both sides of the chirality aisle. Soon everyone had ordered, not a single person disappointed. After some friendly insistence from Liara, Shepard had joined her in an asari delicacy: The pride of the Ascension Hotel, with a price tag to match. In no time at all her reservations were laid to rest. The food was delicious, each dish served within seconds of each other. Even Tali’s order; sealed inside a sterile container. She was happy to see Tali hook the feeding tube to her mask’s oral port, without a care in the world. A small part of her worried at the presence of liquor in Tali’s hand, but if she was comfortable, that was enough for her.

After a little prodding, Liara finally opened up about how she ended up on the borders of asari space. As usual her cadence was soft and friendly, but Shepard could hear pain in her voice. She glossed over the first few weeks, the time she’d spent burying Shepard. The time they’d all spent burying her. The guilt rested at the back of Shepard’s mind, not intrusive, but very much there. Soon Liara’s story focused on her late mother, Benezia. It was strange hearing Liara talk about her mother now. Every word was laced with regret at what happened. She recounted moving back to Thessia for a time, to her old family villa. Soon came the lawyers and accountants, forcing the archaeologist back into the spotlight.

The matriarchs had decided to make an example of Benezia, looking to seize Liara’s rightful inheritance and defame her mother as a monster. The truth of indoctrination hadn’t mattered, but in the end, the public shaming had been enough. With the warmth of home snuffed out, she left Thessia behind, using her mother’s estate and contacts to change her life. Dr T’Soni had hung up her digging gear, wanting instead to use the powers of an information broker to do some good. Away from the suspicious eyes of the matriarchs, Illium had become her new home.

Shepard could tell there were things left unsaid, even under the mask of Liara’s newfound wit. She couldn’t help but sense blame underneath; though perhaps she was just blaming herself. She should have been there for her, for all of them. Garrus, Liara, Tali, they’d all strived to be their best aboard her old ship. They’d put their lives on hold for her, something she’d be eternally grateful for. But one act of “bravery” had thrown them all to the wind, forced to face the consequences of her decisions alone.

An hour ticked by, filled with talking, eating and drinking. It was easy for guilt to get lost in great company. Shepard let it wander, focusing instead on a little archaeology of her own. The old stories were dusted off, with audible giggles and groans in equal amount. She started things small, recounting a certain quarian completely flubbing what she’d called an “easy hack” on an exterior door on Noveria. It had taken nearly fifteen minutes to cut through with a torch. The entire time was filled with an endless chorus of complaints from Garrus, who very much didn’t care for the snowy weather. The whining had been an inside joke between them for two months afterward. She could see the turian lining up an old favourite, glancing to Liara before starting.

“You guys remember that mission on Cohe?” Garrus asked, his mandibles already fluttering with anticipation.

“Oh Goddess…” Liara rubbed her eyes. “Please, not this story.”

“You mean stopping the biotic terrorists?” Tali asked, a little slurred.

“That’s not what he’s talking about…” Liara mumbled. By now both Jacob and Kasumi had moved their chairs closer to the old Normandy crew, eager to listen in.

“So, we’re heading back along the ridge when all of a sudden, Shepard spots something. What was it, another mining site?” Garrus asked.

“An emergency beacon.” The commander clarified.

“Sure. So, she puts the mako in full throttle, and launches us clear off the side.” The turian shot his arm out for effect. “She’s kicking pedals, using the joystick, trying to flatten us out as we’re falling. Then bam! The tank’s on her back.”

“Because of a thruster failure, by the way.” Shepard interjected. “Not driver error.”

“Uh-huh, convenient excuse. Anyway, we crawl out of the wreck. Despite the fall, nothing’s too banged up. Shepard goes off and plants her flag, while the rest of us try to figure out a way to tip the mako over. That’s when our resident asari has an idea. She’s going to lift it, with her biotics.”

Another audible groan escaped Liara.

“She starts glowing blue, doing her hand-thing while the tank’s floating into the air. We’re cheering her on, providing moral support. Wrex gets underneath to spin the thing upright, and then she brings it right down on his head. The krogan’s out cold.”

“Splat!” Tali added, punching the tabletop.

“It was an accident!” Liara argued; her cheeks flushed purple. “Goddess, I hate this story.”

“We all thought he was dead.” Garrus shrugged. “Then, just as Shepard comes back, Wrex lets out the loudest fart I’ve ever heard. He sprung back to life like Scythian lilly after winter. Didn’t remember a thing we’d done that day.”

Shepard watched her poor blue friend’s face retreat into her hands, as Tali became a chuckling mess. Kasumi and Jacob were prying the turian for more details, between sniggers. He was only too happy to explain, with more hand motions to match. Soon Zaeed was shouting comments over their shoulders, throwing his own war stories into the mix. Watching Liara compose herself with an embarrassed smile, Shepard felt an old, familiar warmth inside. The only thing that would’ve made it better was if the old krogan himself could’ve been there. After her giggles and loud slurps died down, Tali pulled the tubes from her induction ports. Up on her nimble feet, she loudly informed them she’d be back soon, with another drink. Her gusto was always charming, though Shepard didn’t want her overdoing things. More mother-hen behaviour, she guessed some things never changed.

“I suppose I should be glad Wrex lived in this retelling of the story.” Liara joked, touching Shepard’s shoulder.

“Look at it this way, without you we never would’ve gotten the mako unstuck.” Shepard offered with a smile.

“Hmm…” Her cerulean cheeks rose in a smile. “That’s what I’ve missed the most these past two years.” She said sweetly. “Your optimism.”

“I’m glad you’re doing okay out here.” Shepard admitted, a little relieved. “I don’t… I’m not sure what to say about what happened.”

“You don’t have to say anything.” She put her hand on Shepard’s. Her voice, her smile, Shepard had to ask.

“Y’know, you could come with me.” She suggested, brightening up. “Your expertise, your biotics, I can’t think of anyone better. Plus just… having you all by my side, it’d help me sleep better at night.”

The smile fell from Liara’s face. After a final squeeze, she pulled her hand away.

“I’m sorry Shepard.” She said quietly, so no one could overhear. “Seeing you again… it’s indescribable. But I have commitments here. I can’t leave, not even for a day. Not until…” She sighed. “…I’m sorry.”

Shepard gave her a nod of understanding, flashing her a brave, lying smile. She had been here before, more than once. Garrus, Tali, she was already asking enough from old friends. She wouldn’t let her feelings cloud that, not like they had with Kaidan. Instead, the commander would just be happy for the time she did have with her friend. Grateful, even if goodbye was only a few days away. Instinctively she looked up from the table, over to the bar. Tali was nowhere to be seen. Her senses sharpened, tracking across the room before catching movement at the balcony doors. With a quick look back to the bar, she pieced together what had happened, already on her feet.

All she’d wanted was another drink. It wasn’t too much to ask for. Tali had made her way to the bar, putting on an invisible smile for the asari behind the counter. She’d brought the liquor capsule, to make sure there was no guesswork. Immediately, the looks began. The bartender stared down her nose, showing off her distaste with no attempt to hide it. She shook her blue head, certain that they didn’t even sell quarian-safe brandy. Why? What terrible inconvenience was she being? As if the endless sea of affluent faces grimacing at her passing by hadn’t been enough.

In one last vain attempt, Tali pleaded with the woman. Just to check, to make sure they didn’t have any more. She watched her lazily stroll down the bar, disappearing behind an employee door. A minute ticked by, then two. She could feel anger building inside, along with embarrassment. She started bouncing on her toes nervously, feeling like a damn pilgrim again. Fed up, she looked around for another employee.

That’s when she saw them: Two asari maidens, sitting together. They were giggling, stealing glances at her: The quarian, the freak in the mask. They didn’t even hide it, mocking her nervous tick and cackling to themselves. Anger flared up into fury. Was having a lifespan ten times her own not enough? Was being the most desired species in the galaxy not enough? These two bosh’tets just wouldn’t be content without sneering at someone who’d never have what they have.

It had been only a few minutes since then, since she’d stormed across the dining area to the balcony’s beckoning solitude. She’d felt eyes on her the whole way, nothing but contempt in all of them. She was probably the first quarian they’d ever seen at this hotel, at least as a customer. The fury was icing over now, holding her captive against the railing. At first she’d wanted to go back to her friends, to Shepard. But when she’d looked over, Shepard was talking to Liara. That twinge of rage she felt seeing them together… she was disgusted with herself. Liara was her friend, and she had endured these two years without Shepard too.

Tali looked up at the stars with wet eyes, trying desperately to clear her head. It wasn’t like she was ignorant; she knew how things were. Maybe she’d just been spoiled, spending her pilgrimage aboard a ship full of aliens, treated as an equal and a friend. Shepard’s ship… Her… friend. But standing out here, listening to the cold wind while the warmth inside festered with hatred, this was the reality. Where better than Illium to be reminded of that?

“You came out here in a hurry.” Shepard’s voice nearly made her jump. Shepard sidled up beside her while she racked her brain for an excuse.

“I was, um…” She was floundering. “I think I had too much to drink, and… I was feeling a little nauseous. You know, story of my life.” It was only half a lie; she felt another dose of medication pass through her nasal tube.

“Everything okay?” Shepard’s voice took on that damned tone, one she’d grown to love and hate. Shepard never used it with anyone else. Just with her.

“Yeah.” She pepped herself up. “Just… looking at the stars, I’m surprised we can see them in the city.”

“Didn’t take you for a stargazer.” Shepard leaned next to her, her broad chest on full display, the tuxedo tailored to the curve of her breasts. “I mean; we see them all the time.”

“I know…” Tali agreed, thankful for the reprieve of their usual rhythm. “But it’s different down here, special.”

“How so?” Shepard followed her gaze up to the twinkling black.

“When we’re up there, they just feel like… background.” She didn’t really know what she meant. “But… standing here, looking up at them… it feels…”

“Mysterious… magical?” Shepard guessed, putting a smile on her face. Hearing it said out loud sounded silly… it made her feel silly. It only made her smile wider.

“Maybe a little…” She managed through a self-conscious giggle.

“I think you’re right.” Shepard kept her gaze on them, her chest rising in a deep breath. “It’s calming, seeing them like this. Reminds me of… when I was a kid.” Tali saw Shepard’s smile dip for a moment, and a part of her ached to guess at Shepard’s feelings. She still struggled with the truth of what happened.

“It’s nice seeing Liara again.” She mused, changing the subject. “Keelah, she’s so different now.”

“You’re telling me.” Shepard nodded, her attention back on Tali. “Two years go by, and I barely recognise her. I guess… everything with her mother, and then… you know.” She couldn’t say it. “…Who wouldn’t change after all that? Still, she seems okay. And she’s got some more bite, which is a relief.”

“She looked beautiful in her dress.” Tali admitted, doing her best to hide her jealousy.

“I don’t think I’ve ever seen her in one before today.” Shepard concurred, rubbing her chin. “Granted, I was probably more shocked seeing Kasumi in a dress. I’m glad she’s up and around again.”

“Me too.” She fed off Shepard’s warm demeanour. “I like her.”

“Although…” Shepard stood up straight, crossing her arms. “She did beat me to the punch.”

“The… punch?”

“Mm-hmm.” Shepard had that smirk on her face. “We’ve been back together what, a few weeks now? And I still couldn’t find time to mention it.” She chastised herself.

“Mention… what?” Tali felt the trap close around her.

“Your new suit.” The smirk was gone, replaced by that wonderful smile. A hint of it returned as her eyes widened. She straightened up, cheeks flushing. Shepard hadn’t breathed a word about her suit; she never even thought about the old one anymore.

“Oh!” She managed, still coming down from the surprise. “…My suit.”

“You thought I hadn’t noticed.” Shepard’s accusation came with a grin.

“No! No-I… I mean it’s just…” She stuttered for the first time in years. “I always wear this suit, and it’s not that different from my old one. I wouldn’t be surprised if you couldn’t tell. N-not that I’m saying you couldn’t, obviously you could it’s just that…” She was gesticulating, talking too much. “W-what did you… notice?”

“Well…” Shepard gave her a scrutinising look-over. She was playing it up, but Tali felt sweat beading on her skin. Those blue eyes, taking in her body again, memories were quick to resurface. Endeavouring not to make an idiot of herself, she kept her hands by her sides.

“It’s more colourful: The purple of your realk? It’s bright, eye-catching.”

“My old realk was purple too.” She reminded Shepard, trying to keep the smile off her face.

“Oh, we’re debating purples now?” Shepard chuckled.

“Sorry, sorry.” She shook her head in apology.

“…I know it was.” Shepard admitted. “But this colour, it’s richer. The boldness of it, that’s you to a tee.” The compliment spurred an answer.

“It’s one of my Clan’s traditional colours.” She looked down at her gloves. “It represents temperance, finding a balance between wisdom and action. It was… actually my Father’s choice.”

“Hoping you’d take the hint, maybe?” Shepard teased. “Not sure it worked.”

“Shepard…” Tali’s hands were on her hips, pouting in spite of herself.

“Sorry. I’ve never been very good with fashion.” Shepard fiddled with her fingers. “…It’s beautiful, Tali.” She gave Tali an earnest smile. “I can’t think of anyone who deserves it more.”

Her mind drew a blank. She just stood there, barely aware of the world around her. After living so long in the damn thing, it was just clothing to her. She thought of its ballistic fibres and advanced combat seals. Or the layers of ruggedized circuitry, woven into the fabric. How effectively her suit’s VI interfaced with her cybernetics, limiting lag almost completely. Her new mask was near bulletproof, with advanced suites for optical clarity and eye-protection. Everything from survivability to combat, hacking to EVA work. The suit was top-of-the-line, a privilege that stemmed from her birth. But also an endless source of torment, a constant reminder of what she was. She had a dozen choice words for the life-saving garment, some Kelish so profane it wouldn’t even translate.

And Shepard thought it was beautiful. Not different, or nice… beautiful. She felt her heart swell, shedding the anger it had been holding. Hearing Shepard say that word, about her, it didn’t feel real. When her senses returned, Shepard was looking back at the night sky. She couldn’t be sure if Shepard had noticed her long silence, but the commander didn’t seem to mind.

“Shepard?” She finally put the words together. “Thanks… for coming to find me.” Shepard gave her a knowing nod.

“I should be thanking you, for not feeding those blue brats their teeth.” Shepard said casually. “Girls their age, they’ve gotta be like, asari toddlers right? You might’ve been done in for child abuse.”

Tali’s laughter filled her ears before she’d even realised what was happening. A mental image of fully adult asari, waddling around with milk capsules in their mouths was just too much. She enjoyed the quip, and the intentions behind it.

“I guess I made the right choice.” She giggled in agreement. “I prefer this.” Shepard ran a bare hand over her short but noticeable hair.

“Still… my favourite quarian having her night ruined?” Shepard clicked her tongue, shaking her head. “Can’t let that go unanswered.”

“It’s okay… really, I’m happy just…” Her placation vanished when she felt Shepard’s solid shoulder against hers.

“Tell you what.” Shepard leaned in close. “We still have a couple days here after tomorrow, right? I was thinking we could go to one of those nice parks. Do some bird watching.”

“You want to go… bird watching… with me?” Tali’s question came with a sharp climb in suit temperature.

“I didn’t get to see them earlier.” Shepard jokingly complained. “I think you owe me a bird or two.”

Tali unconsciously adjusted her hood, feeling the heat radiate off her cheeks. She pretended to look at the stars, glancing Shepard’s face for only a moment. There was a hint of pink in Shepard’s ears, and her smile seemed different. Whatever it was, it made her mouth tingle. Swallowing, she entwined her fingers on the railing.

“…I’d like that.”
