Chapter 2: Back From Hades

Commander Jane Shepard had never really liked parties.

Or maybe, she just wasn’t suave enough to pull off that fun, carefree attitude. Growing up on a small farming colony, most of the festivities had been small affairs: Birthdays, crop-yield milestones, and a wide array of inexplicable rural traditions. The people she’d celebrated with back then were her parents, or her sister, or the kids from the next farm over. After growing up with everyone she’d ever get to know, the young farm girl had taken her people skills for granted. It was hard to get to know new people, outsiders who were oblivious to everyone’s favourite hiking trails, or who’s parents had a faulty lock on their pre-fab that could be fooled by putting on a silly voice. Once someone had spent their life knowing everyone around them so well, it’s hard to imagine ever wanting to learn about anyone else.

But things change. The galaxy Shepard was a part of eventually came to greet her little farming world. Beyond the pain she’d felt when she saw the old farmhouse burning, beyond the agony of finding her parents bodies lying disfigured in the dirt. Beyond the fear and anguish of not knowing where her friends were, where her sister was. There was this all-encompassing loss of who she was. Jane Shepard was a farmer; she knew that Mr Patel’s house had a faulty voice lock. She could lead her friends back from being so hopelessly lost that they were sure it’d be dawn before they saw town lights again. Everything about that girl died on Mindoir. When she was picked up by an Alliance ship and evacuated to Arcturus Station, she might as well have never been Jane Shepard at all.

As years passed, that girl learned the hard way that life goes on. Like it or not, the only way is forward. After enlisting with the Alliance military, Jane quickly found a new beginning, a new point to start from. Her skills at farming gave way to soldiering and the men she was forced into company with eventually became friends. From the gruelling first months in basic training, to the terrifying holdout she’d managed on Elysium, and even the thing’s she’d done on Torfan, Commander Shepard had found her through-line. She was a soldier; a woman with the skills to fight and win against any foe… and she hoped, a woman who did what was right.

After what had been the craziest eight months of her life, Commander Shepard sat comfortably at a little window-side table in Flux, the least seedy nightclub on the Citadel. Even after a week in the hospital, everything still managed to ache. The club’s thumping music mellowed out where she was, just enough to enjoy conversation. Nearly all of the Normandy’s crew had come to celebrate the destruction of Sovereign, certain to be a career highlight for everyone involved. As was typical of her life now, doubts niggled at the back of the commander’s mind. Would the Council take the Reaper threat seriously, could she expect that much after saving their lives? Would it even matter?

She focused herself, burying the worry somewhere quiet. There’d be plenty of time for it later. Anderson was seated across from her, the old man was retelling a bar brawl he’d “won” with the help of a salarian Spectre. Nodding along to a tale she’d heard before, Shepard took the opportunity to glance around the room. She spotted Kaidan and Liara, both of whom were smiling. Hidden away from the music even more than she was, it was good to see them in high spirits again. She knew better than anyone, a chance to laugh was like coming up for air. With her mind on the subject, the sound of booming krogan laughter caught her attention just in time to see Garrus get up from his chair. The frustrated turian was pointing at his much older friend, declaring him a liar. Wrex laughed again, harder this time, slurring his response beyond human comprehension with the exception of the word “pyjak”.

Getting ready to chime in as Anderson’s story came to an end, with a practiced line about the Spectres, the commander’s eyes caught the standout member of her crew.

“Tali!” She hollered out. “Come sit down, I was just gonna grab another round!”

Ignoring her old mentor’s slight look of offense, Shepard got up from her seat just as the bouncy young quarian hopped into hers. After her inquiry into Tali’s choice of liquor was met with the swirling of her half-full brandy capsule, Shepard made her way over to the bar. Ordering the same again for the pair, Shepard looked over her shoulder. Tali and Anderson were already chatting away, despite their difference in age and species it didn’t surprise her one bit. For a girl who lived her whole life in an envirosuit, Tali’Zorah was undoubtedly the social butterfly of the team. A technical genius with the swagger of a ship’s mechanic, the spunky young woman had been her adventure’s biggest surprise.

After a bad first impression with aliens, Shepard never imagined working with so many on her hunt for Saren. Liara T’Soni had been a beautiful introduction to the asari, along with an unsettling look into what sharing minds was really like. Wrex was about the textbook definition of a krogan, a lumbering brute with a language of grunts. Garrus was a turian; thankfully the stick up his ass hadn’t come with any First Contact War baggage. And then there was Tali, the only quarian she’d ever met.

Shepard recalled something Kaidan had told her. How his treatment at the hands of a cruel turian instructor had opened the man’s eyes to how human aliens really were. His anecdote had struck the commander as a little trite at the time, something she thought she took for granted. Dealing with Liara’s crush on her felt like something straight out of high school. But afterward, watching her grieve the loss of her mother, Shepard could see her own pain reflected back through the asari’s tear-stained eyes.

Far from the heartless mercenary she’d thought Wrex to be, talking with the krogan revealed a man struggling to hold on to the last shreds of hope he had left. That one day his people, a people vilified and punished by the galaxy for the sins of their ancestors, could be seen as something more than monsters.

Despite the tensions between their species, Garrus had been one of the easier aliens to connect with. Another soldier and idealist, who wanted to make the world a better place. Different from the collectivist turians she’d met elsewhere, Mr Vakarian was a rebel who hated the rules. But there was more than that. He was uncertain, looking for guidance, a reflection of Shepard’s younger days. And much like Anderson had become more than a mentor to her, Shepard felt that same kinship with Garrus. She couldn’t imagine fighting beside anyone else.

And then… there was Tali. She was a princess of the quarian people, though she didn’t care for the comparison. She’d struck out on her own, to fulfil a sacred rite of passage and earn her place as a valued member of their insular society. The two of them had spent hours talking about all the strange and fascinating aspects of her people. Exiled from their homeworld by their machine creations, and forced to live in a massive cobbled together flotilla. Tali spoke of the toll it took on their bodies, trapping them in bio-suits. She spoke of how close her people were, millions of strangers as dear as kin. Shepard understood all too well her desire to return home, to be among the familiar faces of friends and family.

Yet despite her almost mythical origins, the young quarian had been the fastest friend Shepard had ever made. She was charming and friendly and eager to help. Good with a shotgun and not shy about using it, the engineer had become Shepard’s left hand, shoulder-to-shoulder with her from the day they’d met. But beyond her skills and peppy demeanour, there was something special about Tali. The bravery that made her volunteer for every mission had driven her to seek Shepard out the night Ash died. It had pushed her to take a crumbling woman in her arms and remind Shepard that she wasn’t alone. That one moment of kindness was emblematic of everything Tali was, and she was emblematic of just how human aliens really were.

Unsure of exactly what Anderson was smirking at Tali about as Shepard sat back down to the table, she passed the old man a drink, rescuing her quarian friend from the captain. Settling into the typical easy conversation she enjoyed with both of them, Shepard was happy to see that they got on just as easily with each other too. As usual, Anderson steered the conversation, leading Tali in when she had an anecdote to share and becoming suspiciously quiet when her glowing eyes were fixed on her commander. Of course, whenever her old captain was around, the stories of Shepard’s early days were never far behind. Right when she caught that familiar glint in the captain’s eye, Anderson turned his attention to the young engineer.

Capturing her in his well-practised teacher’s voice, he took the quarian on an agonising journey through Shepard’s most embarrassing moments as a marine, culminating in a particular tale that left the poor woman audibly groaning. Tali was a good sport about the whole thing, quick to throw her hat into the ring of self-made catastrophes. Many were focused around her area of engineering expertise, including throwing up in her helmet when an engine she was fixing fired accidently, sending her tumbling around in a rapidly spinning shuttle. Others had bigger consequences, like killing the power to an entire live-ship, though that one had never been traced back to her. Between Tali and Anderson, Shepard was caught in peaks of laughter and valleys of utter shame. Finally the bottles the pair cradled were empty. Getting up from the table, Shepard went for another round only to have her mentor’s voice stop her dead.

“Shepard, I haven’t seen you out on the dance floor yet?”

She knew it was a trap, but the commander played along cautiously. After some back and forth Anderson’s brown eyes settled on Tali and just for a moment that same little smirk crossed his lined face again.

“Why don’t you take your young friend here?”

There it was. She should’ve seen it coming a mile away, but just like always with Anderson she fell for it hook, line and sinker. Shepard saw Tali stiffen up for a moment in her chair, clearly just as nervous as Shepard was. Tali’s shining eyes were on hers, looking to Shepard for what to do next. The pulsing music seemed louder now and she could definitely notice Tali bobbing along in her seat. Suddenly the quarian burst to life, proclaiming that she’d love to dance, then asking Shepard straight out to join her. Shepard knew it was suicide; she was a terrible dancer. Even in the close-knit parties from her childhood, she had a reputation. But the way Tali looked at her… God she wanted to say yes. Then came the question, pointed right at her from the man she respected most.

“C’mon Shepard. You’re not gonna refuse the lady a dance, are you?”

That was all it took. Shepard fixed her blue eyes on her friend.

“I wouldn’t dream of it.”

Confidence surged in the commander as she made a show of beckoning Tali to follow. Soon her feet found the dance floor, under lights and lasers. All around her was heat and noise and movement. The liquor seemed to smear everything together, like someone had painted over reality itself. Everything else took on this comfortable fuzz, just obscure enough not to intrude. But the young woman in front of her, the brilliant quarian engineer that she trusted with her life, she was in perfect focus.

Tali took to the music like a duck to water, waving her arms and shaking her hips to the beat and rhythm. She’d mentioned once that dancers were held in high esteem among the quarian people; Shepard had to wonder if she sat on some kind of dancer’s throne back home. The way she moved, with well-practiced grace melded seamlessly to that spontaneous bounce in her step, it was impossible to look away. The colourful flashing lights reflected off the fabric of her suit, giving her lithe form a sheen that was captivating. After a few twirls to the melody, her shining gaze was on Shepard, waking her up out of her stupor.

Summoning up all her bravery, the commander cut loose. A wonderful, terrible mess of flailing arms and tapping feet that all at once looked out-dated and somehow never-before-seen. Shepard closed her eyes for most of it, pretending to feel the music that way, when of course she really just couldn’t bear to see Tali’s reaction. With the practiced stealth of an N7 commando, she opened her eyes a crack, expecting to catch her laughing. But when she glanced her friend, she was shocked to see Tali’s own dancing had slowed. Tali was… ogling her!

Shepard had spent every night since their embrace chiding herself for staring at her afterward. But now, the quarian was honest to God checking her out. The commander was glad for the coloured lighting the two were bathed in. She turned to face her; eyes open as she played along. For a long moment the two friends watched each other before the young quarian performed another, far slower twirl. Shepard moved in closer, unable to help taking her in again. The lights seemed brighter, and the music louder. The beat began to slow as the melody bled away. The pinks and greens of the party strobes blazed a brilliant white. All around them faded into noise, it was just the two of them, then just Shepard.

“…There, on the monitor. Something’s wrong…”

Burning white light.

“…She’s reacting to outside stimuli. Showing an awareness of her surroundings!”

Burning. Scorching. Pain.

“…My god Miranda, I think she’s waking up…”

The beeping and pulsing of machines filled Shepard’s ears. She couldn’t move, her body ignored the desperate pleas to get up, to escape! The blazing white light was broken up by shadows looming over her. They were talking, she tried to listen but the words just didn’t make sense. Her breathing quickened, shallow gulps funnelling down a thick tube in her throat. Shepard’s eyes lolled lazily around in her skull, trying to make out anything recognisable. Where was she? What was happening? She tried to remember, there was a club, music. She was dancing, smiling?

“Damn it Wilson she’s not ready yet! Give her the sedative!”

A woman’s voice, needles bit into her arm. The hazy white world began to spin.

“…Shepard, don’t try to move…”

Shepard…

She heard Kaidan calling her name over the roar of the flames. The Normandy was under attack. She could see fear in the lieutenant’s eyes, the same fear she saw back on Virmire.

“Joker’s still in the cockpit! He won’t abandon the ship, I’m not leaving either!”

Jeff…

Shepard gripped her friend’s shoulders, trying her best to stay calm. Home or not, dying here wouldn’t help anyone. She wanted her friends by her side, but they weren’t safe as long as they remained aboard. Looking Kaidan in the eye, Shepard gave him her orders. Get the crew to the evac shuttles; get them out safe. She could handle Joker. There was an explosion; the whole frigate shook. The lieutenant lingered for a moment. Unsure of what else to do, he called Shepard’s name. But soon his training returned, and Kaidan moved to help the others. As the commander made her way past sparking cables and creaking bulkheads, she kept her mind focused. It didn’t matter what happened the next few steps, Joker needed help and that’s where she had to go.

Clambering up the stairs to the command deck, the airtight doors opened to reveal the roof of her ship torn away. A sea of stars shone down on her, along with the mournful visage of the frozen planet below. Feeling the tug of mag-boots on her strides, Shepard carefully marched to the cockpit. She found her pilot tapping frantically at the ship’s dying controls, hoping against hope he could save the ship. When Joker’s eyes met Shepard’s, the crippled helmsman knew it was too late for the ship. With grunts of pain the two of them made for the last escape pod. Spears of burning white light tore through the bridge, threatening to kill them both. With all her strength Shepard threw her friend into the pod, seconds before another beam separated them. She hit the emergency launch button and watched as Jeff’s face disappeared behind airlock doors.

There was an explosion. Recovering from the impact, Shepard looked around to see nothing but blackness. She searched desperately for the Normandy, for any of the evac shuttles, but all she found was the pale world staring up at her. There was a hissing in her ears: A tear in her hard-suit. The commander twisted back and forth, trying to reach the spot on her back. But before long, it dawned on her that this was it. After nine months of the best luck any soldier ever had, she’d played her last hand. Shepard tried to focus, to block out that animal part of her screaming for a way out.

She tried to recall her last day as an N6, right before her commendation. After nearly two hours of waiting, the young woman had sat herself down on the surface of the asteroid, doing her best to outlast her rival marines. Every now and again Anderson would chime in over the radio, saying another name that had passed out from the limited oxygen supply they were given. When she saw her tank finally empty, Shepard closed her eyes. Back then she thought it would help. She’d close them now… and open them again when she woke up.

…When she woke up…

“…Wake up…”

“…Wake up commander!”

That voice. Shepard’s eyes opened to the blazing white light of a surgery room. As her vision adjusted to the brightness, the chattering of gunfire filled her ears. The woman’s voice was there again, ordering her up and out of bed. She commanded her arms and legs, struggling to sit up. Her whole body ached, like it might come apart at the seams. Suddenly an explosion rocked the room. She was on her feet now, protests from her body be damned! Following the woman’s commands, she stumbled over to an equipment locker. Putting on her hard-suit felt like trying to play the piano. The natural dexterity she’d taken for granted seemed completely gone now. After the longest she’d ever taken to gear up, she grabbed a pistol and headed for the door.

She barely made if out of the room before a machine greeted her with gunfire. Crashing down behind cover, Shepard leaned out and lined her pistol up with the robot’s head. The gun clicked, but didn’t fire. Stumped, she retreated back, looking over the weapon to see what was wrong. There was a heat warning displayed, it needed something called a thermal clip. Pulling back the pistol’s slide revealed an empty slot. Shepard glanced back to the machine. It wasn’t a geth, more like a security robot of some kind. It was slow and dumb, ambling toward her and firing inaccurate bursts in her direction. Biding her time, she let it get closer before striking. Bursting out from cover, she clumsily barrelled into the machine, her kinetic barriers eating a burst of gunfire before she took the enemy down. She wrestled the weapon out of the robot’s grasp, accidently tearing its arm off too. She tugged at the gun’s slide, ejecting the clip. Before long she had a loaded weapon of her own.

Nothing made sense. Whatever facility she was in, clearly it wasn’t Alliance. The equipment she saw looked far too specific and high-tech for a standard hospital. Most of it was diagnostic technology, measuring all manner of things. Some of it was surgical, but the micro and nano-surgery machinery seemed beyond the usual requirements. There was a symbol on the walls; a long, vertical hexagon with lines either side. A Biotech company maybe, the layout certainly reminded her of Noveria. Shepard learned quickly to stock up on thermal clips wherever she could find them; her gun went through them fast enough. Slotting a few into the weapon’s magazine, she was still getting used to racking the slide after each volley.

Her biotics felt different too. Judging by the painful incision scar at the back of her head, someone had decided on upgrading Shepard’s L3 implants. With just a thought her whole body shimmered blue, energy arcing off her. Hallway after hallway showed the bodies of employees cut down by the robots, some of them propped up against the bulletproof glass that seemed to be installed everywhere. Wherever she was, it was high-security. A voice up ahead caught Shepard’s attention and she rounded a corner to find another person still alive: A biotic, using his powers to wrench the attacking bots over a guardrail only to plummet to the deck below. Shepard skidded down into cover beside him, and the man’s brown eyes went wide with shock.

“Shepard! What are doing here? I thought you were still a…” He caught himself. “…I thought you were still unconscious.”

“We’ll chat after these things are dealt with!” Shepard’s commanding tone returned to her, getting a nod of affirmation from the other soldier. Together they unleased a barrage of bullets and biotics, smashing the white-painted machines into scrap and servos. With the last enemy crushed against the wall, there was time to breathe.

“Alright.” Shepard started, struggling to collect her thoughts. “You seem to know who I am, maybe you could fill me in a little?”

“Sure. My name’s Jacob Taylor, I’m security chief for this station. It’s a medical facility, you were brought here after the attack on the Normandy.”

“Where is here? This doesn’t look like an Alliance facility.”

“It isn’t.” Jacob pursed his lips for a moment. “I’m sorry Commander, I can’t tell you much more than that until we’re off the station.” Shepard bristled, but right now this man was her only way out of here.

“How are we getting out of here Taylor?”

“There’s a shuttle bay not far from here. We’ll head through the server rooms and get off this station.”

“Sounds good, let’s get going.”

“You bet, I’ll just-” Suddenly a voice interrupted them.

“Check, check! Is anyone on the frequency, anyone alive out there?”

“Wilson?” Jacob asked, tapping his intercom. “Where are you?”

“I’m in server room B! I’ve been hit and need help!”

“I just found Shepard, we’ll head your way now.”

“Shepard’s alive? How the hell… never mind, hurry up!” Wilson’s voice dropped from the airwaves.

Shepard was grateful for the backup. She still felt groggy and was missing more shots than she was landing. Luckily her biotics made up for it, knocking down the attacking bots like bowling pins. Working together the two soldiers pushed forward, clearing each room of hostiles before quickly moving to the next. After passing up a flight of stairs, she opened the server room door to find a man slumped down alone, his gun at his side. Rushing over to the medi-gel, Jacob quickly applied it to the wound on Wilson’s leg. A wound that looked suspiciously self-inflicted.

Something wasn’t right. The two strangers argued about a woman named Miranda, but she struggled to pay attention. The voice in the back of her head was getting louder. Nothing was adding up: This wasn’t an Alliance station but they had military-grade tech. It was human run yet she didn’t recognise any of their iconography. Shepard had worked with just about every human PMC out there during her time as a marine. Plenty of times the Alliance had to work in concert with other forces. She’d seen just about every tacky, overly designed logo they had: But never this one. Suddenly the conversation was interrupted by a group of robots entering from the opposite door. The three humans found cover and opened fire, tearing them to pieces. Shepard moved to lead her new allies out when Jacob stopped, taking a breath.

“This is getting tense.” He said, rubbing the back of his neck. “Shepard, if I tell you who we work for, will you trust me?”

“This really isn’t the time Jacob!” Wilson chimed in, similarly amped up.

“We won’t make it if she’s expecting a shot in the back!”

“You wanna piss off the boss? It’s your ass.” Wilson sounded nonchalant, but his hands sat rigid by his side.

“This facility… it’s funded and controlled by Cerberus.”

Shepard was taken aback, the horrific news settling in. She’d been captured? How long had she been here? Why couldn’t she remember anything? Her mind turned to Corporal Toombs. A bunk buddy from boot camp, subjected to torture and experiments at the hands of these bastards. His story had sounded like a nightmare, but now Shepard was right where he’d been, a prisoner to this twisted organisation with god knows what done to her. She could feel her heart quickening, the hair on her arms standing up. Her skin disappeared under a blue barrier as her biotics reacted to her mood. Jacob took a careful step forward, his hands up.

“Shepard… it’s okay.”

Then Wilson went for his gun.

The commander reacted in an instant. A powerful biotic punch sent Jacob hurtling back across the server room. Wilson fired his pistol point black, twelve shots plinking harmlessly against Shepard’s barrier. With her jaw set she turned her own gun on the attacker, Wilson’s eyes wide with terror before he crumpled to the floor dead. Seeing Jacob struggling to his feet again, Shepard ran for the door. She wasn’t going to be kept here a minute longer! She stumbled, legs still struggling to carry her after waking up. Guessing her way through a few corridors, she hoped Jacob’s directions were correct. Relieved to find they were, she exited the constricting halls into a large departure bay, overlooking the shuttle hanger. There were several racks of Kodiak shuttles, each one stored and ready to be called up. Blasting her way past the few remaining robots, the doors to the shuttle control room opened ahead of her.

“You’re late.”

A woman stood between her and freedom. Shepard recognised her voice, the prominent Australian accent was unmistakeable. Nearly matching Shepard’s height in her heels, the woman wore a white bodysuit, the form-fitting outfit emblazoned with the Cerberus logo. Her beautiful pale face was twisted into an impatient frown, her dark hair falling over her shoulders as she crossed her arms. Shepard noticed the gun on the woman’s hip, and her cold blue eyes watching Shepard closely.

“Get out of my way!” Shepard warned her, levelling her pistol. The woman’s frown curled into a smirk.

“I don’t think so.”

She didn’t have time for this. Whoever the woman was, she’d thrown her lot in with terrorists. Shepard’s finger slid down around the trigger and the woman blazed to blue life. The first shots struck against her barrier, but soon she was racing toward Shepard. Her speed sent Shepard’s other shots wide, the commander’s arms refusing to keep up. The woman fired her machine pistol at Shepard, burst after burst clattering against her biotic shield. Shepard’s heart was pounding now, ears ringing as the two of them drew closer. When her gun clicked empty, Shepard tossed it aside, bringing her right hook to bear with biotic strength. The woman flowed around the attack, quicker than Shepard could react. She felt the woman’s fists and knees connect with her torso below her breasts, crashing into her with enhanced force from the woman’s powers. Shepard managed to land a punch on her, but the woman seemed totally unfazed, returning the favour with a powerful kick to the gut. Winded, Shepard finally collapsed to her knees. Before she could process what was happening, she felt the woman’s slender hand around her throat, tilting her head. A needle pierced her neck. She fell back, hearing Jacob’s voice coming from the hallway.

“Come help me with this, Taylor!” The woman called out, rubbing her knuckles. She bent down over Shepard, anger stencilled over her perfect features.

“…What a fucking waste.”

When Shepard regained consciousness, she was sitting in a wheelchair. Her arms and legs were restrained, and the automated seat had rolled her into a strange, dark room. She looked around; there weren’t any tell tale signs of interrogation or torture. Her chair was positioned over a small metal circle, but by all accounts it was just an empty, featureless room. With her head still hurting from whatever the woman had stuck her with, the circle she was sat in began to hum with life. A circular holographic curtain rose around the commander. Soon the room vanished, replaced by a large office. It’s massive panoramic window looked out onto an enormous red super-giant star, blazing behind the polarised glass. The office was similarly featureless, with a semi-reflective floor and ceiling. The only things to stand out were a collection of hologram screens, a comfortable chair… and the man seated in it.

“…Commander Shepard.” The man spoke first after a long drag from his cigarette. He was older, maybe fifties or sixties and well kempt. Obviously wealthy from the suit he wore, but the slouch in his posture certainly hinted at a more hands-on approach to how he’d gained that wealth.

“Who are you, what the hell do you want from me!” Shepard’s typical composure was completely absent. The outburst seemed to surprise the older man, straightening himself before answering.

“My apologies Shepard. This… wasn’t how I planned our introduction.” He took a drag. “Unfortunately in my line of work, pseudonyms are a necessity. You can call me the Illusive Man.”

“Cute.” Shepard spat.

“A frustrating side-effect of my reputation. You rarely get to choose your own nickname. Now, as to why you’re here. As I understand it you’re already aware of my organisation?”

“Very.” Shepard answered darkly. “You bastards tortured my friend, killed admiral Kahoku! And now… what, planning to kill me too?”

“Hardly.” The Illusive Man scoffed. “Considering what I’ve spent to bring you back, the last thing on my mind is killing you.”

“Bring me back?”

“Your injuries were more serious than you might imagine, commander. Restoring you from them was a difficult and time-consuming endeavour. By the time you woke up, the cost was four and a half billion credits.”

Shepard was quiet for a moment.

“How… time consuming?” She asked the question, but she was already dreading the answer.

“It’s been two years.” The Illusive Man said grimly.

Shepard felt sick. Her heart was pouncing in her ears again. Holding herself together, the commander took slow, deep breaths. The stranger waited patiently for her to calm down.

“I understand this is a shock Shepard, but the lengths we went to save you weren’t for nothing. While you’ve been healing, human colonies have been disappearing. Tens of thousands of people have been taken: Men, women, children, everyone. It’s obvious our species is being targeted, but don’t know why, or who’s responsible.”

“Then… why isn’t the Alliance looking into this?” Shepard asked, still struggling to come to terms with what she’d just been told.

“They’re stretched thin, still rebuilding their fleet after the attack on Sovereign. The colonies struck have all been in the Terminus Systems, so the Citadel Council has refused to act. Right now those colonies don’t have anyone to help them, that’s why you’re here.”

“Forget it.” Shepard said bluntly. “I’m not working with Cerberus, I don’t care what you did to save me. After everything you’ve done… no.” The Illusive Man’s face-hardened, but a moment later he took another drag of his cigarette.

“I understand your feelings commander. But this is bigger than Cerberus… or you. My organisation is in a position to help you stop these abductions and honestly, I’m convinced you’re the only one who can stop them.”

“…Why?”

“You’re unique. Not just in your abilities or accomplishments, but in who you are and what you represent. You see Shepard, buried in the data we’ve found; I’ve noticed patterns forming. They’ve led me to believe that whoever is doing this… is an agent for the Reapers.”

That got Shepard’s attention. Judging by what she’d heard already, the council had been the back peddling politicians she feared they were. If the Alliance was crippled and the Citadel refused to act, she couldn’t just ignore the threat.

“You’re not going to convince me with talk, Illusive Man.” She said flatly, drawing out the fake name.

“I’d be disappointed if I could, commander. I have a shuttle waiting to take you to Freedom’s Progress, the latest colony to be hit. Find any clues you can there. Who’s behind this, do they have any connection to the Reapers?”

“…Fine.” Shepard hissed through her teeth. “But if I don’t find anything…”

“If you don’t find what we’re both looking for, we can part ways. But first, go to Freedom’s Progress.”

With a wave of his hand, The Illusive Man undid the restraints around Shepard’s arms and legs before his holographic visage flickered and vanished. Struggling out of the chair, the commander made her way out of the small corridor and into the docking lounge. Dressing herself again in her hard-suit, she looked over to the two Cerberus operatives waiting by the shuttle. Jacob Taylor was still visibly in pain from the punch. But the woman, Miranda, she stood as tall as she had in that shuttle bay. Shepard sighed, she wanted nothing more than to leave, to get away from these people and find her friends. Did they even survive the attack? Her heart was in her throat thinking about it. She had to focus, calm herself.

She’d play along for now. But when this mission was done, there would be hell to pay.
