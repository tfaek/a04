Chapter 1: Another Rescue Mission

Eight months…

Tali’Zorah vas Neema tapped the button on her station, causing a new round of targets to spring up. At any given time, the dreadnought’s shooting range would be busy with marines keeping their skills sharp. Pressed for space, the quarian soldiers had learned to be efficient with their time, taking down targets quickly to vacate the spot for the next person. However, the young engineer had found herself in the uncommon position of having nobody tapping their toes behind her, waiting their turn.

Fiddling with the core settings on her pistol, she lined up her shot and started the timer. The room filled with the popping of semi-auto gunfire, two or three shots per piece of plastic. She counted down, first twelve, then eight, then three. As the last shot left the pistol, the heat warning displayed. She racked the slide back, ejecting the thermal clip from the weapon as the one below it slid into position. Back on target, shot after shot finding its mark until finally the last dummy fell. She checked the timer, twenty two seconds.

“Bosh’tet!”

Taking a breather, Tali leaned against the cubicle wall of her firing station. She could still feel the burning in her arms and legs; maybe she’d tried to do too much? The running, the aerobics, the weights, she’d been hitting the gym like a woman possessed. In the past eight months she’d gained nearly fifty pounds through strict dieting and relentless exercise. She was stronger now than she ever remembered being, with a kick that could launch a krogan into the air. That being said, after eight months with only sweat drenching her sensitive skin, she’d started dreaming about showers again.

It turned out her new fascination with exercise had brought some unforseen benefits along with the newfound strength and stamina. After the first few embarrassing attempts to bulk up her regimen, Tali found herself swamped with advice and help from the resident marines she trained with. Away from drills and missions, the men and women of the Migrant-Fleet Marine Corps were a wonderful bunch: Grunts who’d been more than happy to spot the technical young woman and keep her from self-induced decapitation by barbell.

What started as a tentative locker-room alliance quickly became planning hangouts and syncing Omni-tools. Tali had been nervous at first. Though plenty sociable herself, ever since coming home she’d had trouble with relaxing. With things in motion, her mind was clear, but when she settled, idle thoughts turned dark. But she’d made a promise all those months ago, and she intended to keep it. So she forced herself to go, to attend movie nights and bar crawls. Excluding a particular night that ended up with all of them covered in profane stickers, which had been a nightmare to get off, Tali had really been enjoying herself again. Her downtime was now a welcome relief, even if her schedule didn’t include a fun night out or re-watching terrible old vids. Even when she was all by herself, with nothing but memories for company, things were all right.

Tali checked her Omni-tool. Swiping her finger across the glowing orange gauntlet, she checked for new messages. With a frustrated sigh, she closed the tool again. Was checking in every two weeks really too much to ask? She’d been more than lenient with the lectures, and the guides, and the admittedly large handful of gifts she’d bought. Tali caught herself tapping her foot against the floor, her impatience manifesting visibly now. Uncrossing her arms, she shook her helmeted head. It wasn’t like little miss nar Rayya had been any different.

The engineer hesitated for a moment before grabbing her gun off the station desk. She hit the button again and more targets popped up. The timer began and the room flashed and cracked with noise. She’d already sent him a message, asking after the poor kid. Keelah, she wasn’t his mother. Besides, there was more than enough to worry about in her own life. The last few targets clattered back and she checked the timer. Nineteen seconds.

“Not bad, ma’am.” Kal’Reegar’s voice caught Tali by surprise; she spun around on her toes and glared at him.

“How long have you been there?” She asked pointedly, her hands on her hips.

“Long enough to see you shave three seconds off your time.” Kal answered, giving her a congratulatory nod. “Hal check in yet?”

“No.” Tali huffed. “I don’t ask for much, just a quick message to know he’s okay.”

“Cut him some slack. The kid’s late for his pilgrimage, he probably just wants to make up for lost time.” Kal offered as Tali holstered her pistol. “Besides, you were on his ass for weeks after he got out of hospital.”

“He nearly gave me a heart attack!” Tali protested, throwing up her hands dramatically.

“A what?” He asked, confused.

“It’s… a human expression. It means he nearly scared me to death.”

“Oh…”

“Y’know Kal, I get the feeling you didn’t come down here to judge my shooting.”

“Sadly not. Admirals wanna see us.”

Tali shut off the range terminal before joining Kal on the crew deck. Moving single file the two of them made their way to the elevators with a well-practiced rhythm, catching a half-full one. The red-suited marine selected the command deck, getting a series of sighs from the other passengers. While there was certainly a culture of respect for the military, along with a near reverence for the defence of the Fleet, all the propaganda in the galaxy was toppled by the first hint of inconvenience.

The elevator shuddered and made its way up, bypassing other selections and heading straight for command. Kal and Tali readied their Omni-tools as the menacing red letters lit up in front of them. “Authorisation Required.” The scanner slowly flashed from one tool to the other before the doors opened. The two quarians hopped out, immediately double-timing it toward the briefing room.

“So, what’s the mission? More pirate hunting?” Tali asked.

“I don’t think so. The guys who told me thought it was something big. Like, geth big.”

“Great, more scrap hunting.”

“Wouldn’t bet on it ma’am. Whatever we’re getting called in for, I think it’s got ‘em scared.”

Kal’s clarification left Tali silent. After months of strike operations and quick, in-and-out missions, the Admiralty had never been outright scared. Sure, there was always the implication that failure wasn’t an option, but by now the Nualli had a reputation she could stand on. There hadn’t been another op like the batarian frigate since. Hell, nothing even close. Hitting merc bases and securing hostages was easy for First Recon, and even Father’s “off the books” strikes against geth outposts were nothing they couldn’t handle.

Over the past few months, Tali had honestly been hoping for a more substantial mission. Something that wasn’t just clean up, racing the Alliance to a synthetic enclave and wiping it out for some new find. Saving lives always felt good, but the engineer increasingly felt like her assignments were just busywork, something to keep her occupied where certain people could keep an eye on her.

The two friends entered the Neema’s high-tech briefing room to find three quarians waiting for them. Even in the dim light Tali immediately knew who they were. Rael’Zorah’s practised posture and signature glare were a dead giveaway. Shala’Raan kept close to his side, her arms folded. Han’Gerrel stood apart from the other two; his own relaxed figure the mark of a ship’s captain. Even in the presence of the other admirals, the Neema was his kingdom.

With only three admirals present, there was the obvious absence of the other two, who made up the fourth and fifth members of the Admiralty Board: Ron’Daal must have still been bedridden, working under Rael in Special Projects had it’s dangers, like accidental exposure to a virus. The other admiral, Zaal’Koris, had precious little to do with military matters outside of fleet defence. Besides, if Kal’s instincts were correct he wouldn’t have attended a geth mission anyway.

“Thank you both for coming.” Shala’s raspy voice carried the same politeness she was famous for: A diplomat’s tongue. “Due to the sensitive nature of this mission, information is strictly need-to-know.” Kal and Tali bowed their heads in acknowledgement.

“After careful study of the flight recorder young Tali’Zorah decrypted from the batarian pirates, we’ve been able to significantly update our own knowledge of the geth systems.” Han spoke now, leading the briefing.

“The pirate’s sensor recordings took routine scans of both Dholen and Tikkun, along with their surrounding planets. From what we can tell, the geth are building something around our home world’s star. Whatever it is, the construction is too spread out for an accurate guess. All planets show geth presence, with LADAR pings showing clear space station silhouettes in orbit. With such a recent snapshot of enemy activity, along with the data retrieved by Tali back in ’83, we now have a more comprehensive idea of geth strength and positions than we’ve had in decades.”

“However.” Rael interjected. “That isn’t all we’ve discovered.” Activating the briefing room’s hologram projector, an image of the ancient quarian system shimmered into view.

“This is Dholen from the Neema’s last recorded scan, two hundred and ninety years ago.” With a swipe of his gloved hand, the large orange star disappeared. “This is it now.”

Tali’s eyes widened in shock, as an enormous red giant now sat there, dominating the whole system.

“I don’t need to explain that this is impossible.” Rael said grimly. “Dholen is millions of years ahead of schedule for its red giant phase. Which means this can’t be natural.”

“If it’s a geth super-weapon, then… the implications are staggering.” Han added; his own eyes fixated on the crimson monster. “We need to know exactly what’s going on, and we need to know now!”

“Reegar, Zorah, your team will use the Nualli to infiltrate the Dholen system undetected and land on Haestrom, a former colony of ours. Once you arrive, start collecting core samples so we can determine the rate of radiation increase. We need to know when Dholen began to expand and how quickly it is aging. There should be some old ruins still on the surface, but I wouldn’t expect anything to be in working order.”

“It goes without saying, but be careful while you are in-system. The geth make planetary sweeps looking for visitors. Keep in stealth as long as you can, and make your presence small when you’re groundside. We can’t afford to lose the Nualli or her team.” Shala’s voice betrayed her concern.

“Any questions?” Rael asked.

“Admiral…” Tali’s voice caught his attention. “We’ve never tested the Nualli in a geth-controlled system before. Her stealth can fool conventional scanners, but if the geth have something more advanced… I can’t promise that we can hide from it.” Her admission chilled the room. For a minute, nobody said anything. Finally Rael spoke up.

“That’s a risk we’ll have to take.” He said; the silence of the other admirals was deafening. “Now, Project Haestrom will take time to organise, so we have other matters that require your attention.”

“Kal’Reegar, I need you to coordinate a strike force against a Blood Pack fleet in the Raheel-Leyya system. They’re becoming entirely too comfortable loitering near us.” Shala said, looking at the red marine.

“Yes ma’am.” He answered with a salute.

“Admirals.” The older woman turned to her peers and bowed her head. “Come along commander, we’ll discuss the details aboard the Tonbay.” Kal nodded an affirmative before turning to Tali.

“Good luck Kal.” The young engineer said warmly, raising a hand in farewell.

“You too, Tali.” He lingered for a moment before following the admiral out. Soon she was alone, turning back to face her father and uncle.

“Tali, due to your experience with humans, we want you to investigate a distress call we received. Veetor’Nara decided to take his pilgrimage on Freedom’s Progress, a planet currently being prepared for human colonisation. There’s a small settlement set up planet-side, a few hundred people and some automated security. As far as we could tell the place seemed normal, until Veetor sent out his distress call. Shortly after receiving it, our comm buoy intercepts showed the whole colony went dark.” Han said, worried.

“Do we know anything else, admirals?” Tali asked.

“Only that the communications blackout seems to be intentional, but we’re not picking up any jamming signals from local radio. Whoever’s doing it has some advanced gear.” Han answered.

“We want you to go to this human colony and recon the situation.” Rael ordered. “Once you’ve assessed the threat, rescue the pilgrim if possible. However, we can’t afford to risk you or the Nualli before Haestrom.”

“The Nualli, sir?” She asked, surprised.

“Yes, you’ll be in command for this mission. The stealth drive gives you the best opportunity to infiltrate the colony undetected and make the rescue.” He answered nonchalantly, but Tali was utterly stunned.

“I wont let you down, admirals!” She promised, snapping to a salute.

“Of course you wont, kid.” Han said, giving her a friendly thumbs-up. She nearly burst out of her suit.

“Han, If it’s alright I need a word with my daughter.” Rael’s request instantly torpedoed her mood. The Neema’s captain gave him a respectful nod before leaving the two of them alone. Once again the two Zorahs found themselves in the icy grip of each other’s company.

“Unfortunately, Shala’s taken most of First Recon for her strike force. So I’ve got Prazza accompanying you.”

“Prazza?” Tali exclaimed. “He’s a bosh’tet!”

“You’re not a kid anymore Tali, I expect unit cohesion for this mission.” Rael said sternly. “Besides, some of his old squad were available and they’re the best marines I could secure you on short notice.”

“Great, so not only do I get a bosh’tet for my XO, but his third-rate squad mates for support.” She grumbled.

“Make it work. You wanted a command, wanted to do things your way? Here’s your chance. Find the kid and bring him back, no mistakes!” Rael’s frustration lingered for a bit. “After Haestrom… I’ll see what I can do about getting you something more permanent.”

“…Really?” She asked, her tone dryer than she meant.

“Keelah, Tali! I haven’t had you running around these past eight months for fun. I know you’re capable of command; you’re my daughter. But we both know that isn’t enough, nor should it be. The admirals needed to see you in action, to see you tackling situations that arise when people’s lives depend on you. Frankly if it were up to me, you’d spend another eight months out there just to be sure.” Rael half-chuckled, a rare sound indeed.

“Do you… really think I’m ready?” Tali asked, not a hint of sarcasm in her voice. He took a step toward her, holding her gaze.

“You’ll do fine.” He reassured her, his arms clasped behind his back. “Head to your ship, you’ll be departing soon.”

Tali gave her father a final nod before striding out of the briefing room. She proceeded toward the docking cradles in a trance. Rael’Zorah, the man who had spent his whole life watching her every move, who’d always found the flaws in her work or known the solution to a problem just beyond her reach. The man who had led the Migrant Fleet for two decades, one of the most venerated officers and scientists the quarian people had ever known. That man thought she was command material.

Her mind began to race, had she mistaken his intentions all this time? Had he really just been grooming her to be his replacement? To serve as an admiral after one’s parent, it was unheard of. Before long her thoughts were all over the place. Could she really live up to his legacy, did she want to? Being responsible for seventeen million lives, an entire civilisation resting in her hands. The scale of responsibility was impossible to imagine. It awed her… and terrified her.

By the time she reached the Nualli’s docking cradle, the teeming thoughts had settled. If there was one thing she’d picked up about command, it was always best to have a clear head. Instead she looked out at her ship, her baby. Even with Hal gone, she’d continued to tinker on the Nualli any chance she got. Eight months of keeping her engineering skills sharp had made the scout ship the most advanced vessel in the Flotilla.

She’d managed to completely overhaul the Tantalus core and Internal Emission Sink, advancing the stealth capabilities to just a hair off the old Normandy. With some cajoling, she’d had the thrusters refitted for antimatter fuel, giving the Nualli nearly twice her old range. Tali had even managed to customise the main gun’s cycling rate, giving her two shots per second for nearly ten seconds. Even if she was just a copy, the Nualli was a pint-sized powerhouse.

Tali caught Prazza and his team loitering outside the airlock. He had a gloved finger pressed against the intercom, arguing loudly with Meeko.

“Open the damn door, we’ve been standing here for nearly an hour!” He shouted.

“And you’ll stay there until you get authorisation.” Meeko answered coolly.

“If you don’t open this door, I swear I’ll-”

“Enough!” Tali yelled over the two of them. “Meeko, they’re clear to come on board. Last minute changes from the admirals.”

“Naturally… copy that ma’am.” Meeko answered, unlocking the airlock door. “Welcome aboard big man.” She added venomously.

“So it’s true then?” Prazza asked. “You’re running this op.”

“Orders from the top, lieutenant.” Tali bit back. “So it’ll be ma’am from now on.” Prazza backed off.

“Understood. After you… ma’am.”

The lieutenant and his team made way as Tali took the lead. Once they were all on board, the newly minted commander made her way down the Nualli’s crew deck toward engineering. She was surprised to find not one but two engineers holding down the fort. Clearly “promotion” had been taken into account already. Both were new arrivals and jumped to attention when she entered.

Feeling a little smug, she chatted with them for a bit, subtly testing their knowledge through the seemingly laid-back conversation. After deciding they had her old job well in hand, Tali finally made her way toward the dreaded bridge. The small size of her ship meant she barely had a minute to herself before she’d squeezed through the tight entry and was leaning on her pilot’s chair.

“So… Captain.” Meeko stretched out the word with an obvious grin.

“Keelah, I knew you’d be like this.” Tali groaned, getting a chuckle from the other woman. “Nothing’s changed, Meeko.”

“No ma’am, not at all ma’am!” She answered stiffly, sitting up straight in her chair.

“You’re going to be an asshole about this the whole way there, aren’t you?” Tali asked, giggling.

“I’ll try not to be.” Meeko answered warmly. “Bet the old man was happy with you.”

“Are you kidding? I nearly shit my suit when he told me.” Tali said, disbelief still in her voice. “He gave me this big speech, talking about responsibility… and the future… Keelah.”

“Don’t dwell on it too much, we’ve all served with you long enough to know you got this.” Meeko’s amost motherly tone reassured the young engineer greatly.

“We’re behind you Tali, one hundred per cent.”

“…Thanks, Meeko.” Tali gave the pilot a squeeze on the shoulder as she stood up straight again. Bringing up the galaxy map, she keyed in the course to Freedom’s Progress. The Nualli shuddered for a second as it disconnected from the Neema’s docking cradle, before gently accelerating to FTL toward the Mass relay.

“Nice to have the lieutenant aboard once again.” Meeko commented as she typed away at her flight console.

“You knew he was clear to board when I got there, didn’t you?” Tali accused her pilot. The sound of husky, devilish laughter filled the cockpit.

“Captain… I have no idea what you’re talking about.”

Soon Tali’s own lilting laughter joined her friend’s as the two of them settled in for the long flight.
