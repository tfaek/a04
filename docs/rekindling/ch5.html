Chapter 5: Duty

The mood aboard the Nualli was grim. What was supposed to be an easy mission; retrieving a pilgrim from a backwater colony, had turned into a slaughter. Five marines were dead, their bodies secured in the cargo hold for retrieval and burial-in-flight. The mission report had already reached the Admiralty Board, and no doubt a few others. Prazza and his men, veteran soldiers entrusted to the command of an admiral’s child, had mutinied. Hundreds of years of Fleet law and tradition saw the betrayal of one’s captain to be among the most despicable and unforgivable crimes a quarian could commit.

For such a thing, their names would be stricken from the manifests of their home ships, banished from accompanying the hallowed memories of countless ancestors past. To turn on your own kin, precious brothers and sisters of the Migrant Fleet, it was unforgivable. However, these men had been spared such indignity. A special order was contained within the report, a request from Tali’Zorah herself. She pleaded the admiralty to spare their names. While betrayal of a captain was a terrible crime, it was ultimately the captain who decided the fate of mutineers, including their memory.

Tali’Zorah vas Neema was resting in a chair on the bridge, the only chair they could fit besides Meeko’s. Even after some bunk time on the flight back, she still felt exhausted. Half-heartedly looking over the engineering readouts from her bridge terminal, she paid enough attention to know the ship was fine. Her mind was elsewhere, far from the Fleet. What had happened on Freedom’s Progress still felt surreal. Seeing Shepard’s face again, fighting alongside her, it was like they had never been apart. Hearing her laughter and jokes and slipping back into that easy rhythm once more, it felt like waking up from a nightmare.

When Shepard had asked her to come along, she knew she couldn’t. She’d hated saying the words, but she said them regardless. No matter what she wanted, there was a mission to complete. Asking someone else to take her place was wrong, and her commander understood that more than anyone. Watching Shepard’s face disappear behind the cargo bay doors had almost been too much for the young woman. Cutting her suit audio and finding a little spot away from the crew, she’d allowed herself to cry until she stopped shaking.

“Tali?” Meeko called her name, tilting her helmet toward her a little. “You okay?”

“…I’m okay, just a lot on my mind.” Tali answered calmly. “Sorry, did you say something?”

“Yeah.” Meeko chuckled. “I was just letting you know we’ll be docking soon. No quarantine this time but there’ll probably be some waiting while the bodies are collected.”

“That’s fine, there’s no rush. Besides, it gives me some time to prepare my speech.” Tali forced some levity. Meeko shook her head, the patterned fabric of her veil moving back and forth.

“Why’d you ask for the bastards to be pardoned anyway? Scum like that don’t deserve to be remembered.”

“You’re probably right. But what if it was your mother’s name being stricken, or your husband’s?” Tali asked.

“Roto would never do anything like that… he knows I’d tan his stripy ass.” Meeko made a theatrical show of spanking the air in front of her, getting a laugh out of Tali.

“I’ll have to practice my technique for the next time someone mutinies.” She got out through giggles. Meeko gave a long sigh as her own laughter died down.

“So… how are you feeling about the… other surprise?” The pilot asked carefully. Tali went quiet for a moment.

“…I don’t know. Relieved? Terrified?” She shrugged, trying to come up with an answer.

“You’re not angry?” Meeko asked.

“What? No, why would I be angry?”

“It’s been what, two years? Then out of the black your friend shows up with Cerberus.”

“She’s not with them, Meeko!” Tali snapped. Whatever the pilot had planned to say next was bitten down into silence.

“I’m sorry.” The older woman offered. “I didn’t mean it like that.” Tali slumped in her chair, blowing out a sigh.

“I don’t know why Cerberus rescued her. But Shepard would never join them willingly.”

“She sure didn’t seem the type.” Meeko agreed. “But… maybe things have changed? Maybe those attacks on human colonies have made her more, human-oriented?”

“No… never.” Tali answered firmly. “You don’t know Shepard like I do. When we met, she didn’t say anything about me being quarian. No mistrust, no disrespect. The geth data I brought back for my pilgrimage? That was classified Alliance information. And she just let me copy it, no questions asked.” She gestured back toward the infirmary.

“She’s the reason we’ve got Veetor back. When those YMIRs were destroyed, he was nearly caught in the blast. But Shepard saved him. Then that Cerberus bitch wanted to take him away, and Shepard stopped her too. After the Idenna, I know as well as anyone those bastards can’t be trusted. But we can trust Shepard.”

Meeko just listened, giving a nod as she gently manoeuvred the Nualli into position for docking. It took Tali a minute to notice the woman was giggling to herself over the sounds of the bridge.

“What?” She asked her pilot.

“Nothing. It’s just I’ve never heard you talk about someone like that before. She certainly made an impression on you.” Meeko’s observation got an unexpected flush from Tali’s cheeks. She hadn’t realised she was gushing.

“Shut up Meeko, that’s an order.” Her grumpy tone caused the pilot to snicker even louder. Second’s later the scout ship locked with the dreadnought. She got up from her seat while Meeko began to power down the main systems, moving toward the doorway.

Tali found Veetor sitting up on the infirmary bed, awake and talking. She was relieved; the whole ship heard the nightmares he’d had only a few hours ago. After setting him up in the infirmary, the kid hadn’t really spoken again. Little things here and there: mostly when asked if he was hungry or thirsty. With the ship’s new medic among the bodies in the cargo bay, Tali had mostly been tending to the pilgrim herself. She really only knew the basics of medicine, enough to survive a gun battle, but she had some experience comforting others.

After his restless night, she’d made extra time for him, trying her best to keep him calm. Veetor struggled to express it, but she could see in his eyes how grateful he was. After the last of the remains had been collected, they were given the all clear to disembark. She gently ushered Veetor through the Nualli’s airlock, walking every step by his side until they reached a pair of doctors waiting for them. She didn’t know the physicians, but they each offered a hand in thanks for her efforts before leading the traumatised kid away.

Despite how relieved she was that Veetor was finally safe, Tali’s mind was already occupied with a new problem. She turned to look out the docking cradle’s window, the Nualli’s unmistakeable visage looking back at her. She received a message on her Omni-tool, one she’d been expecting ever since the Nualli had docked. Heading up to the Neema’s crew deck, she made her way up and aft, to the officer’s quarters. Soon she was outside the guest admiral’s room. As usual there was a guard posted outside, a marine named Nil. A running buddy at the gym for nearly a month, Tali was happy to see him. Nil brightened up also, seeing the woman approach.

“Hey, Tali!” He raised a hand in greeting, a little too eagerly. “I didn’t know you were… uh, I mean did the admiral send for you?”

“Hey Nil. Yeah, I just got a message from him a few minutes ago.” Tali lit up her Omni-tool, showing the young marine.

“Oh, okay. Any idea what he’s seeing you about?”

“Probably something classified.” Tali said sharply, getting Nil to tighten up a little. “I’m just messing with you Nil, he’s probably just going to chew me out over something.” She gave the young man a friendly punch in the arm as she went in.

The personal quarters for visiting admirals wasn’t quite as lavish as those of the ship’s captain. It was spacious, maybe twice the size of the typical officer’s stateroom, but the amenities were largely the same: A simple bed, a desk with a private terminal and racks for the occupant’s personal effects. Unsurprisingly, Rael’s quarters were mostly filled with paper documents. Paper might’ve been the wrong word for it; the material was actually a combination of multiple organic by-products left over from the Rayya’s food and oxygen production. With both his rank and close ties to the liveship, the admiral had the stuff shipped to his room, where a small portable machine he’d built would convert the materials into usable paper.

Being a people known for technological prowess and distaste for anything wasteful or space consuming, some viewed Rael’s use of paper for bookkeeping and notes to be his strangest quirk. But the genius of a man had never been swayed or deterred from its usage. The one time Tali herself had asked him about it, she’d only been a girl. Stuck inside an environmental unit with her tablet for entertainment, the seven year old had made a ruckus to get her father’s attention. Breaking him from his concentration, Rael had placed the girl’s bubble up on the large desk. Watching her father’s wrist swirl back and forth across the paper had been mesmerising. Handwriting was considered downright artisan to a modern quarian.

Even at her young age, little Tali was already bold enough to ask a probing question. Further distracting the man, she wondered out loud why he wrote in paper when typing on a data-pad or Omni-tool would be faster and more convenient. She watched as her father set his pencil down, before devoting his full attention to her. He told her that he worked with technology all the time. He’d used and mastered almost every kind of device he could think of, and taken them all apart. He knew the functions of machines, the theory in their use and creation. Tali herself had already began to show signs of similar skill.

But the younger Rael then moved closer to her, his presence dominating her tiny clean world. He explained that he also knew just how dangerous machines could be. No matter the safeguards, complex systems were always fragile. Safety required certainty, and certainty came from simplicity. He pointed to the paper. It couldn’t be hacked, or corrupted by a virus. Once locked away, the information was fully secure. The lesson had stuck with Tali the rest of her life. Even all these years later, in the prime of her adulthood, she still kept a steel knife on her boot… should all else fail.

Looking up from his work, a much older Rael watched as his daughter approached the desk. His gaze carried with it years of expectation, and Tali felt tense just standing there. Typical of all their private meetings, silence reigned for a few moments.

“Father, you wanted to see me?” She spoke first, trying to dispel the unease. She was surprised to hear Rael snort through his helmet.

“What the hell happened on Freedom’s Progress?” He asked, barely keeping his voice down. “I send you out on a pilgrim snatch from some backwater planet, and you come back with five marines dead?”

“Prazza and his squad mutinied, it’s in my report.” Tali answered him calmly.

“Damn the report!” Rael yelled. “I’ve known lieutenant Prazza since he was a cadet. He had his problems, but he never showed any signs of mutiny, and especially not under my orders!”

“Maybe he didn’t like your choice of captain?” Tali mused, quickly matching her father’s boiling temper.

“Or maybe…” Rael said dangerously. “It’s because my daughter was fraternising with Cerberus!”

Tali’s eyes went wide.

“I had the Nualli’s sensor records pulled the moment you arrived. A Cerberus Kodiak landed near your squad. Then the ship spots you with three humans, and Prazza’s entire squad slaughtered!” Rael took a breath, suppressing his shaking.

“You had better explain yourself, this instant.” Tali took a breath, collecting herself.

“We didn’t have the firepower to push through the colony’s mechanised defences. We fell back to the admin buildings where we ran into the humans. You’re right; two of them were Cerberus. But the leader of their squad wasn’t. She offered to help us get to Veetor.”

“And how exactly did you ascertain that?” Rael asked, his fury simmering a little. “Have you become so familiar with humans you can pick out their allegiances by sight?” Tali pushed through the insult.

“Because… It was Shepard leading them.” Her answer took the admiral aback.

“Keelah. That’s what this is all about? You played nice with terrorists to catch up with your old captain?”

“I didn’t play nice with them!” Tali hissed. “Shepard offered to help us, and the bosh’tets with her fell in line. If it weren’t for her, Veetor would’ve died on Freedom’s Progress. Prazza was the one at fault! He disobeyed my orders and got his people killed!”

“Prazza had family on the Idenna!” Rael roared. “He lost his parents when Cerberus attacked the ship. You didn’t think to handle the situation differently knowing that about him? Or didn’t you bother to read the reports on your own men?”

Tali fell silent, a sliver of shame piercing through her outrage.

“First two pilgrims die on your watch, now five marines. To say nothing of the Nualli’s footage, showing you working with Cerberus to rescue Veetor. Do you have any idea how much damage you’ve done? I’ve had to intercept everyone who read your unaltered report, supply my fellow admirals with doctored versions. How many times must I lie to my close friends to keep you out of trouble?”

“I never asked you to do any of that!” Tali yelled.

“Of course not! You’ve never given a single thought to your future here! This reckless behaviour has put your candidacy for command on a knife’s edge. You need to grow up, Tali. I cannot protect you forever.” Rael took a breath, bringing his voice down.

“It’s my fault. I made the mistake of entrusting too much to you before you were ready. While you’re on Haestrom, watch commander Reegar. Learn from his example. In the meantime… I’ll do what I can to sort this mess out. I’ll be damned if my daughter’s good name will be tarnished by a handful of poor choices and naiveté.”

Tali stood still, unable to speak. Hearing him speak about her like that, she let tears slide silently down her cheeks. After what she’d hoped had been real progress between them all these months, she realised the truth. He’d only ever see her as a child. Unable to bear another second of empty air, she spoke up.

“I… can’t accept the position anyway.” Rael looked at her. “After Haestrom, I need a leave of absence.”

“What the hell for?”

“Shepard’s in trouble. I don’t know how she got mixed up with Cerberus, but she needs my help. I can’t just-”

“Absolutely not.” Rael cut her off with his answer. “I tolerated your insubordination when you were in mourning. Human or not, Shepard was your captain, and I can understand that pain. But clearly you’ve been lied to. She’s alive, and with Cerberus. You will go to Haestrom, and once the mission is over you’ll report directly to me from then on. This human has done enough to jeopardise your standing within this Fleet.”

“…Yes Father.” Tali said the words.

“Kal and First Recon should be mustered at the Nualli soon. You’ll be working with Dr Tola’Vassa and her team. She’s an astrophysicist. With any luck they should be able to figure out what’s going on with the star. As usual, bring back any geth technology you find. Remember, new iterations take priority.”

“I’d better get going then.” Tali said, already turning toward the door.

“…Yes.” Rael waved goodbye, but she didn’t look at him.

Tali left the admiral’s quarters, forcing a cheery word to Nil as she went past him. Heading back down the same flights of stairs, back along the same crowded thoroughfare, the young woman was on autopilot. She tried to keep calm; she knew this would be his answer. Father wouldn’t understand no matter how she’d explained it. They’d been down that road before. She slipped through the crowd toward one of the side exits, leading out to the docking cradles. She was glad Kal was back, safe and sound if he was already gearing up again. Of course, that made what she had to do so much harder. Weaving through the cramped docking bay halls, she eventually saw her ship, resting right where she’d left it. The cargo airlock was already funnelling in supplies and equipment for the mission, and much to her delight there was a group of First Recon marines clustered outside the main airlock.

Kal’s red armour jumped out at her first, but Tali recognised most of the grunts coming with her. Riah was back, looking completely healed from the wound to her leg. The engineer was looking forward to having a real medic aboard the ship again. Myrr was back too; she hadn’t seen him since the batarian pirate raid. Most of the others were gym buddies, a few she’d served with on missions. Beyond the squad of troopers Tali could make out some new faces, two women and a man. Before she could greet her technician colleagues, marines swamped her.

“Alright, stand down, stand down!” Kal’s voice echoed over the hearty greetings and Tali emerged from the scrum, every part of her suit ruffled. “Good to see you again… Captain.” He said with a bow. The marines whooped and hollered at that.

“Kal, this is a joint mission. We’re sharing command, remember?” Tali answered dryly, tilting her helmet.

“Share command? With big bad Captain Zorah? I wouldn’t last a minute.” Kal looked at the others, terrified.

“You’re more of an asshole about this than Meeko was.” Tali said pointedly, flicking the man’s visor. Taking the injury to heart, he recoiled. “Good to see you’re better, Riah.” She offered a wave to the medic.

“Takes more than a scratch to keep me down, Captain.” Riah stood up straight and gave a nod. Tali groaned.

“So… I can assume you’re all going to be like this?” Her answer came when every single marine snapped to attention, saluting her. Rolling her eyes, Tali made a show of pushing two of her gym friends aside as she went to greet the scientists.

“Doctor Tola’Vassa? Tali’Zorah vas Neema.” She introduced herself earnestly.

“A pleasure to meet you Captain Zorah. These are my colleagues, Nella’Tasi…” She gestured to the woman beside her. “…And Kel’Hatar” She gestured to the man.

“Have you been briefed on our mission, doctor?” Tali asked.

“Yes ma’am. We’re investigating Dholen for the cause behind its rapid expansion.”

“I don’t suppose you have any theories?”

“None, ma’am. As far as we know, expansion like this shouldn’t be possible given the timeframe. Even if the star had been entering its red giant phase three hundred years ago, that still wouldn’t be enough time for this kind of natural change.”

“What about unnatural change?”

“Frankly ma’am, that’s too terrifying to speculate on.” Tola answered, shifting on her toes.

“Alright. Myrr, Zara! Get the scientists aboard, take good care of them.” Kal’Reegar’s orders were instantly heeded, the two marines moving to help them with their luggage. Tola offered one final thanks before being ushered down the docking tube. The other marines followed, leaving Kal and Tali alone outside.

“I uh, heard what happened to Prazza.” He started.

“I’m sorry, Kal.” Tali looked at him, trying to gauge his response. “We should talk about this on the bridge.” She suggested.

“Yeah… after you ma’am.”

The two friends were soon aboard the Nualli. They lingered on the crew deck while the stealthy scout ship pulled away from the Neema. Once the vessel had jumped to FTL, they headed up to the bridge. As usual, Meeko was up there; her helmet buried in her flight console. She greeted the two commanders as they came in, sealing the bridge behind them. Even after they settled in, an awkward silence lingered in the air. It had been a long time since the three of them had felt uneasy in each other’s company. Kal looked uncomfortable, but he spoke first anyway.

“So… what exactly happened to Prazza’s squad? I only got the official reports.”

“He mutinied.” Meeko answered, giving a disdainful shake of her head. “Bastard got what he deserved.”

“What?” Kal exclaimed. “I know he could be uptight, but mutiny? We served together for five years.”

“It’s my fault.” Tali interjected, bringing his attention to her. “I… made a decision on Freedom’s Progress, Prazza didn’t like it, and I guess he didn’t see any other way to handle it.”

“What decision?” Kal asked. Tali took a deep breath.

“While we were down on the planet, another ship landed at the settlement we were scouting: A Kodiak shuttle. We thought it was an Alliance team, coming to investigate the disappearance of the colonists. But it wasn’t. It was… Cerberus.” Tali explained.

“What, you two disagree on how to kill them or something?” Kal asked, bewildered.

“No…” Tali mustered up her strength, looking him in the eye.

“Shepard was there… she’s alive, Kal.”

That struck the marine like a bolt of lightning. He sat there dazed for a moment.

“Keelah… but, how’s that possible?” Kal’s question deflated the young woman.

“I don’t know.” She admitted. “She told me, that they found her after the attack on the Normandy. They spent two years patching her up, comatose the whole time. She offered to help me find Veetor. The other Cerberus agents didn’t like it, but they did what she told them to. I tried to work things out with Prazza, but he rushed ahead to get Veetor first. By the time Shepard and I got there, the colony’s robots had killed him and his men.”

“This is… a lot to take in.” Kal admitted. “Meeko, did you meet her too?”

“Yeah I did. She was just like Tali talked about, polite and decent. No sideways looks or shitty comments. My instruments were dazzled from the airstrike, but I registered a biotic barrier where Veetor was. She protected the kid from the blast. So the hero stuff checks out.”

“…Huh.” Kal managed a grunt of affirmation.

“But mostly it was Tali that tipped me off. Kal, I’ve never seen this girl buzz in her suit like she did with this human. I could hear ‘em chatting back and forth like old friends all through the ship.” Meeko added with a grin beneath her mask. Any other time, Tali would’ve felt embarrassed.

“So… she really is alive then?” Kal asked the question more to the open air than anyone in particular. “But, why would she be working with Cerberus?”

“Shepard said that the Alliance wasn’t able to look into their disappearing colonies after the battle of the Citadel. The Council won’t act because they’re useless; just like last time. So being a pro-human group, Cerberus wanted her to investigate. I don’t think they gave her a choice.” Worry crept into Tali’s voice at the last part.

“This is a hell of a development. You tell the admirals?” He asked. Tali nodded.

“My father.”

“Ah, how’d that go?”

“…Poorly.” Tali said, slumping a little. “He’s covered up the whole thing. And once I’m back from this mission, I’ll probably never set foot outside the Fleet again.”

Kal rubbed the chin of his helmet. He looked over at his friend, curled up against the wall. Glancing over at Meeko, he caught her giving him a look he knew all too well.

“So… what do you wanna do?” Kal’s question snapped Tali back to reality.

“What?”

“Like you said, your friend’s out there, and she needs help. Now I don’t know if there’s too much we do can about it officially, but we’re not gonna leave you in the lurch. Right Meeko?” Kal looked over to the pilot.

“Anything to see her bounce around the cabin again.” She said, giving a thumbs-up from over her shoulder. Tali was utterly shocked, nearly in tears from the show of support. Before he could react, Kal was suddenly pulled into a hug from the young engineer.

“Thank you.” She said, giving the man a tight squeeze. Recovering a little from the surprise, Kal returned the embrace as best he could. Taking care not to crush her friend’s chest, Tali pulled away and collected herself.

“Right.” She started. “First we figure out the sun problem on Haestrom. We’ve got a mission to complete, and I’m not going anywhere until we’ve seen it through. After we’ve got the data we need, we’ll go to the Citadel.”

“We’ll need an excuse for that.” Meeko noted.

“I’ll work a little magic in engineering; make it look like the drive core was damaged from overstrain. We’ll head there looking for special parts to repair the ship. Once we arrive, I’ll slip away and contact Shepard. If all else fails, I can always go to the human councillor, I know him pretty well.”

“Huh, friends in high places.” Meeko mused to herself.

“What about your disappearance?” Kal asked. “Surely your father’s gotta be expecting something like this?”

“Tell him the truth.” Tali finally decided. “I left to help Shepard.”

“Tali, going AWOL is a serious offence. You could be looking at…”

“I know.” She finished his sentence. She looked around the bridge, taking in all the little details she’d worked so hard on. Two years of memories all bound up in this one perfect thing she’d made. She ran a gloved hand across the wall, her palm grazing over a pronounced weld. She shook her head.

“I don’t care. Shepard needs my help, I’m not abandoning her.” She said, resolute.

“…Not again.”
