Chapter 50: Devil's Due

The air felt heavy aboard the Normandy. Anticipation tickled the senses, sharpening the mind and quickening the step. General quarters had been announced, with every lose object tied down and every crewman strapped in. Those who still moved around the decks did so with purpose. The sleek frigate swooped in low over the rings of Xerceo. Millions of tonnes of rock surrounded them, stretching out around the gas giant’s horizon like a massive, glittering river. The planet itself dominated the entire port side, bands of bronze and gold clouds roiling back and forth across its surface, carried on hurricane force winds. Dozens of moons orbited, smattered all around. Some glinted as tiny silver dots: others only appeared on the holo-map. The turian separatists had settled all of them, and all were militarised. Unrecognisable beneath her industrial disguise and all but invisible while in stealth, the Normandy crept forward. The towing gravity well’s influence was stronger near the bridge, but Shepard could feel its effects even in the CIC.

“Still hellbent on coming along?” She asked the asari standing beside her. “My team are professionals.”

“Your team’s a hodgepodge of galactic dregs who are freakish enough to qualify as interesting,” Aria sneered, looking down at the holo-map. “Plus, your barrier’s not strong enough. Passable for a human, but that won’t cut it in this fight.” She adjusted her hard suit.

“And your lackey?”

“Bray’s the only one here with experience fighting Cabal. Turian SF aren’t your usual customers. Trust me, your little friend in the mask could use the help.” Shepard’s lip curled, and she glanced to Aria’s waiting gaze. “If he causes problems, I’ll kill him myself,” she added.

“Don’t worry. I already gave Jack the greenlight to turn him into paste if anything happens.” Shepard’s morbid remark got an uncharacteristic smile out of Aria.

Her N7 armour looked markedly different. Gone was the black spec-ops finish that usually adorned it, along with the crimson stripe down her right arm that denoted her deadly rank. Instead, now the ceramic plating was painted a piercing blue, emblazoned with a white ellipse. As far as deniable operations went, the next best thing after not being seen was being seen as someone else. To the turians and salarians waiting for them, Shepard and her squad would appear to be Blue Suns. Exactly the kind of mercenaries who would dare to try something like this.

“What’s your angle, helping us?” She finally asked, leaning on the CIC’s railing. “You know who we’re up against.”

“I do.” Aria nodded. “Mell’s good, no denying that. And she’s still got those charming delusions of grandeur. Galaxy-spanning legacy or some such shit. Turning the Terminus Systems into company towns and fucking resort worlds. Maybe uplift a couple new species to fill out her workforce.” She grimaced, hocking up a disgusted snort. “Even at the top of the heap her biggest dreams are someone else’s.”

“Sounds personal,” Shepard said, and Aria’s eyes hardened.

“Hardly. Professional rivalry, nothing more. Just so happens that only a few people can play the game this long.” Shepard watched those eyes turn on her squad as they moved to the forward airlock, all of them disguised as Blue Suns. “Although, if you want personal, I’d suggest asking your Justicar friend when this is all over.” Aria looked back at her with a smile so cold and self-satisfied it made her twinge.

“Commander, we just closed within ten thousand meters!” Joker announced over the PA system.

“I’m on my way!” Shepard called back. “Go join the others in the airlock.” Her final orders to the crime lord were done without a backward look, but the sound of Aria’s boots echoing past crew in vac-suits followed her just short of the bridge. Resting a gloved hand on the back of her pilot’s chair, Shepard took in the sight from the view-screen.

“God, look at the size of that thing,” Joker muttered to himself.

They were close enough to see through the ship’s telescope now: Three turian Squalus class frigates, in a triangular formation around a large matte-black shape. The dreadnought. It was huge, just from the cross-section alone it dwarfed its escorts. The ship was completely invisible to scanners, even at this distance: A kilometre-long void of silent death. The smallest of chills ran up Shepard’s spine. The thought of what the Salarian Union planned to do with this thing was frightening enough, never mind what Aria would do with it.

“Any change to their scans?” She asked.

“Intervals are constant, area sweeps. Doesn’t look like they know we’re here.” Joker bit his lip. “Let’s hope they’re not in the mood for stargazing.” Nine thousand meters, eighty-five hundred. As the distance calculator ticked down past eighty-two hundred, she let go of Joker’s chair.

“EDI, prep the jamming signal and sync it to phase one.” She patted Joker on the shoulder. “Hunting mighty big game today, huh?”

“I dunno if it’ll share a spot with Sovereign in my trophy room, but it’s not bad.” Joker agreed. “Good hunting, Commander.”

“Good hunting.” She echoed, stepping into the airlock. Each step was firm and purposeful as she passed the squad. She gave them all a look, or a touch on the shoulder. She made sure all their eyes met hers. And for a certain set, shining at her behind Tali’s mask, she let her look linger. Beneath blue armour, Tali shared a wordless wish, one Shepard returned to her. It was enough to keep her heart from racing.

“Separation eight thousand, phase one is go!” Joker announced. “Brace for combat manoeuvres!”

Hidden against the backdrop of a thousand moving objects, the Normandy struck. Autoloaders growled and launch-tubes puffed as they spat out fourteen missiles. Each of them silently took up formation around the ship. With a wordless command their boosters ignited, solid fuel burning bright orange. Ten fiery spears shot forward at high speed, closing the distance in seconds. Four more followed closely behind. The first salvo broke into three groups of two as they locked up the frigates, with a main group of four headed for the dreadnought. Laser beams from the Normandy, buying the missiles that last crucial second, melted off point defence turrets on the aft of the squadron. Enemy chatter was drowned out by the screech of the jamming signal.

All at once the first volley made contact, detonating in crescendos of brilliant azure flashes. Disruptor warheads worked in pairs to shatter the shields of the frigates, while the quartet harmonised into a warp that stripped the dreadnought bare of her barriers. Now nothing impeded the boarders. In the blink of an eye the last four missiles followed. Smashing into one of the drive cones on each ship. The kinetic warheads punched holes through them, and somewhere on each target an engine blinked out. The squadron’s thrust was asymmetrical, and at such close separation the primary engines went into auto-shutdown, leaving the targets coasting on bare momentum. Just as planned.

The Normandy lunged forward on brilliant antimatter plumes. Vents on her hull opened, releasing a thick cloud of red smoke that soon engulfed the entire ship. EDI’s newfound control of the tantalus core reshaped the shields, capturing the smoke inside their kinetic barrier’s elliptical envelope. Not a second too soon, as beams of ultraviolet and infrared light lanced forth from the enemy. The enemy ships were flipping upright, bringing their lasers to bear. Suddenly the Cerberus frigate flipped end-over-end, her powerful engines directing their white fire just clear of the rapidly approaching squadron. Each finger of invisible light diffused inside the smokescreen, rendered harmless as the elliptical crimson cloud approached the centre of the action. Everyone felt the Normandy shuddering to a stop as she matched velocity with her prey, the last few seconds counted down through gritted teeth. The final struggles against momentum stopped, and the airlock door opened to a hellish red abyss.

“All teams, green light!” Shepard yelled. “Alpha on me, let’s go!”

Tali’s breath caught in her throat as Jane kicked off from the airlock. Aria, Zaeed and Legion followed her into the red gloom, particles in the smoke illuminated by the constant laser fire: Everything quarian inside her roared to follow, to keep Jane safe. But she clenched her pointed teeth and forced ten thousand years of social evolution back down. She gripped her breaching cutter. “Bravo, on me!” She yelled, steely. With a powerful kick she launched herself into the smoke, feeling it rush past her in zero-gee. Crimson gave way to the inviting black, and the planet held in its bosom. The quarian oriented herself. In seconds she spotted her target: A Squalus class frigate with a modified bow section. Their prize waited inside. Jetting forward on her mobility unit she raced towards the ship, blocking out the flashes of ultraviolet in her periphery and the false colour projections of deadly beams all around her. Jacob, Bray and Jack followed just meters behind, keeping pace all the way to the hull. The strain of impact shot through her legs, suppressed by adrenaline. She hissed but didn’t stagger.

They had landed about thirty meters away from the insertion point near the aft hull. She gave the order over the radio, and they marched, feeling the tug of mag-boots the whole way. Each beam overhead sent a shiver down her spine. Finally, they reached the target zone. She flipped the switch on the side of her tool and squeezed the trigger. A neon purple beam fizzed against the warship’s armour. Blue shimmered over her as Jack created a barrier bubble. “Work fast, Dog Legs!” the convict bellowed over the comm. Tali tried to ignore the thumping in her chest.

“PDLs are locking us up!” Bray yelled, and her eyes were up on a pair of laser turrets either side of them. One didn’t have enough clearance to aim down onto the hull, but the other did.

“Bravo to Black Bird, we are being targeted by enemy point defences, twenty meters to our right. Request-” Tali hadn’t even finished before her eyes caught sight of it. Inside the elliptical cloud of billowing red smoke, a hole appeared, like the eye of a hurricane. A flash of false colour came down in a thin green line. She watched it burn a hole of molten slag through the target, completely disabling it. Then the hole disappeared back into the swirling mass.

“Black Bird to Bravo, target destroyed. No other firing solutions detected.” EDI’s voice answered. She could have watched forever as the Normandy’s drive core gracefully opened portals of death with unmatched precision. But a shift beneath her feet sent the barrel of the cutter clanging into the hull.

“The hell’s going on?” Jack exclaimed, her barrier faltering as she braced. Keen quarian eyes scanned the dreadnought. She saw the defence drones pouring out from toward the bow, with Normandy’s lightning-fast laser fire blocking their exit. Further down, a glow caught her eye. Jane’s team. She was nearly through. Tali’s frigate was moving away from the others. The realisation and horror struck together.

“The frigate’s repositioning!” Tali yelled. “I think - it’s going to fire on the dreadnought!”

“What? Why the hell would-” Before Jack could finish, a hail of bullets clattered against her barrier. Turians in vac-suits crouched down on the hull, with more hovering in the void above. Bray and Jacob started firing back, sending stasis grenades from their launchers. All around Tali were yells and the rumble of the hull. She blocked it out and focused. Just a few more seconds… there! The glowing circle was complete, and she slammed down a foot into the metal. Just like the mock-ups, they were inside in under a minute. “We need to get to engineering. From there I can initiate a core shutdown and paralyse their systems, emergency lock the cannon!”

“What?” Bray balked. “An emergency shutdown could fry the main capacitors. This whole ship would be crippled!”

“Not your call, squint!” Jack growled, standing shoulder-to-shoulder with Tali. “Dog Legs says we blow shit up, that’s what we do!”

Without waiting for a reply, Tali took off down the tight hallway with Jack in tow. Bray and Jacob followed, trying to keep pace as she sprinted. The turian ship lurched and rolled on RCS thrusters as it jockeyed for position. In a couple of minutes, they’d have a firing solution. The two women rounded a corner right before engineering, coming face-to-face with an entire turian squad. Eight heavily armed soldiers, some glowing with biotics. “Contact!” One of them called out, and a wall of bullets followed. They were Cabal troops, quickly pinning Bravo to a choke point. The rattling of ricochets was deafening, but Jack’s barrier held firm. Tali and Jacob returned fire, sending more stasis grenades downrange. But after one hit, the turians changed tactics. Their biotics caught the grenades in mid-air, tossing them harmlessly aside. Jack swore, and Tali resisted the urge to join in. Already sweat was pouring down the convict’s faceplate.

But as the sphere of protection began to shrink, Bray pushed up beside them, a strange object in his hands. Tali barely got a glimpse before the batarian lobbed it towards the turians. They snatched it again, but as they did, he hit a remote detonator. A massive flash and battering crack rocked the hallway. Tali’s helmet rapidly adjusted to spare her senses. The turians fell back in disarray while Bray roared victorious. Together the three shooters brought down the stunned troops with stasis shots. The squad was down, and Jack stumbled forward with a gasp. The barrier fizzled with her laboured breaths.

“What was that?” Tali asked the batarian as they moved to the door.

“A couple flashbangs and a disruptor grenade always does the trick with biotics. Especially turians.” Bray croaked with a sly look. “Got sensitive eyes.” No sooner had he finished the sentence then another squad rounded the corner. Bray and Jacob flanked the convict while Tali seized her chance. Cutting through the door to Engineering,

Inside, she saw only one guard. A tall, female turian. Her reptilian eyes locked on the intruder. She didn’t say a word, just levelled her sidearm. Tali sprung forward, curling her body like a spring and kicking with all her might. She heard the bullet crash against her shield as her feet connected. But they didn’t connect with the turian. Instead, the quarian’s eyes widened as she was flung into the railing with biotics. A choking grunt escaped Tali as her ribs made contact.

“You picked the wrong fucking ship, merc!” The turian hissed, looming over her. The first bullet collided with the barrier above her faceplate, and Tali grabbed the gun. Quarian flexibility shot her back up, and she used her weight to twist over the turian’s arm, tearing her wrist apart. The pistol clanged to the floor. Reaching into a pouch, Tali rammed the tip of a stasis grenade into the shrieking turian, freezing her in place.

Her stomach churned and she held her ribs as the ship’s movement inverted, bringing her crashing down to the floor. Dragging herself upright, she reached the main control panel. Power levels were spiking in preparation for the shot. Her fingers flew over the controls, past the security, past the override commands. She forced the main capacitor into a back-cycle, and emergency klaxons blared. Waves of energy warped through her as the core bucked and wavered, starved of electric charge. Then came a final, violent shudder across the ship. She gripped the metal tightly, a knot in her stomach as the lights went out. The frigate drifted helplessly past its target, and the thanix cannon remained silent.

“Bravo h… target… moving to… -out.” Tali’s voice barely carried over the static. Shepard strained to hear it.

“Bravo, this is Alpha, say again.” She called out over the radio. Nothing. “Alpha to all callsigns, radio check, over.” The hiss of static waited in her earpiece. “Legion, anything?”

“Surface scans of the dreadnought’s hull detected unique composites. It is possible they are designed to absorb and block transmissions.” Legion’s answer got a nod from her.

“So, no comms until we take their operations room.” Aria noted with a huff. “Fantastic.” Shepard could hear the long, slow breaths leaving Aria’s helmet. One of the parasite drones had slipped the Normandy’s net, firing on them as they breached the hull. Even in vacuum the commander swore she could feel the heat of those relativistic rounds hailing down on them. A calibre of shell reserved for anti-fighter combat, and Aria’s barrier had held against it for nearly ten seconds. She was big enough to admit that a barrier of her own likely wouldn’t have lasted half a second under such an onslaught.

A dozen troops lay frozen behind them, successfully neutralised without a single casualty. So far, Aria was playing by the rules. They had sealed the engine room behind them, the hair on Shepard’s neck was still on end. They had found a scuttle charge set there, one of substantial sophistication. Even all her experience in bomb disposal hadn’t cut it. Luckily, that was precisely why she’d brought Legion along. The geth was able to disarm the device in seconds. Now Alpha was making a beeline for the CIC. With it, they could track the stragglers down and capture them.

“Shepard-Commander!” Suddenly Legion gripped her shoulder, stopping her dead in her tracks. Shepard looked to the geth. A red light shone from its optic, and a gleaming ruby strand became visible.

“Fucking tripwire.” Zaeed swore. “Good spotting, robot. You see that, Shepard?” his finger pointed up at the ceiling. “Microwave mine. Nasty little thing. Had a mate back on Alban trigger one a those. Flash boils the water and fat in ya, popped him like a balloon.”

“We gotta get through here. Legion?” Shepard didn’t have to explain.

“Acknowledged.” With perfect precision, Legion cut the wire. Slowly the squad moved past the deadly trap. The whole trip from engineering to ops had gone by without another engagement. They’d swept the joining rooms and corridors, feeling the tight, inky black walls closing in on them. Bunks, footlockers, but no troops. The number suggested a skeleton crew. Clever for avoiding information leaks, but not for boarders. The thought drew Shepard’s mind back to the Normandy, how close it had come to the same fate just a few weeks ago. Legion’s scans were interrupted by the strange materials of the ship, so there was barely any intel on what to expect. But as the four of them stacked up outside the bulkhead door to the CIC, she gripped her launcher tightly.

“Alright, counted thirty bunks on this tub. Means there could be over a dozen hostiles waiting for us in there.” Shepard waved Aria to the other side of the door. Zaeed followed to cover her back, while the commander let Legion take point. “Soon as this door opens, I want a barrier plugging the hole. The three of us tag everyone inside. Make your shots count, only a few rounds left.”

Two sounds of acknowledgement, one a grunt and the other mechanical. Aria simply nodded. With a tap on the geth’s shoulder, Shepard gave the signal. The door had been mag-locked, and the console taken offline. No choice but to cut through it. The hiss of melting metal filled the stale air, and that familiar electricity danced in the void between hard-suit and skin. Five more seconds. Three more, one more… the sound stopped. Shepard sucked in a breath as Aria wrenched open the door with biotics. Under the cover of a blue, shimmering sphere the squad entered. Suddenly an assault of sensation poured through the gap. Stark, overwhelming brightness was accompanied by a pitched whine that drilled into the skull. It only lasted a moment before their helmets adjusted, cancelling all sight and sound completely. Completely alone inside her helmet, Shepard called out on the radio. “Alpha, status?”

“I can’t see shit!” Aria hissed through gritted teeth. “Barrier’s taking hits. Multiple shooters!”

“Legion, gonna need your eyes on this!”

“Negative contact, Shepard-Commander. Enemy has deployed chaff particulates. Optical scans ineffective.” A warning flashed on Shepard’s helmet, indicating strikes against her suit’s shields.

“They’re gonna chew us up if we don’t do something!” Zaeed growled through the comms. Another warning flared, more hits. Then it struck her.

“Legion, how long can your chassis withstand small arms fire?” Shepard asked.

“At current intensity, ninety-four seconds.”

“Drop your barriers and take point! I want you to relay the bullet impact trajectories back to our HUDs! Everyone else, sync your guns’ lasers to visual, go!”

“Acknowledged.”

No fear, no hesitation. Shepard knew she could’ve ordered Legion to certain death and the machine wouldn’t have even flinched. Without sight or sound, only a primal sense of awareness followed the geth as he stalked through the protective barrier. She shouldered her stasis launcher, a green beam jutting out from darkness was all she could see. More warnings, more alerts as their shields were depleting. Just a few seconds more and they would be ripped apart from the sheer volume of fire. Then bright red lines appeared from the all-encompassing blackness. Spreading out from a central, unseen point, like the spokes of a wheel. Five, ten, sixteen, eighteen. More and more lines drew back to those eighteen spots. Shepard’s breathing quickened as she chose her targets.

“Aria, spark your barrier!” Her order was followed by a shudder of the room as the asari crime lord sent her biotic energy forward like a wave of vengeful fury. The powerful warping front was enough to shred the undoubtably powerful shields of the enemy. Shepard counted. With no other form of information, the best she could do was guess. After three seconds she yelled “Fire!” The warnings had stopped, and the kick of her gun’s recoil sent stasis grenades colliding with unseen enemies. She swept six targets, until the magazine ran dry and the weapon clicked. “Clear?” she called over the radio.

“Room clear. Dazzler offline.” Legion’s buzzing voice was accompanied by that strong and sure grip. The geth’s expressionless optic came into view as her helmet adjusted its audiovisual displays. Her eyes widened at the sight. Divots covered the front of Legion’s body, white conductive fluid pouring from a stump where his left shoulder should be. The geth’s arm lay on the floor, severed. “Shepard-Commander, are you damaged?”

“I’m good,” she answered, slapping the robot on the shoulder. “Jesus, you okay?”

“Superficial damage to exterior plating, twelve non-critical errors logged. Left arm severed. Repairs needed, but Platform is operational.”

“Don’t worry, we’ll get you patched up again. That’s two I owe ya.” She gave the machine a smile through her faceplate. “Aria, Zaeed, you in one piece?”

“Roger that.” The old merc groaned. “Fucking hairy for a second there…”

“I’m fine.” Aria brushed her shoulder off. “Let’s get comms up.”

Shepard nodded, ordering Zaeed into the other room to check for any more surprises. It was little more than an alcove, but better safe than sorry. The dreadnought’s CIC was oddly small. Shepard had stood in the CICs of Alliance dreadnoughts and carriers, both would have dwarfed this in size. Everything was sleek and minimalist, with a central command holo-table surrounded by control terminals. Already Legion was attending them, doing the work of ten specialist officers, even one-handed. A view screen was emblazoned with the huge ship’s layout. It bristled with point defences turrets, all of which had been locked down now. Dozens of missiles sat in launch tubes and autoloaders, and a massive spinal gun purred along the length of the ship. One thousand, one hundred meters long. Easily capable of lancing an enemy dreadnought with one shot. Or annihilating a city the size of Los Angeles. Not only that, but this beast could do all that from stealth. The perfect first-strike weapon.

Soon communications were back up, and Shepard breathed a sigh of relief. A complete mission success, no casualties on either side. A few of the enemy had been wounded, but they’d survive. The jamming signal was down, and the fictional freighter that had been cover for the dreadnought’s squadron was sending out an SOS. Falsified of course, with a few dozen “concerned responders” en route to assist. Aria’s fleet had done a good job of hiding themselves. Swooping in after the danger had passed. Soon the unnatural constellations grew into drive plumes, and the pirates settled in around them. Technicians were coming aboard the other captured targets, assisting with collecting the incapacitated. Already the turians and salarians were away on a few escape shuttles, their transponders set to come online in a couple of hours. Shepard had remained in the CIC, coordinating her squad. Her current conversation had been the most pleasant of the whole mission.

“So, how’s the removal coming along?” Shepard’s question got a theatrical grunt of effort.

“Slowly.” Tali answered, her omni-tool crackling in the background. “These pirate bosh’tets keep altering power flow through the ship. Brute forcing access. I had to decouple primary charge cables to ensure no damage to the cannon.”

“Need me to come over and keep the peace?”

“Mm… tempting.” She put a little purr in the reply. “I’ve uh, been looking through the frigate’s external cameras. Aria’s ships are well armed for a salvage job.”

“I think they’re used to their salvage running away when they find it.” Shepard joked, getting a worried noise from Tali. “Something on your mind?”

“No, it’s nothing. I’ll finish up and get this thing loaded within the hour. See you soon.”

Not soon enough, she thought. Shepard’s skin itched as she brought up the dreadnought’s external feeds. EDI still had primary access while Aria’s techs patched up the punctured thruster cone. There must’ve been two dozen freighters swarming over the site, all of them retrofitted into gunships. She noticed how tight their cordon was, turtled up and waiting for one of the militia vessels to come take a look. Still, easy to turn that kind of barrier into a cage. She cast a look over to her asari associate as Aria shooed away one of her workers. Now it was just the two of them in the room. Aria made a show of examining a particular spot just behind the main console, pacing around it.

“Looking for somewhere to put your throne?” Shepard asked, standing opposite her.

“And a stripper pole, if I can fit it.” Aria shot back in that cool voice. “Might have to settle for lap dances only.”

“So, mission’s a success, you got what you wanted, your gang’s arrived… this about the time you double-cross me?” Shepard asked, enough edge in her voice to draw the asari’s attention. She casually rested a hand on her hip holster, watching for any tells. As always, Aria was a stone. No flinching as she rose to her full height, no clenched fists or set jaw as she closed the measly distance between them. No fire behind those azure eyes, even as they gripped Shepard’s gaze in hers. There was a small curling of her lips, and a tattooed brow raised.

“Would that make it easier for you?” She asked, her tone perfectly balanced between genuine and snide. “Let your conscience off the hook for drawing first?” Shepard held her ground, fingers tightening over the top of her gun.

“Brought a lot of firepower to a clean up job.” She noted, Tali’s anxious tone still on her mind. “You expect me to believe that’s a coincidence?”

“I don’t care what you believe.” Aria said flatly. “This whole system is infested with turian militia. Not all of us can just sneak away in our stealth frigate. These ships will need another hour or two’s repairs before they can jump. You think a passing patrol would be inclined to take the bribe and keep flying without a show of force?”

“Right, a show of force. That what this’ll be used for?” Shepard gestured to the room. “Omega’s first diplomatic vessel. The stealth and super-heavy gun are the definition of negotiation.”

“Exactly. See, I knew you’d catch on.” Aria’s smirk was not returned in kind. Shepard stepped into the asari’s personal space, making a show of her larger size.

“Of course, you could just as easily use this ship to terrorise every world in the Terminus Systems. Level a city that didn’t pay its protection money on time. Or take out a colony’s orbital defences before they’re invaded. Skyllian Blitz could’ve been a very different story if the pirates had come with one of these…”

“Is that what you think I spent all this money for?” Aria cocked a hairless brow. “To shake down colonies and play ship captain in some stupid war?” She shook her head, her dismissal both earnest and palpable. “Never grew out of your helmet, did you Shepard?”

“What’s that supposed to mean?”

“I don’t need a dreadnought to destroy a colony or terrorise people. Do you know what I have to do if I want a place burned to the ground? Nothing. This is the Terminus Systems; violence is the norm here. No, what I provide for everyone is the exact opposite. Peace, order, I’m the one who maintains this place.”

“Yeah, my first thought was how peaceful Omega seemed. Could’ve mistaken it for the farm back home.” Shepard made a show of her disdain. But Aria didn’t shrink, or even blow her off. Instead, her hackles rose.

“What you see on Omega is prosperity, without the asphyxiation of some busybody bureaucrat.” She matched Shepard’s tone. “Who do you think built those towers into that asteroid? Some mining company too rich and comfortable to realise what they had. And when things got dangerous, they abandoned it. All those workers? Left to fend for themselves, left to be pillaged by another outsider. Gangs, armies. Same shit different flag. But I saw the value in that place. All that eezo is worth more than money, it’s worth compromises, negotiating. Worth peace, even between bitter enemies. That’s power, Shepard. All it needed was the right hand to guide it. Now one little rock is the hub of trade in the entire Terminus. Omega is beholden to no one; it answers to no one.” There was fire in her words.

“Except you.” Shepard noted, cutting into Aria’s confidence.

“And who would you trust, with your mission?” She asked. “The one you’re willing to cross the Council for, the one you’re willing to work with terrorists and criminals for. I’ve read your puff piece news articles, and this doesn’t seem like something Commander Shepard would have done.”

“Don’t compare me to you!” Shepard snapped, heat rising in her gut. “You live like a queen, you reap every last benefit your position gives you. My mission is about saving lives, hundreds of thousands of them. Lives no one else gives a shit about. I’m doing this because I don’t have a choice. There is no one else who can do it.”

“No, there isn’t.” Aria agreed. “You think anyone cares about the people on my station? There are warlords who would kill every last one of them if they could, just to steal Omega’s riches. Those people have nowhere to go, and no one is coming to save them. Right now, it’s only a matter of time before someone musters the courage to blockade Omega, or invade it. But with this ship, none of them would ever dare.” She leaned in so close their noses almost touched. “I make no apologies for who I am. I’ll do whatever I have to, to secure Omega’s future. Which means I’m taking this ship. If you want to stop me, you’re welcome to try.” Her scaly forehead pressed against Shepard’s. “You got the balls to strike first?”

Her eyes held Shepard’s in a captivating gaze, twin sapphires aflame with sheer will. The thunder of boots had never come, nor had the crackling aura of Aria’s formidable biotics. Right now Aria stood right in front of her, close enough she could feel Aria’s breath on her skin. Some part of her knew this was just an act. As soon as she so much as twitched, Aria would come alive with all the fury a thousand years of experience and malice could muster. Even if she managed to survive that onslaught, the crime lord’s ships buzzed all around them like wasps. They would descend on the Normandy, ripping her to shreds while her team were gunned down across the frigates that she’d strewn them on.

The quickened heartbeat of confidence drummed in her ears. They had fought the Collectors and won before, taken down countless mercs in their short months together. Even separated, her squad were among the deadliest fighters in the galaxy. She hadn’t revealed the extent of her own biotic power. Shepard’s fingers itched for the hilt of her KABAR knife. She could cut out the tyranny of Omega with a single slash.

So why didn’t she? What stopped her? Indignation and suspicion carried Shepard all the way to the ragged edge. One more step and she would not only have the thanix cannon, but also prevent this dreadnought from falling into the hands of a criminal. But if she did, if she struck Aria down, there would be nowhere else to go. Melassania’s influence stretched deep into the Terminus Systems, enveloping the regions affable to Cerberus. Beyond those murky borders swam the larger predators of the galaxy. Warlords and robber barons still rebuilding from the failed attack on Elysium. None of them would offer her shelter, most would be happy to mount her head on their wall.

How long could the Normandy keep running? Stopping only for mere hours at one small station or other, always on the lookout for bounty hunters or military patrols. Her disguise would only work for a short time anywhere they went. Was this what held her back from the plunge, practicality? Or was there something more, something uncomfortable to admit?

“Shepard-Commander?” Legion’s voice broke the simmering silence. The mechanical buzz carried an inflection, almost like fear. She pulled away from the asari and faced the machine. The sight of his maimed shoulder twisted something inside the woman. Not everyone had gotten away unscathed.

“Legion…” She worked the word up and out. “Everything okay?”

“Yes. Primary repairs are complete, and software locks have been overridden. Remaining tasks do not require our presence aboard. Additionally, all boarding crew have returned to vessel. They are awaiting our arrival.”

“Any word on the cannon?”

“EDI has relayed successful transfer of the model three-A-X Magnetic-Hydronamic weapon system to Normandy.

“How about you?” Shepard pointed to the geth’s shoulder.

“We are functional. However,” Legion paused, his optic constricting. “We require assistance to EVA to Normandy. Our centre of mass is misaligned with our mobility unit.”

“Of course, I’ll join you at the airlock.”

“Acknowledged.” Legion marched out of the room, clutching his own severed arm. The amount of damage he’d taken dug into the Commander. Enough to shake the last rage out of her. More than that, it was enough to quash any further appetite for violence. With her helmet tucked under her arm, the woman marched to the exit. This time Aria didn’t pay her a moment’s notice. She stopped at the threshold, debating whether to ask, or just keep walking. After everything her team had gone through for this, she wouldn’t leave anything to chance. She took a breath and resurrected the diplomat she used to be.

“So, our deal…” She probed, and Aria shot her a sideways look.

“A berth, repairs, and resupply. And asylum, of course.” She added the last part with a small grin. “I remember the terms.”

“All right.” Shepard gave her a nod.

“For the record, I would have been disappointed if you hadn’t thrown your little tantrum. There are enough yes-men in this galaxy already.” She pivoted on her shoulder, facing Shepard. “Word of advice, though? You should drag that quarian up to your cabin when you get back. You seem a little tense.” She gave her a playful wink. “See you around, Shepard.”

The words followed her all the way to the dreadnought’s airlock. Sure enough, the reflexively frightening figure of a geth was waiting for her. The air hissed and melted away, and Shepard put her arms around the machine’s body. After lining up her position with the Normandy, she nodded, and Legion kicked off from the hull. Drifting through cold emptiness, she turned back to look at the salarian dreadnought. A familiar fear gnawed at her, that she had made a terrible mistake. One that others would pay the price for.

“Is EDI’s software in place?” She asked, so close to the geth she could see every detail of its facial disk.

“Yes, we have placed the worm inside a minor storage server. When activated, it will introduce subtle errors in the dreadnought’s weapon protocols. The main gun will utilise an excess charge and overheat, melting the mechanism.”

“Alright. We’ll hold off for now.” Her answer seemed to surprise the geth. “No need to risk our one safe haven.”

“Acknowledged.”

It both reassured her and bothered her, knowing there was a back door in place. But Shepard knew it was a sensible precaution. However, practicality wasn’t what stayed her hand in letting the malware loose upon the hapless warship. It was the look in Aria’s eyes. Hot and sincere, stripped of their practiced façade. She truly believed the things she’d said back there. Shepard didn’t know if that made her feel better or worse. But at least it was something. One inflexible point in Aria’s serpentine mind, more than Shepard thought she had before. She was gambling a lot on that singular bit of principle. She just hoped her odds held long enough to finish the mission. Or at least, long enough to see her best friend again.
