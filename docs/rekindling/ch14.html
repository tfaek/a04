Chapter 14: Zakera Lanes

“Zakera… Lanes?” Garrus read the large neon sign, confused.

“Yep.” Shepard nodded, looking at them expectantly.

“Never heard of it.” The turian grunted.

“It’s a bowling alley.”

“A what?”

“It’s a human sport: Something laid-back, good for killing time. But still very skill-based.” Shepard explained. “This place is supposed to be somewhere inclusive. For all species to learn and enjoy it.”

“That sounds great!” Tali piped up supportively, looking over the pop-up with her.

“Yeah…” Garrus added, his brow plates falling heavy over his eyes. “All-inclusive, laid-back sporting. Fun.”

“We could always go back to the Normandy. Spend a few hours with Miranda while they finish restocking?” Shepard suggested, watching the turian wince.

“…I guess a little cultural exchange couldn’t hurt.” Garrus relented.

Tali was grateful that Garrus hadn’t put up more of a fight. Ever since they left the Presidium, she’d been feeling that familiar and unpleasant aimlessness. Her mind had started idling in the back of Kello’s skycar, something the engineer desperately was trying to avoid. Shepard had been seated in the front, and Garrus had been quiet again. Despite her dread of silence, she hadn’t wanted to bother anyone. So instead she looked out the window, watching as countless holograms and lights bathed the Ward in colour. Trying to focus on all the little details she loved about the sprawling seedy streets, it wasn’t long before the colours bled together. Things began to fuzz, losing sharpness. She’d slipped her hands between her legs to hide the trembling.

With Garrus sufficiently coaxed, the trio headed in. Tali brought up the venue’s website, reading over the rules of the place as she walked. Glancing away from the screen, she took in the rather odd interior décor. There was something kind of, rudimentary about the whole thing. The room itself was a large, mostly open plan. In the centre was a registration desk, with an attendant lurking around somewhere. Off to the right was the bar, with menus offering just about everything she expected for council races and a couple of oddities.

What surprised her the most was a sign stating they did in fact do quarian-safe prep for dextro meals. Suddenly her mind wandered to the last time she’d actually eaten something. She felt hungry, and not for the kind of nutrients that passed through a nasal tube. Pulling her eyes away from the promise of dinner, she looked over to the left. There were a series of lanes divided up with machines, seating and holo-screens above. She surmised that they must’ve been part of the sport. Maybe each player got a lane, or groups of players?

Somewhere beyond her reading material, the clerk had emerged. She half-listened to him, giving the canned introduction to the place, what they offered for food and drink. He went on, looking from one of her friends to the next, explaining the details offered to turians and humans, when all of a sudden he trailed off. At first the fall to silence didn’t get the woman’s attention, but what happened next, did.

“Tali?”

The orange hologram vanished. Her eyes widened, filling her helmet to match the look of the young quarian in front of her. He was wearing an oversized branded bowling uniform over his green realk, but she would’ve recognised him anywhere.

“Hal!”

She leapt forward and picked the boy up in a tight hug, a flood of emotion filling her. After weeks without contact, after those horrible moments spent thinking she’d never see him again. It was the most wonderful surprise. The young woman was caught somewhere between laughing and crying, forgetting her own strength until the pilgrim tapped her back in alarm. Snapping out of it, she placed him back down, giving the kid some space. Tali looked him over, her warm feelings growing hotter and sharper. She couldn’t control herself.

“Hal’Doran!” She snapped, pointing a finger at him. “How hard is it to send me a damn message? Keelah, I didn’t hear a thing from you in three weeks! What if something happened to you?” She asked, demanding a response.

“U-uh, first of all, I’m very sorry, Tali.” Hal put his hands up defensively. “I meant to message you, really. But when I got here I had to find a place to stay, and there were all these rules I didn’t know about, and I was looking for work but then I couldn’t find any and then…” His babbling stopped when Tali put her hands on his shoulders.

“It’s… okay.” She sighed, giving them a squeeze. “I’m just glad you’re safe.” Her relief was cut short by another voice coming up to the desk.

“Hal, everything alright? You need any help… here.” She trailed off, looking at the pair of quarians.

“Kasey! Come here, I want to you meet someone!” Hal beckoned her over. The human girl was freckled with red hair, about the same height and age as Hal. She quickly joined him, dressed in a similar Zakera Lanes uniform. Tali felt very strange, having this human look up at her, brown eyes full of respect.

“It’s so nice to finally meet you Mrs Doran.” Kasey beamed. “Hal has been-”

“What! Kasey that’s not my mom.” Hal quickly interjected, ignoring the astonished look in the older quarian’s eyes. “This is Tali’Zorah, my mentor from the Fleet.”

“Oh!” Kasey’s pale face flushed red. “I am so sorry. I just thought that…” She gestured at the engineer. “You looked so mature in your realk, and Hal was all excited.” She quickly fell silent again, composing herself. “I am so sorry.”

“It’s fine.” Tali smoothly brushed the awkward moment off. “I just hope he’s remembering to message his real mother.” She added, glaring at the pilgrim.

Looking for an escape route, Kasey turned her attention to Shepard and Garrus; setting up a game for them before quickly helping the pair pick out drinks from the bar. Soon the three of them were out of earshot, leaving Tali and her apprentice alone. He looked at her, clearly sharing an eagerness to catch up.

“Are you doing all right?” Tali asked. “Do you need credits, or anything for your suit?”

“No-no, thanks Tali, but I’m doing fine.” Hal assured her.

“How did you end up working in a… bowling alley?” She asked; trying not to sound too surprised.

“Well, after I started my pilgrimage, I thought I’d go to the Citadel. Y’know, hub of the galaxy right?”

“Hmm.” Tali grumbled to herself.

“But when I got here, there wasn’t really any work. I thought there’d be plenty of people looking for technicians or mechanics, someone to do EVA stuff or repairs. But… nobody would hire me.” Hal admitted, looking at his feet.

“Has anyone hurt you?” Tali asked, lifting the boy’s chin. “Are they forcing you to work here?”

“What? Oh no.” The pilgrim promised her. “Mr Omar’s been very kind to me. When Kasey introduced us, he said I was Zakera Lanes material. I told him I could maintain the lane machinery, that I was good at fixing stuff. He hired me on the spot.”

“And he’s paying you properly? Enough for your needs?”

“Yeah. Rent’s still expensive, but I’m saving up. I’m making even more now, since I started working the front desk as well. Mr Omar said I’d do great with customers, even with the suit. Kasey helped me get started, and so far nobody’s been rude at all.”

“That’s… good to hear.” Tali said warmly. “Where are you staying, you said the rent’s high?”

“It’s not really that bad.” Hal assured her. “I’m sharing an apartment with Kasey and a couple others. We all chip in, for food and stuff too. It’s kinda like being back home, only all my bunkmates are aliens… and naked.”

“Naked?” Tali asked, tilting her helmet.

“Well, not really. Maybe it’s just me.” Hal tried to explain. “It’s just a little weird being around people who don’t wear suits.”

“Ah.” Tali nodded sympathetically. “It does take some getting used to.”

Suddenly, a familiar voice called out to her. The two of them turned to see Shepard and Garrus, drinks in hand. Owing to life on a military ship, Hal quickly introduced himself, apparently having no clue who Shepard was. After a handshake for the human, and a timid nod for the scarred turian, the pilgrim noticed their drinks. He flew off to the bar, leaving the trio alone for a moment. When he returned, he placed a turian beer capsule in Tali’s hand, with the straw already inside. Despite not wanting to drink, she couldn’t help a smile. It was nice to see he was still the same sweet kid she’d worked with all those months ago.

By now Kasey had also returned, and the two of them offered to help the friends get set up with their own balls. Despite the customary three holes for each ball, they were nonetheless divided into two major groups. Hal took Tali and Garrus over to the “Tridactyls” section, while Kasey led Shepard over to the “Pentadactyls” section. The young engineer put it together quickly, seeing the labels for all three-fingered species present for selection. Garrus pawed his way through the turian balls, while she tested out the handful of quarian ones. Despite the meagre choice, she found one that fit her gloved fingers perfectly. She was surprised when she hefted it; the ball was heavier than she thought. Carrying the thing over to the lane, she placed it down on a rack. Garrus followed suit, as did Shepard a minute later. Soon the three of them were ready to begin. As instructor, Shepard was up first.

“So, how do we play?” Tali asked, peering down the lane.

“It’s simple.” Shepard said ominously. “All you have to do is roll the ball down the lane, knocking over as many pins as you can. The more pins, the higher the score. You get two tries, knock them all over the first time and you get a strike, which means you’ll get another set of pins to knock down. The person with the most points at the end; wins.”

“What’s the prize?” Garrus asked, his reptilian eyes glinting.

“A thousand credits.” Shepard retorted. “If you think you can handle that.” She added, smirking at the turian.

“Big talk Shepard, hope you brought your credit chit.” Garrus shot back, flaring his mandibles and sure of victory.

The two of them watched as Shepard took her ball from the rack. Tali noted something strange. Despite Shepard’s bowling ball being designed for five-fingered species, it still only had three holes. She watched Shepard ready the ball before stepping up to the line that divided the lane from the player’s spot. The commander was wearing her N7 armour; she had been ever since they’d gone to see the council. Tali hadn’t asked, but she guessed it wouldn’t be a good look showing up in Cerberus colours. Something Tali personally agreed with, she hated seeing Shepard in that uniform. Despite the bulk and stiff ceramic plating, the hard-suit was designed to maximise movement potential. It didn’t seem to bother her, as she got ready. Sitting there with Garrus, Tali took a moment to look at the woman properly. Maybe it was just the armour, but Shepard looked… bigger than before. She wasn’t sure if Shepard had gained weight, or put on more muscle during whatever rehabilitation Cerberus had her go through, but she certainly noticed it.

In one smooth motion, the commander leaned back and down, offering her arm the greatest swinging arc she could before she carried the ball back down again. At the precise moment of contact, she leaned forward, into the momentum. Instead of launching the object, she let it go on the lane. The ball raced out of her hand, sliding and rolling at speed toward the pins. The quarian had carefully watched every little movement the woman had made, she’d noticed the slight twisting of Shepard’s wrist as the arm came down: Something unconscious maybe? The further the ball travelled, the more the deviation grew until it finally collided with the pins on the far left. Knocking over just half of them, the ball disappeared down a chute, and Shepard stood again. She walked back to the booth, looking both frustrated and happy.

“Was that good?” Tali asked her, a little unsure. Shepard shrugged.

“I think I put some spin on it, I was aiming for the centre.”

She nodded; pleased her theory was correct. Shepard’s ball appeared from the machine connected to the rack, and Shepard was back at the line again. One long motion, the ball rolled, better this time. Two thirds of the pins knocked down. Tali could see the problem, written out in front of her. She had to control her body, giving the ball enough kinetic energy so that the pins it struck would be sent flying from the force into others, causing a chain reaction. Aim was key; a direct hit to the central pins would create the largest chain reaction and thus, give the highest probability for a “strike” to occur. She watched Shepard’s movements, drinking in the details of her form and release. Shepard was strong, and precise, showing the control she remembered in her friend. But there had been another tiny mistake, and now it was Garrus’ turn.

“Alright, point and shoot. Easy.” The turian said to himself, making a show of effortlessly plucking the heavy ball from the rack.

Again, Tali looked on. Shepard sat down next to her, adjusting her armour for more comfort. Out of the corner of her eye, Tali caught Shepard looking at her. At first she thought Shepard would say something, which would’ve easily been enough to pull her attention away from Garrus’ unwieldy form. But Shepard didn’t, instead she joined her in looking on. The turian lined up his shot perfectly, using his impeccable aim to find the exact centre. She made a mental note, adjusting the position from Shepard’s. Somewhat copying the commander’s movements, he swung his arm back. The armour Garrus wore was heavier, bulkier. It made precise movement difficult. But beyond that he twisted at the waist, trying to use more force at the cost of control. The ball came down hard; shooting out faster than Shepard’s had. But the exaggerated movement still modified the roll, sending the ball careening off to the left and slipping into a small gutter before touching a single pin.

“Wha…” Garrus returned to the booth. “What does that mean?” He asked the human.

“Gutter ball. You’ve got to keep it straight all the way down the lane.” Shepard explained, prickling the turian.

“It was straight.” Garrus cooly protested, retrieving the ball from the machine. Another, more frustrated attempt later, and the ball just clipped a single pin before falling in the gutter again. Tali had guessed as much, instead taking the time to appraise the truly hideous animated videos playing after every round. The models were simple, and moved freakishly, but they did convey just how terrible Garrus had done. The mocking fashion of them tickled something in the quarian immensely.

“Just warming up.” Garrus assured them. “Calibrating my approach.”

“Uh-huh.” Shepard nodded.

Tali enjoyed the ribbing, but her mind was quickly sharpening on the task at hand. She lifted her ball, slotting her fingers in and getting used to the weight. She loped up to the line, taking a tiny step to the side. Accounting for her arm’s position, she was perfectly lined up. Her shining eyes watched carefully as the machine on the far end of the lane set down the ten bowling pins. They were lined up in a pyramid, perfect for a powerful, central hit. Thanks to her environment suit, the quarian didn’t have to worry about being restricted. She was naturally flexible, and the suit was like a second skin after so many years. She lifted the ball to her chin, taking a breath of filtered air. She let the unobtrusive club music dance in her ears for a moment. She saw the whole equation, no different from managing a drive core. Account for all variables, and work the problem.

Copying Shepard’s movements, she leaned into the serve with one smooth, perfectly fluid movement. From the swing of her arm to the placement of her wrist, down to the arch in her back, everything was precise. She felt the ball slip from her gloved hands, cutting straight down the middle of the lane toward the stacked targets. The weighty sphere of urethane resin impacted the first pin, driving it back into the others as it barrelled forward. Sure enough, the impact force was substantial. The core group of pins were dispersed, flying into the others and knocking them down in turn. By the time it was all over, not a single pin remained. Caught up in the moment, she hopped on the spot, utterly pleased with herself: Her first strike. She walked back to the booth, feeling a flush in her cheeks when she saw her friends staring at her, mouths open.

“Alright Shepard, if this thing isn’t rigged, how do you explain that?” Garrus gestured to her, raising his brow plates.

“I… have no idea.” Shepard admitted, looking at her with awe. “Well done, Tali.”

“Thanks Shepard.” She said bashfully, searching the machine for her ball. It wasn’t often a person had done something to so totally floor Commander Shepard, and no small part of her swelled with pride that she’d managed to do it twice now. She felt like she had on Freedom’s Progress, hearing the woman go on about the Nualli. More than that, she felt like she did two years ago, that small part of her past still giddy at her captain’s approval.

Soon she was on the line again, repeating her move exactly. She noted that even with the same impact point and velocity, the pins moved a little differently. The trajectory of each subsequent pin couldn’t be exactly accounted for, which filled her with concern. Of course, the feeling wasn’t enough to take away from another perfect strike.

She couldn’t help it this time; she bounced on her toes as she strutted back to the booth. She smiled as Shepard took a swig of her drink, before giving her a stunned nod of approval. She sat back down, watching the ugly victory animation for the double strike. After a couple of fumbling attempts, Garrus began to follow suit, knocking down the majority of the pins with each go. Shepard was getting better too, consistently getting most of the pins. There was more deviation with them however, sometimes a strike; other times only half the targets. Tali struggled to really understand the issue, but she didn’t mind. By now the two of them were drinking and playing. The alcohol seemed to be negatively affecting Shepard’s aim, while the opposite appeared to be true for Garrus.

After the trio had cleared the first half of the frames, they decided now was a good time to take a break and get something to eat. After ordering, they sat down at one of the tables near the bar. It was a quiet day for the place, and their table afforded a little elbowroom. Tali knew she shouldn’t have, but after getting into the spirit of things, she’d been taking victory sips from her beer capsule. The taste was typically unpleasant for sterilised, low cost liquor, but she was enjoying the buzz. The gourmet soup she got helped, with the spicy turian vegetables quickly drowning out the aftertaste.

The three of them mostly chatted about the game, with Shepard unable to resist giving Garrus pointers for better performance. Turians were often difficult to read, due to the plates covering their face, but it was very evident that the helpful advice was only aggravating him. Between mouthfuls the issue of the prize was brought up again, determining how the thousand credits should be doled out. It was quickly decided that the winner could make that call. Shepard’s head start on the drinking caught up to her, and she excused herself from the table. For a minute, Tali wasn’t sure what to talk about with Garrus. She didn’t want to needle him about bowling, but she wasn’t sure what else they could talk about. Before she could settle on a topic, the turian spoke first.

“So, what’s your secret?” He asked her, raising a brow plate.

“My… secret?” She tilted her helmet, confused.

“To this damn game.” Garrus waved his bottle toward the lanes. “You’ve been getting perfect strikes every time.”

“Not every time.” Tali protested, before he raised a hand to shush her.

“You missed one shot.” He admitted. “Which was probably on purpose.”

“Wha-”

“Look, you’ve got some system. I just wanna know what it is. It’s not like I’m going to catch up to you.” He was practically begging her now.

“I don’t know, it’s just… math?” Tali shrugged. “Positioning, velocity, point of impact…”

“Right.” Garrus nodded. “…Thanks.”

Tali took another sip of her drink, feeling substantially more awkward after the exchange. She wanted to talk more, to get comfortable again. But there was just something blocking it. Questions, accusations, constant noise that kept the young woman from just slipping back into their groove. Before her buzzing mind could settle, the turian spoke again.

“Y’know, this wasn’t exactly how I saw my week turning out.” He admitted to her, looking around briefly for Shepard.

“…Yeah.” She agreed, her eyes on the table as she took a long sip. “It’s… crazy.”

“Two years go by, and the bitch just waltzes right back into our lives.” Garrus mused. “Like she never left.”

“You sound angry.” She noted, causing the turian’s eyes to widen.

“No.” He assured her. “It’s not that… maybe I’m just mad about this stupid game.” He let out a breath. “It’s just… I don’t know. It feels like I’m getting all this time back, but I never noticed it gone in the first place. Like the last two years were…”

“…Were just a dream?” She finished the turian’s thought. “Like you just woke up again?”

“Yeah.” Garrus agreed, picking at the remaining food on his plate. “…Like I was wasting my time.” He said quietly to himself. Tali’s brow knit, a pang of empathy running through her. She looked at him, seeing something she remembered after the Normandy attack. The friend she trusted, losing everything.

“Garrus…” She started.

“I’m sorry.” He interrupted her. “I should’ve kept in contact, checked up on you.” He pierced the meat with his utensil, his mandibles pressing against his face. “I… wasn’t in a good place. A whole lot of things just, happened at once.”

“I get that…” She let out the answer in a long breath.

“Then Shepard shows up… and I…” He closed his eyes. “…Now I don’t know anymore.”

Tali reached out, putting a hand on his shoulder. Giving Garrus a gentle shake, he looked at her. Perhaps it was a combination of alcohol, a winning streak and just getting to talk with him again. But the longer she looked at the scarred turian, the harder it was to stay distant. Even if he didn’t share the details, she knew exactly how he felt.

“I’m glad you’re here.” She said warmly.

“Thanks.” He flared his mandibles, giving her a smile. “Probably just so you can have someone to beat at this… bowling game.” He surmised.

“I’m beating Shepard too.” She corrected him, just in time for the woman herself to sit back down next to them.

“Trash talking me during my big comeback?” The commander asked, taking a sip from her glass.

“Maybe.” Garrus nodded, raising his own drink.

“Mostly I was just gloating.” Tali joined in. “Reminding him just how easily I’m going to kick your-”

There was a sound, a bang from one of the machines. Something routine or broken she couldn’t be sure, but the effect was immediate.

Tali suddenly felt short of breath. Her eyes widened, looking around the room. She could feel her stomach churning, threatening to make her wretch. Spotting the restroom, she turned to see both her friends had realised something was wrong.

“Tali? You okay?” Shepard asked, losing all pretence of joking around.

“Just a… suit thing…” She assured her between breaths. “Back in a minute.”

Without waiting for reply, Tali took off from the table. She kept herself as composed as possible, the temperature was rising in her suit by the second. Putting one foot in front of the other, she tapped the restroom door with a shaky hand. Slipping inside, she secluded herself in a cubicle, locking it. Already she could feel more symptoms coming. Her breathing grew quicker and more ragged, skin beading with sweat. The quarian wrapped her arms around her belly, enduring what felt like a vice clamping down on her intestines. The pain didn’t stop, instead she felt flashes of heat and cold. She shivered all over, her ears filled with the pounding of her heart, broken up only by her shallow breaths.

When the tiny world of the cubicle began to spin, she screwed her eyes shut. Voices began to push past the sounds of her body. Yelling, screaming. The music outside bled away, replaced by the booming of explosions, the cracking of gunfire. Even in self-imposed darkness, she could see them again. Advancing through the rubble, amidst the chaos. She could see Riah again, lifted into the air by a cold, grasping hand before the machine monster buried its claws in her gut.

Her eyes opened, looking at the graffiti on the cubicle door. She doubled over on the toilet seat, her head between her legs as she struggled to regain herself. Her breathing slowed again, the beating of her heart slowly fading behind the muffled music outside. She felt weak, slumping back against the seat. Covering her mask with her gloved hands, Tali tried to calm herself. She hated this, so much time recovering, and now she was right back where she started. She couldn’t even enjoy time with her friends, with Shepard, before her body forced her to relive it. She’d promised her, she wouldn’t be a burden.

Why… why couldn’t things just be like they were?

By the time she returned to the table, Shepard and Garrus had forgotten their half-eaten plates. The turian eyed her carefully, but didn’t say anything. Unsurprisingly, it was the commander who got up, meeting her just before she sat back down.

“You okay?” She asked, any pretence of joking around utterly gone. “You looked a little shaky when you left.”

“Yeah.” Tali gave her a nod, ignoring the tears still rolling down her sensitive cheeks. “I’m fine, just something I had to check on.” She pointed to her arm. “Sometimes it’s more comfortable to do in private.”

“Are you sure? If you’re not feeling well, we can head back. Get you checked out?”

“Please, Shepard. I’m really okay.” She pleaded, trying to convince her. “I want to finish our game.”

She could tell by the way Shepard looked at her; she didn’t really buy it. A part of her wanted Shepard to ask, to press for more. But another part abhorred the idea. With everything already on her plate, the last thing Shepard needed was to babysit someone else. Tali forced a spring in her step as she led the way back to the booth they were playing at. She sat down, having trashed her beer capsule. Maybe the alcohol had nothing to do with it, but she didn’t want to take the risk. So instead she watched as her friends took heir shots, clumsily trying to match their earlier form. Riding the buzz she already had, Tali began to relax again. She assured herself it was something minor, a one-off event that could be curtailed now that she knew what to look for. She expected this; it was normal. Who wouldn’t be a little shaken up after such a dangerous mission? The young woman took strength in knowing that… or at least, wanting to believe it.

Soon it was her turn, and the small sliver of distraction that now hung in her mind was enough to bring an end to the perfect strikes. The frames played out, with Shepard and Garrus slowly gaining ground, while Tali managed with either single strikes or spares. By the time the whole thing was over, she was comfortably sitting at the top. Shepard made a big deal of it, showering the woman with praise while the turian noted her strategy was impressive. During the commotion, Hal and Kasey had found a spare minute to join in congratulations. It was a good feeling, until Tali noticed the pilgrim tilting his mask at her, wanting to speak privately.

Delaying her gratitude, she asked the others for a few minutes alone with her old protégé before following him somewhere discreet. Winding past a couple of doors into an employee only area, Hal took a seat on a box that had been repurposed, along with another one for her. Tali took her seat, considering perhaps the box was a little small for her, and looked at the boy. She could tell he was nervous, possibilities already forming in her head before he aired the problem.

“So…” Tali started. “What’s up?”

“I was just, wondering…” Hal wrung his hands. “How long did it take for you to… find your pilgrimage gift?”

“Hal, you don’t need to worry about rushing to find your gift.” Tali tried to assure the boy. “This is your pilgrimage. It’s meant to be a journey, to help you learn about the galaxy.”

“I know.” He nodded. “I was just… curious, I guess.”

“It took me about six months.” She informed him. “And that was with a lot of help. Nobody is expecting you to be done by then. You can take your time.”

“Oh, that’s… good to know.” Hal said cheerfully. Tali gave him a friendly nod, but she could see the tension still rising in him.

“But what if… I mean how long would it be before people started worrying about me?” He asked, visibly agitated. “I don’t want my parents to worry, o-or to send people out looking. It’s just… that…”

“Hal?” Tali put a hand on his shoulder. “What’s this really about?” She watched him take a deep breath.

“What if I…what if I never go back?”

Tali was shocked. All that time she’d spent with him, it never occurred to her that he didn’t enjoy the Fleet. He was always ready to help, eager to do his part. Being born on the Neema to a military family, it seemed like he would relish the opportunity to go home. But she could see it in his eyes; he meant it. And he was terrified.

“You don’t want to go back home?” She asked.

“…No.” He shook his helmeted head. “I know it’s selfish, but out here, it’s like a whole other world. My friends… They don’t talk about what I should be doing, or where I would benefit others the most. They just wanna hang out, enjoy things.”

“What about your friends back home, or your parents?” She asked gently.

“I don’t want them to worry about me.” He said quietly. “But they always talked about the Fleet. They wanted me to be an engineer, to benefit the cause. I just… I can’t live like that.” His head sunk. “You’re the only real friend I had back there.”

“I know things back home are difficult: But life out here, for a quarian? Hal, it isn’t easy.” She gave his shoulder a squeeze, getting the boy’s attention. “Especially if you’re alone.”

“I know.” He nodded. “But, my friends really care about me. Kasey really…” He trailed off, bringing his feet together. “She helped me get this job, she shared her apartment with me. She’s always so kind, and we talk all the time, and we do… everything together.”

Hal went quiet for a moment. The realisation dawned on the engineer as she looked out past the door. Now it all made sense.

“Is Kasey… is she why you want to stay?”

He nodded weakly.

“I like her Tali… I mean… I really like her.” His voice was shaking.

“…Is that… is there something wrong with me?”

He looked at her, tears in his eyes. She could see just how scared he was. All those years spent back home, being so used to other quarians. She knew how scary those feelings could be. She put her arm around him, pulling the boy into a hug.

“Hal, there is nothing wrong with you.” She cooed, squeezing him tight. “You are a sweet, smart young man. If you care about Kasey, then she must be special, human or not.”

He returned her embrace, squeaking out a quiet “thanks”. For a while the two of them sat there, enjoying each other’s company after so long apart. Tali hadn’t wanted to take away from Hal’s moment, but in her heart she understood his feelings all too well. She remembered what it was like, to discover something about yourself you never even knew was there. To find comfort and acceptance in someone beyond the Fleet, beyond everything she’d ever known: All the suffering that had come from waiting, from being unsure of which path to take. She didn’t want that for him. Finally the two quarians broke away, the young pilgrim looking more relaxed.

“I’m sorry I didn’t contact you earlier.” Hal’s tone was sombre. “I was worried I’d mess things up, say something stupid about what was going on. I didn’t want to make anyone upset.”

“I understand, believe me.” Tali looked him in the eye. “Feelings like these, they’re… I just want you to be happy. If that means staying here, then that’s fine with me.”

“Okay.” The boy blew out a sigh of relief. “We should probably get back to the others.”

“Lead the way.” She chuckled, following him out.

By the time they’d reached the booth again, Hal was nearly bouncing. Shepard and Garrus had spent the time wowing the young human, filling her head with equal parts adventure and nightmares no doubt. The two quarians got within earshot just in time to hear the old C-Sec agent describing a fight with a thresher maw. He waved his arms, gesticulating the scene to Kasey. The whole thing spellbound her until she noticed Hal. Tali broke away from the pilgrim, giving the two some space. It was barely a moment before the girl was recreating the scene, holding the boy’s wrapt attention. Despite the unpleasant feelings still lingering in the back of her mind, the sight made the engineer smile. After everything he’d been through, nobody deserved a little happiness more than Hal did. She moved to rejoin her friends, seeing Kasey returning to the desk.

“Hey, Tali.” He called her name, spinning the engineer around. He looked up at her.

“Thanks… for everything.” With a parting nod, he went to join Kasey again, helping her with some paperwork.

“Giving the boy some pilgrimage advice?” Garrus asked, coming over to meet her with Shepard beside him.

“Something like that.” She answered, glancing back to the couple.

The trip back to the public shuttles was a lazy one, punctuated by conversation about the match. In the end, Tali hadn’t had the heart to strip her friends of a thousand credits, so she’d settled for five hundred from each of them. Garrus had asked for a reprieve, being a first timer, which brought out an unexpected guffaw from Shepard. She promised the turian that they’d have another game, just the two of them. The way the commander put it; their next encounter would be a free training session, to help the struggling alien with all the intricacies of the noble human sport. Garrus’ response hadn’t been quite so eloquent, or condescending. Instead, he’d simply threatened to use Shepard’s shaved head for lane practise. The morbid scenario had the two of them giggling like idiots.

Tali enjoyed the spectacle, just as she always had. Nothing ever felt more natural than hearing the two of them nip at each other. But that was the problem. This wasn’t natural. Even after getting something out of him, Garrus had barely explained anything. She could tell he was hurting, but had no idea how to get in anymore. It wasn’t like it used to be, they were different people now. There were new walls; new winding paths neither could travel. The anger remained, the want to accuse. It was childish and petty and yet, it wouldn’t leave her alone. She wanted to help, and beneath that she wanted answers. As for Shepard, though it shamed her a little to admit it, Shepard could always read her far easier than she could Shepard. There was something there, hidden away in the details of her face. Something hard to focus on when she heard Shepard’s laugh and felt the commander’s eyes on her. Whatever it was, she needed to figure it out. She couldn’t ignore the scarring, the dents in the wall. The woman needed help, she was certain of it. Feeling the return of a tremor in her arm, Tali hoped that whatever she could manage would be enough.
