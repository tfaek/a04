Chapter 25: Laid Bare

Shepard stood behind the shuttle door, holding Kasumi in her arms. She watched the display on the cracked view-screen, counting down the seconds as the Normandy’s cargo bay pressurised. There was debris littered everywhere, with gym equipment and cargo thrown about during the fighting. Artificial gravity had failed more than once, judging by some of the damage. The mass effect core must’ve been pushed to breaking point. Of course, none of that had even crossed her mind as they approached. Instead it was the glowing scars raked across the ship’s hull, heavy armour melted and sliced open by the Collectors’ deadly weapons.

It was the trailing plume of smoke and gasses, leaking from the stern: So much mayhem, the consequences of fighting an impossible battle. The characteristic blaze of the Normandy’s engines had been absent, with two of them being completely dark. The remaining two barely flickered with life. Wounded and adrift, she was limping her way forward, just trying to keep her orbit. The whole thing should’ve filled Shepard with rage; these freakish bastards had dared to strike at her ship and crew. But any anger she felt now was buried under a palpable sense of fear: For her ship, for her people, for the injured woman in her arms. Even with Mordin’s help, Kasumi’s leg still bore a gruesome gash.

The doors opened to a hustling group of crew, working hard to clear a space for the Kodiak to land. Still a few feet off the ground, Shepard wasn’t going to wait a second longer. She hopped out, groaning as her feet hit the floor. Everyone filed out quickly behind her, looking for what they could do to help. Zaeed and Jacob stayed behind, pitching in with the cargo bay crew. Tali and Garrus kept close, along with Mordin and Miranda. All of them stole glances around the large open room, the shock of it all still sinking in.

Shepard pushed ahead, marching towards the elevator. Her eyes were on that small cubicle, before darting back down to her master thief. Kasumi was awake, her face in full view with her hood around her shoulders. She was younger than Shepard thought. Realising the commander’s concern, she gave Shepard her best smirk, vainly trying to conceal the pain she was in. Shepard got inside the elevator without pause, turning to find the others anxiously waiting for her orders.

“Miranda, Mordin, with me.” Shepard moved aside to give them room. “Doctor Chakwas is going to need all the help she can get, I want you two tending to the wounded.”

“Of course Shepard. Do what I can.” Mordin’s tone was atypically dour. Miranda kept silent, giving a nod as she slotted in beside him. A twinge ran up Shepard’s back, and her grip faltered around Kasumi.

“Shepard?” Tali’s keen eyes had caught everything, and she saw Tali moving toward her.

“I need you in engineering.” Shepard strained, standing tall again. “We don’t have much time before Alliance ships turn up.” She shot a look to the turian at Tali’s side. “Garrus, you go with her. Gotta get the big girl moving again.”

“We’ll get it done, Shepard.” Garrus locked eyes with her, giving his friend a knowing look before the doors closed.

Cloistered inside the elevator, Shepard could feel Miranda’s gaze on her. Pressed up so close together, she could sense the truth boiling just beneath Miranda’s perfectly constructed exterior. To the world outside, she was readying for the task at hand. Behind that gaze could’ve been determination, or a grim understanding of how bad things were. But to Shepard, there was nothing but pure, naked fury. Of all her crew, all her teammates, perhaps it was only Miranda who shared her knowledge of the truth. She had known, like Shepard, that they weren’t ready for this fight: That even the new Normandy wasn’t ready for this fight.

Miranda had heard her over the radio, patched in as the second-in-command. Despite the risks, Shepard had given the order to pursue the Collector ship. Miranda had known, down on Horizon, that the battle was lost. Seeing the ship rise up into the sky was as clear a signal as any. But Shepard couldn’t let it go. So she gave the order, deliberately ignoring Joker’s reservations. Miranda didn’t say a word. Shepard’s own insulting retort came back to her, playing in her mind as the doors opened onto the Crew Deck.

“That’s not your call to make… It’s mine.”

As she rounded the corner towards the med-bay, Shepard stopped dead. There were a dozen stretchers set up outside, each one filled with a familiar face. The door was open, and inside she could see Karin tending to four more patients, laid out on the beds. Several more crew were huddled nearby, nursing injuries. The sight put a knot in Shepard’s stomach, but she kept moving forward, leading the other two. She caught a few looks, people still reeling from what had happened. They were hurt, and scared. There was quiet murmuring, most of it indistinct. But as she walked by, it quickly stopped. Carefully bringing Kasumi through the doorway, her presence immediately captured Karin’s attention.

Miranda and Mordin set to work, looking over the bedridden patients to assess their conditions. Among them, a particular face caught Shepard’s eye. It was Gabby. She’d suffered a horrible burn, melting the skin of her left cheek and down her neck. The sight was almost too much for Shepard. After how friendly Gabby had been when Shepard came aboard, how she’d helped watch over Tali during her harder moments, the capable engineer had certainly earned Shepard’s trust. Now she was lying on a slab, mutilated, because of Shepard. Gabby’s shallow breathing was the only thing that kept the woman upright.

“Commander? God, are you alright?” Karin’s voice pulled her away from Gabby. She saw Karin looking over Shepard’s wounds, carefully examining them through her torn under-suit.

“I… yeah, I’m fine.” Shepard managed through the flood of emotions. “Kasumi’s leg, is there anything else we can do?”

“She’s…” Kasumi winced through her snark. “…A real drama queen, huh?”

Shepard held her steady, suppressing her shakes as Karin inspected the cauterised gash. With barely a word she went over to her supplies, before spraying another layer of medi-gel over the wound. Kasumi shivered from the pain and the cold, struggling to grin through it all. Karin sealed the whole thing with a bandage strip. By the time it was done, she’d already guessed Shepard’s question.

“Kasumi’s injuries will heal Commander. But…” She gave a frustrated shrug. “I believe she would be more comfortable in her own bed.”

“…Alright.” Shepard gave a nod, not wanting to take up her time a second more than necessary. Redoubling her effort, she turned to leave.

“Come back here once she’s settled, your injury needs looking at.” Karin’s medical instruction followed her out the door. It was professional and dispassionate, just what Shepard needed in that moment.

The short walk across the deck was surreal. Everywhere Shepard looked, there was disarray. Holographic screens stuttered and buzzed, barely keeping active for seconds at a time. Half the lights were out, replaced instead by a row of crimson warning strips. There were cables loose, splayed out on the floor. One of the sleeper pods had been broken, stuck halfway extended. Most noticeable of all was the haze in the room: An air processor had blown, not quite filtering out the dust and grime.

One detail that was thankfully absent, were fires or flying sparks. In the time it had taken her to get back, things had been brought mostly under control. But even still, the sight was a terrible one. Just a few hours ago this place was a shining, pristine example of the new Normandy’s youth: Fresh from the berth, the last few bits of paint still drying on her wings. Now she’d been beaten and scarred, the illusion of her invincibility shattered. It dredged up memories, of all-too-familiar flames, burning down all-too-familiar hallways.

As gently as she could, Shepard laid Kasumi down on her bed. After struggling to carry her for so long, this last effort was enough to make Shepard stumble. There was no hiding her fatigue as she caught herself. Kasumi looked up at her, but didn’t say a word. By the time Shepard had recovered, Kasumi’s face had disappeared beneath her hood again, with only her painted lips and the glint of her eyes showing. Shepard took a moment to breathe, mentally preparing for whatever else she’d see as she cobbled things back together. The silence in between her wheezing breaths was broken by a quiet sniggering sound. By the time she realised just what she was hearing, her eyes were on Kasumi again. The thief made no effort to hide it, instead flashing her a smile.

“Saw you take one for the team back there, Shep.” Her levity was tinged with pain. “Pretty nasty burn for your troubles.”

“Didn’t want you to be lonely.” Shepard answered with a fake chuckle. “My back was chilly anyway.” She appreciated Kasumi’s hoarse giggles, genuine or not.

“You saved her life.” Kasumi noted, her voice sombre. Shepard looked down at the floor, feeling that momentary spike of terror come back to her. The particle beam had been inches away from Tali when Shepard had noticed it. If she’d hesitated for even a second… She gave the thief a shrug.

“It’s just another scar for me.” Shepard mused, ignoring the feeling of hot needles in her back. “But if Tali had been hit…” She blew out a sigh, not wanting to think about it.

“…She’s got more to lose.” Kasumi finished Shepard’s thought.

Silence began to fill the air. Shepard wasn’t sure what else to say, she felt a pang of guilt for not showing Kasumi the same diligence. Kasumi had been nothing but peppy and decent to her: Never one to butt heads or cause problems, despite how “observant” she could be at times. And in return Shepard had dragged her down into a near death sentence, almost losing a leg because of Shepard. Her thoughts turned to the people outside, to Gabby. Good people, true believers when it came to Commander Shepard.

Now they were fighting for lives on a medical slab, or holding back tears while their friends were lying there: All because she couldn’t let go. Before Shepard could ask after her, Kasumi shooed her out. She made it perfectly clear there was no need to fuss over her. Shepard appreciated the gesture, but honestly it felt like plunging back beneath the surface, after a gasping breath of air. Now there was only the desperation to focus on, and it was all around her. She refused to sit still, not while there was something she could do to help: Anything to keep the silence at bay.

Long hours passed aboard the stricken ship. After a bout of feverish work, the drive core had been stabilised again. There were no signs of damage to the element zero mass, and the charge capacitors were all but untouched. The engines had been another story. All four of them had required bypassing of primary circuits, quick and dirty rewiring, and a slew of touch ups to the limiters. Direct antimatter feeding had been taken offline; the magnetic containment fields were already stressed enough. This left the Normandy with only her standard fusion torches, good for cruising at least. Feeling the ship roar to life was the first bit of hope anyone had felt since the attack. It made a break for the nearest planet to the Relay; hugging close to a moon, keeping hidden while more repairs were done.

Things in Engineering had been unnaturally, uncomfortably quiet: The typical banter was absent. Ken had simply handed whatever tools were requested, fighting back sniffles as he did so. He’d barely managed to mention Gabby’s name without breaking down. Garrus had done his best to keep things calm. He was focused and meticulous, putting his technical knowledge to work as he fixed the general problems. He spent his time with Ken, whatever unease they shared utterly gone in light of the circumstances. Of course for Tali, both of them had been little more than radio chatter for the past few hours. She’d had spent every moment since arriving inside the ship’s guts.

Crawling through ventilation and service tunnels, she’d found problems everywhere: Loose cables, busted pipes, and small breaches in the hull. She remembered Shepard’s orders, and scoured every inch of the ship. It was all she could do to keep the paralysing worry out of her head. All around her people were suffering, and despite their allegiance it all struck far too close to home. Rerouting power had been joined by hushed arguments, patching the interior pressure hull had come with muffled crying.

A few times she’d heard Shepard’s voice, working alongside others trying to fix things. Her suit’s audio receivers were top-of-the-line, thanks to her father. She heard the strain in Shepard’s orders, the groans of effort. Trapped inside the Normandy’s walls, she could only do her part. By the time she was finished, the crying and murmurs had died down. The crew had returned to their posts, trying to foster an atmosphere of normality. Tali dragged herself up out of the floor panel, exhausted. She saw Garrus alone in engineering.

“Is Ken…?” Tali’s question got a nod from him.

“He left a little while ago to check on Daniels.” Garrus said grimly, looking over the holographic screen. “Watching a console’s not the hardest thing in the world… I figured he could use a minute.”

“I can take over, if you want?” Tali offered, feeling guilty at her friend’s predicament. Garrus coughed up a chuckle, shaking his plated head.

“Is that really what you wanna do right now?” He asked, lifting a brow-plate. His reptilian eyes found hers through the purple visor, seeing right through her. “Go on, I promise not to break anything.”

“Thanks, Garrus.” She reached up to pat his shoulder before leaving.

“Don’t mention it.”

Tali stopped off on the crew deck, wanting to check on her colleagues first. Ken was still in the med-bay, making himself inconspicuous near the wall. He hovered over Gabby’s bed, eyes red from crying. She followed his gaze down to the sleeping engineer. Doctor Chakwas had already bandaged up the injuries, leaving only a portion of the woman’s face showing. Tali made her way over to Ken, giving the man a hug. She hated feeling useless, not having even the slightest way to help either of them. In the end, all she could offer was her sympathies. She wasn’t sure just how deep that sense of mourning was for humans, but she knew the feeling of losing friends all too well.

The thought danced in head, tugging her mind upstairs. She glanced at the ceiling for a moment, her gloves compensating for the sweat on her palms. Tali looked around the room, the feeling growing at the sheer number of wounded. In a twisted way, the whole thing reminded her of Haestrom. The details were different, but that acrid taste in the air was the same. She spotted Miranda in the mess hall, walking from one patient to the next. Her anger was obvious, but there was no yelling or cursing. It was mystifying, seeing her be so gentle. The salarian, Mordin, was similarly tending people: More doctor than scientist now. It gave Tali some small comfort, to know everyone was in good hands. But soon her mind focused back on where she was going. That sense of unease was growing again, tensing her strides to the door.

“Tali?” Doctor Chakwas caught her at the threshold. She couldn’t refuse the older woman, any more than she could aunty Raan.

“Yes?” Chakwas’ expression was kind as always, but tired.

“How are you feeling, dear?” She asked, looking the environment suit over.

“I’m all right.” Tali did her best to assure her. “Just a few bruises; nothing serious.” Chakwas nodded, her eyes flicking just past the quarian for a moment.

“Could you do me a favour?” Chakwas fiddled in her bag.

“Of course.”

“Shepard’s gel pad needs replacing. The burn won’t heal properly otherwise.” She placed the pad in Tali’s hands. “She’ll need some help applying it. I think she’d prefer your help over mine.”

Tali simply nodded in acknowledgement. Chakwas squeezed her hand gently, closing her fingers over the pad.

“Thank you, dear.”

Tali was back in the elevator. The gel pad was nestled in her right hand, while her left was drumming incessantly against her thigh. The hardened fingertips of her gloves clicked quietly against the fabric, sending a rhythm to match her increasing heart rate. The ride up seemed to take forever, leaving the quarian with only her thoughts for company. Nearly every part of her was sore, the suit doing its best to massage the pain away. Aside from a few small surface tears, suit damage was negligible. Her vitals were strong and none of her monitors were showing any fractures, punctures or long-term issues. When an image of her brain activity popped up on her HUD, she frowned. She didn’t need readouts to know how things were inside her head.

Back down on the planet, she’d felt the beginnings of another attack coming on. After two weeks of waning issues, the sheer desperation she’d heard in Shepard’s voice had been enough to spark up that deep sense of dread. She remembered feeling Shepard shaking as she protected Tali: The sound of Shepard’s warm voice, crying out in pain. For a few seconds she was right back in a nightmare. The battle against the praetorian blurred for her, mostly a mix of explosions and shrieks. Training took over, and she was composed again: Until she saw that… thing, on top of Shepard. The way it glared down at Shepard, with those horrible burning eyes. It brought back memories she’d put aside for months. When she shot at the Collector, desperate to save her friend, she recognised those eyes. Or at least, whatever was behind them.

As the doors opened, she loped out into the small partition: Just a few feet of empty space between her and Shepard’s room. Her fingers clenched against the pad. For a moment, she hesitated. She remembered what had happened after Korlus, the last time she’d pressured Shepard. Shepard had been angry then too, more so than she’d ever seen before. Tali cursed herself for flinching in the Kodiak. It wasn’t like she’d never struck a piece of machinery to blow off steam. After such a horrible day, anyone in Shepard’s position would’ve done the same. Yet instead of an understanding friend, a stupid reflex was all Shepard saw in response. She wasn’t going to leave things like that.

“Shepard?” Tali called in through the intercom. “Shepard, it’s me.” She waited a few seconds, bristling at the lack of response. Shifting from one foot to the other, dark thoughts were beginning to form. Concern gave way to worry. Going against every polite bone in her body, she used the access key Shepard had given her. The door unlocked, and she went inside.

The Captain’s cabin had been a victim of the attack too. Despite the weeks since she’d been there, her memory could still recount the layout of things. At some point most of the room’s objects had been scattered. They’d been put back now, but all of them were just a little out of place. The bed had been remade, hastily. It all carried the same sense of desperation, trying to make things right again. Tali’s shining eyes immediately caught Shepard. She was standing at the far end of the room, beside her bed. She was looking at the wall, her back to Tali. The top half of her under-suit had been discarded, hanging around the waist of her armour.

Shepard slouched; clearly the woman was just as tired as Tali was. The deep purple bruises and cuts contrasted starkly with her pale skin. The sight of them made Tali’s mouth run dry. She was so used to seeing Shepard in armour, just the impervious exterior. Despite her impressive size, Shepard looked smaller now: Frailer, more mortal. Even at a distance, her helmet’s audio could pick up the wheeze in Shepard’s breaths. They were controlled, and painful. She could sense it was an effort for Shepard to merely keep standing. Slowly she approached, as if trying not to disturb her. Soon she was at the tier steps, closer than she’d ever gotten without Shepard’s notice before.

“Shepard?” She asked quietly, looking past her to the bloody dents on the wall. Shepard tensed for a moment, before looking over her shoulder at Tali.

“Tali?” She sounded genuinely surprised. “Sorry, I should’ve buzzed you in.” Shepard turned to face her, Tali’s eyes glancing Shepard’s scarred abdomen before she could stop herself. She could see the recognition on Shepard’s face. She didn’t want to be seen like this.

"Doctor Chakwas told me you’re gel pad needed replacing.” She held up the replacement for Shepard to see.

“I remember, Karin had that mean look in her eye when she put the first one on.” Shepard joked, trying her best to warm the room up. “Was supposed to go back down a while ago, but…” She shook her head. “…Still working up the courage, I guess.”

“She said you wouldn’t heal properly if it wasn’t changed.” Tali made a poor attempt to hide her worry. Her gloved fingers slid along the packaging. Tilting her helmet, she saw Shepard nod in understanding.

“Can’t do it myself.” She said sheepishly. “…Give me a hand?”

“Of course I will.” She reassured Shepard.

Shepard slumped down on the edge of the bed, leaning forward with a grimace. Tali clambered up onto the mattress, trying her best not to rock anything as she positioned herself behind Shepard. Being so close, she could smell the blood. Her olfactory filters processed the metallic scent: Iron, like quarian blood. The familiarity pulled up painful memories. It reminded her of the attack on the Nualli, of Hal. She’d felt that same fear again, just hours ago. But here, in the relative calm of the cabin, there was only the aftermath. Her heart sank as she took in the details of her friend’s marred skin: Every bruise, every welt, the indents left from bullets. Beneath the recent injuries, she could make out the scars Shepard had received on Freedom’s Progress. Peppered along her back, where shrapnel had struck her. The price she’d paid for her bravery. Another time Tali had felt that pang of terror. She unwrapped the packaging as her mind focused on the task at hand.

Quickly tapping her Omni-tool, she activated her suit’s nerve stimulator. The last thing she wanted to do was hurt Shepard through clumsiness. The effect was always strange, having her sense of touch duplicated. Her fingers could feel the fabric of her gloves, but also the porous texture of the pad. It was only an approximation, fed through neural implants at the base of her skull. Mostly its use was for feeling changes in texture or temperature. Knowing how hard to push down, or if something was still too hot for the suit. While highly sensitive, none of if was perfectly accurate. Just a best guess for how the world felt around her. Shaking off the momentary oddness, her fingers gripped the corner of the old pad. With natural precision, she gently pulled it off. Shepard flinched a little, unable to suppress the pain. Tali cursed herself again in frustration.

The sight of the burn was enough to turn her stomach. She’d seen things like it before; the effects of geth weapons were similar. But seeing it done to Shepard… Thankfully, the previous pad had been covering another filling of medi-gel. What she saw now was like a lake inside a crater. Her mind pondered morbidly, just how deep the wound was. Shepard had protected her for nearly seven seconds, while the particle beam burrowed into the commander. How much pain had she endured for Tali, how much permanent damage would she have to live with? The skin was like Gabby’s, melted and discoloured. Tali wanted nothing more than to take it away, to give it all back to Shepard. As gently as she could, she placed the second pad over the wound. Watching it disappear below the white material felt like covering up a crime. Like she was just pretending it never happened. Tali’s hands began to tremble, the lightness in her belly intensifying.

“Tali?” Shepard’s voice snapped her out of it. “You okay?”

“Yeah…” She let out a shaky breath. “Sorry, I just… I’m not hurting you, am I?”

“No.” She shook her head. “It was always gonna hurt, it’s not your fault.”

“Okay.” She forced her voice to steady. “I’ve got to hold it for a while, so the adhesive takes effect.” Shepard gave a nod, taking a breath in before letting it out slowly.

“How’s the Normandy looking?” Shepard asked, looking to fill the silence. Tali was grateful for a technical distraction.

“We’ve restored the engines and drive core to functionality. I’ve sealed the inner pressure hull, and most electrical is working again. There shouldn’t be any serious risks for FTL or Relay transits.”

“You must’ve been at it all day.” Shepard noted, guilt creeping into her voice. “I appreciate it, Tali. Really.”

“The Normandy’s my home too.” She reminded Shepard. “You don’t have to thank me.”

“So, what about the bad news?”

“Our stealth systems are offline. With so many punctures in the outer hull, the ship can’t retain emissions. The heat sink on the main gun failed during the fighting. The firing mechanism is fused together; it will need replacing. Our long-range comms are broken, tight-beam emitters were destroyed. We’ve only got standard radio for now. I’ve had to disable the antimatter feeders; our magnetic bottling fields are struggling with just containment.” She stopped there, before delving into the minutia.

“Is that all?” She joked weakly.

“We might be able to bring a few more systems online.” Tali offered. “But honestly, the ship needs a week in dry-dock, minimum. This kind of damage, it’s just not something we can fix on our own.” She felt disheartened even saying it, like she’d failed Shepard. But Shepard simply nodded.

“I know.” She heard Shepard blow out a snort. “…Fuck.”

She watched Shepard’s head fall forward, shoulders sinking. Running her fingers carefully along the sticky borders of the pad, she focused on making sure the seal was perfect. After everything that had happened today, this would be done right. For a while they both sat there, neither one saying a word. Under her gloves, Tali could feel the heat coming off Shepard’s body. A small part of her wondered how accurate the sensation was. She could feel muscles tense under the skin. Something was bothering Shepard.

“Shepard?” She ventured carefully. “Did something happen?”

Shepard sighed. “I just got off a call with the Illusive Man, down in the QEC. He wants to…” She could hear the shift in Shepard’s voice.

“He wants to pivot direction” Shepard spoke the words with barely contained hatred.

“What does that mean?” She asked, an icy feeling growing inside her.

“He’s re-tasking Cerberus operations to get us through the Omega-Four Relay. He thinks intercepting the Collectors at the colonies…” Shepard went quiet for a moment. “…It’s not viable anymore.”

“What? But you saved the colonists on Horizon!” Tali’s protest only caused Shepard to deflate more.

“We saved two-thirds of them.” Shepard corrected her. “Intel gave me the population numbers. Six hundred and fifty thousand before the attack.” She shook her head, letting out a strained sigh. “They took two hundred thousand people, Tali.”

Shepard hunched forward, as if weighed down by the news. The staggering number took Tali a moment to process. During the mission, she’d shared her team’s feelings of foreboding. The empty streets had reminded her of Freedom’s Progress, the same eerie emptiness. But losing so many people… so many families. Any platitudes she might’ve offered were kept silent. When Shepard looked up again, her attention was focused on the wall. Tali followed her gaze to the bloody dents, her helmet’s sensors detecting the human and turian DNA samples. Both the crimson and cobalt blue colours had dried to black by now. She’d seen it the day she first woke up there. But right now, a chill ran up her spine as she felt its presence being acknowledged.

“Tali?” Shepard called her attention back to herself.

“Yes?”

“There’s… something else we need to talk about.”

By now the pad’s adhesive had fully bonded to the wound. It had been for some time, but Tali felt hesitant to leave it alone. Shepard patted the spot next to her, inviting Tali to sit. Making her way around carefully, she sat down beside Shepard, trying her best to make eye contact. Shepard’s typically bright blue eyes were glazed over with weariness. They were glassy, surrounded by dark rings. The cuts on her face were still healing, ugly marks breaking up her features. She didn’t look right at Tali, her hands nervously clasped together. Tali could feel something bad was coming, worse than the mission. Somewhere in the background, her mind raced at the possibilities, but the droning was kept at bay. Whatever it was, she wanted to know. She watched Shepard taking a bracing breath.

“I… I um…” She tried to find the right words. “I found out something, after we said our goodbyes on Freedom’s Progress. It’s been eating at me for… well, ever since really.”

“…What?” Her question seemed to go unheard. “Shepard, what’s wrong?” Tali’s voice carried a fearful impatience.

“What I told you back then, about Cerberus patching me up. I thought it was the truth.” Her expression tightened.

“But… it’s not. I didn’t just get hurt Tali.” Shepard looked her in the eye as she said it. “I died.”

Tali’s eyes widened, her breath hitching in her throat. What Shepard saying, she knew it was impossible. Every dictator and trillionaire throughout galactic history had spent countless credits trying to circumvent death. None of it had ever worked. Even the closest attempts were little more than botched failures. Her heart was pounding in her chest, taking in the presence of her best friend, living and breathing. Shepard looked perfect, exactly how she remembered her.

But the longer she looked, the more she noticed it. Shepard was missing a scar on her lip, one she’d had since they’d first met. Others were missing too, little nicks she’d picked up during their hunt for Saren. Tali’s perfect memory had captured them all, keeping them close. To her horror, there were new scars. Not just the ones that glowed on Shepard’s face, but others. They weren’t inflicted during battle; she could see the precision in them, done so finely as to be nearly invisible. Her hand trailed unconsciously along her arm.

“How…?” She finally asked, bewildered and teetering on the verge of tears. “How is that possible?”

“Miranda gave me the project files.” Shepard looked down at the scars on her arm, mouth twisting into a scowl.

“I saw my own body… blackened… and frozen… Jesus, I don’t think I’ll ever un-see it.” Her eyes narrowed, tears beading. “I don’t even know how much of me is…”

“Keelah…” Tali could hear the pain in Shepard’s voice, the fear.

“It’s getting worse.” Shepard said quietly, like a confession. “Sometimes it’s like… everything’s normal. I don’t even think about it. But then… It’s like I’m in the passenger seat, and I can’t stop it.” She looked at Tali. “You remember that sparring session, between me and Garrus?”

She nodded silently.

“He told me about what happened to him, before I got to Omega. It was eating him too, and I wanted to help… but then he made some comment about me, and I just lost it. I felt guilty about lying to you… so I took it out on him.”

Shepard’s voice shook, taking a sharp breath in. She tried to blink the tears away.

“Garrus nearly died because of me. Gabby’s lying in a hospital bed, because of me. This whole mission is hanging by a thread: Because I came out… wrong. Because I’m not really…”

Those last words were choked out as her hands covered her face. Shepard’s sobs were hushed, kept as quiet as possible. Tali felt an indescribable sensation in her chest. Her mind had slowly put the pieces together, observing the surgery scars. She could hear it in Shepard’s voice; this was the truth: The awful, horrifying truth. Part of her wanted to recoil in disgust, to strangle Miranda for what Cerberus had done. They had defiled a hero’s memory, forced someone back to life just to do their bidding. They’d covered it up in a lie, one they even kept from the woman sitting beside her. And worst of all, in doing so they’d left Shepard with this turmoil. It was monstrous… beyond monstrous.

But when she looked at her, she only saw Shepard there. The woman who’d saved her life on the Citadel, who’d been kind to her in a galaxy full of prejudice. She could remember every moment they’d spent together, every long conversation, every silly joke. Most of all, she remembered that infectious warmth Shepard had, something able to pull her even out of the depths of Haestrom. Shepard had come for her, in her hour of need. She’d sat here with Tali, and helped her through the pain and the nightmares. Somehow… despite the sheer impossibility of it all… she really had come back. Tali reached out, pulling Shepard’s hands away. Looking her in the eye, she held them between hers, the heat radiating through her gloves.

“I’m… so sorry, Jane.” She said gently. “I can’t even imagine what you’re going through.” She squeezed them gently, looking down at them intertwined.

“But you are still you, never doubt that.” She saw Shepard’s eyes fall, felt the commander’s hand try to pull away. But she held her there.

“I don’t… I want to believe you, but…” Shepard started.

“Then believe me.” Tali said, resolute. “I saw you risk your life to protect Veetor, you jumped into an airstrike for him, you crazy bosh’tet.” She saw a break in Shepard’s agony, the ghost of a smile. “I know we didn’t save them all, but those people on Horizon owe you their lives, Shepard. Nobody else could’ve saved them.”

She touched Shepard’s cheek, leaning in close.

“I thought I was going to die on Haestrom.” She admitted, her voice low. “I thought I was never going to see you again.” A tear slid invisibly down her cheek. “Then there you were.” Her voice broke as she said it. “Even after I, left you alone… you came for me.”

She pulled her close, pressing her mask against Shepard’s forehead.

“You were carrying… all of this. And you still made the time to help me.” She held Shepard’s eyes in hers, not even an inch apart. “That’s how I know it’s you.”

Tali saw something come to life behind Shepard’s blue eyes. She felt Shepard’s arms wrap around her. The commander’s head slipped to her shoulder. Her suit’s nerve stimulators did their best to convey the sheer warmth of Shepard’s embrace, like a blazing fire. She wanted to return it, dearly, but she kept her hands in her lap. The pain that even a little contact had caused Shepard was obvious. Instead, Tali let Shepard cling to her, let her pour that need into someone else. In that intimate, quiet moment, she felt strange new tears roll down her face. She wasn’t even sure why she was crying. It was like a great weight had been lifted off her chest. She could finally breathe again. By the time Shepard’s embrace began to soften and fall away, her body ached for that warmth. Shepard bashfully dried her eyes, trying to put on some semblance of the Commander everyone knew.

“I’m… going to need some time.” She said, still a little uncertain. “But thank you. I don’t… I couldn’t do this without you, Tali.”

“Probably not.” She agreed, giving Shepard a smile. “But I won’t tell anyone.” Her levity got a sniffling chuckle out of Shepard. She relished the sound.

“Y’know, it’s funny.” Shepard ran an arm across her face a final time. “Garrus said he knew it was still me too. Though he wasn’t quite as… eloquent about it.”

“Well now I have to know.” Tali said impatiently, making a show of crossing her arms.

“He said, ‘because you’re still a terrible shot, and ugly to boot!’” Shepard giggled at the memory, shaking her head. But Tali didn’t laugh. She just watched Shepard smile, taking in every part of it. She felt her fingers touch, unconsciously.

“You’re not ugly.” The words left her mouth before Tali realised she’d said them. Shepard’s giggling faded, but the smile remained.

“Thanks.” She said warmly. “I’ll take that as the deciding vote.”

Shepard got up from the bed, offering Tali a hand. She felt Shepard’s strength returning as she was pulled up. After such a long day, both of them were a little unsteady on their feet. She saw Shepard stumble back, the gel pad dangerously close to hitting the wall. Pulling back, she kept her from going any further, bringing the two of them nearly chest-to-chest. Tali’s eyes drifted upward, meeting Shepard’s for just a second. Without a quip or a turn of phrase, it felt like an eternity. Seeing the bruises down Shepard’s arm, she quickly released her.

“Speaking of Garrus, I should get back down to engineering.” Tali nearly brought all the words up at once. “I kind of left him down there, covering for me.”

“Alright.” Shepard gave her a nod. “I’m probably gonna pass out for a couple hours. Don’t work yourself to death down there, okay?”

She returned the nod, padding over toward the door. Despite hours of exertion, she felt lighter on her feet than she had all day. Passing by the gentle blue light of the empty fish tank, she headed through the door to the waiting elevator. When she turned around to select the fourth deck, she saw Shepard still standing there. As the doors were closing, she gave her a wave goodbye. Shepard’s tired arm carried it back to her just as they sealed shut. Feeling a wave of fatigue, she leant back against the wall.

Suddenly cold shot up her back, a reminder that her nerve stimulator was still active. Lighting up her Omni-tool to shut out the sensations, Tali paused. Instead, her hand slipped through the orange holographic gauntlet, making contact with her suit. The feeling of being touched flowed into her mind. Slowly her other hand followed, and she wrapped herself in a tight self-hug. She closed her eyes, letting her mind run free. There in the privacy of that little cubicle, she let something she’d buried for two years, come back to life.
