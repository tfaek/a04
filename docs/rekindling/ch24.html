Chapter 24: Locusts

“Tali! How long until those guns are online!”

Shepard yelled the question into her helmet. Gripping her rifle tightly, she fired another burst at a group of Collectors advancing on her flank. The insect-like aliens had stronger barriers than she’d ever seen. Again her bullets simply deflected off, kicking up dirt at their feet. Frantically she searched her pouches for another grenade. After several empty seconds, she threw the last one she had. The disk-shaped explosive skidded into the middle of them, a puff of dirt obscuring the enemy. When the warm wind blew the dust away, the three Collectors remained. Forcing down her frustration, Shepard took in a sharp breath. She focused her eyes on them, tensing her body before sweeping her arm upward. A line of biotic shockwaves surged towards them, knocking debris from the settlement aside. The last wave struck the centre of the group, ripping two Collectors apart and flinging the third into a wall. It bought them some time, if only a minute.

The whole mission had been a disaster. Despite Mordin’s accomplishment with the seeker swarm countermeasure, it could only be calibrated for organic heat signatures. With the sheer complexity and unfamiliar design of Collector technology, there simply hadn’t been time to develop something wider-range. This meant that Shepard and her squad had been forced to go in on foot. Even though she didn’t like it, the whole team would be needed for a mission like this.

After getting lucky with a quiet landing, things had quickly gotten out of hand. Collectors were swarming all over the main settlement. With a jamming field cutting off communication to the Normandy, there was no chance of fire support. The best they could do was pick their battles, and try to keep from getting overwhelmed. Fighting the bugs for the first time had been a wakeup call. Despite their strange appearance, the Collectors were formidable opponents. Armed with exotic and deadly weapons, along with hordes of husks and lumbering scions. There was a small army of them. Bringing down just a single platoon had been a challenge.

It had ultimately been a waste of time, with the victims in stasis requiring more medical attention than Mordin could provide in the field. So instead the commander made the hard decision: to keep the fighting to a minimum. Slinking around back-alleys and through abandoned buildings while innocent people wailed and cried out for help. She knew it bothered the others, and she felt sick hearing it. She dreaded the possibility that they’d come too late, that those people were the last that could be saved. That she’d sacrificed them to reach her main objective.

Even now, all those precautions seemed in vain. No sooner had her squad secured the anti-air control tower than dozens of Collectors had flown in, attacking in force. Kasumi was already down, with Mordin doing his best to treat her wounds. Tali worked as quickly and diligently as possible, but even the humble minute of complex coding it took was a minute too long. All around them the enemy was closing in.

“Tali!”

“I-I’m done, they’re targeting the ship now!” The quarian’s voice shook from adrenaline.

Above them, the Alliance anti-air guns creaked and groaned on their gimbals. The mass-accelerators were soon pointed directly at the Collector ship. The enormous, monolithic structure barely seemed to notice as the thunderous shots sent hypersonic rounds speeding toward it. Soon all four were firing, the deafening chorus reducing the squad to helmet radio for any hope of hearing each other. Shepard slapped her friend’s shoulder to congratulate her, when suddenly a beam of golden light struck the panel near Tali’s hand. Without hesitating, she wrapped her body around Tali.

The Collector weapon passed straight through her shields, burning into the ceramic plates of her body armour. After seeing one of those things cut open Kasumi’s leg, she wouldn’t take the chance again. Tali’s reaction was one of surprise, held completely still beneath Shepard. Shepard grit her teeth, a hiss of pain escaping as the searing light burned through her under-suit. She heard the familiar shots of Garrus’ rifle behind her, and the beam faltered in position. But the heavy barriers of the enemy delayed their deaths a few crucial seconds. By the time the particle rifle fell silent, the commander’s whole body shook with pain. It had bitten deep into her flesh; the horrid smell of roasting meat invaded her nostrils. She released her friend with a moan, falling back.

“Shepard!” Tali called out, her voice breaking with worry. Shepard felt Tali’s hands on her, trying to help her up.

“Go! Get to cover, now!” With effort she pointed to the nearby barricade. With a flinch, Tali obeyed her.

Shepard’s guts twisted as she watched Tali scramble to the wall. Bullets whizzed past, some plinking against her barrier in places. Tali’s long legs carried her there in seconds, just before another particle beam lanced her way. She was safe, inside Miranda’s powerful biotic field. Jacob and Zaeed were with her, returning fire. All around them the bullets and beams of Collector weaponry failed to penetrate. Despite the circumstances, the commander was once again grateful for the curt woman. She owed her a drink after this. The concern for others was short lived when a strong hand wrenched her up from the floor, pulling her into cover. Another shout of pain escaped her, her eyes glancing over to the turian.

“Thanks.” Shepard managed, unsure whether to hug the bastard or punch him.

“No time for napping.” Garrus retorted, handing her her avenger. “Here, you dropped this.”

“How’s it-” The woman grit her teeth as cold metal pressed against her wound. “How’s it looking out there?”

“Ugly, but they’re pulling back. Looks like those guns are doing their job. If you can keep upright, I’ll let you finish them off with me.” Garrus’ offer got a pained laugh out of her. She grabbed the turian’s shoulder.

“Let’s get to it.”

Breaking cover, the pair of soldiers lined up their targets. Catching the afterglow of one of Miranda’s warp fields, Shepard fired her assault rifle into a cluster of the bugs, her bullets ripping through their chitinous shells. Without their powerful barriers, the Collectors were dead in seconds. She heard the report of her friend’s sniper rifle, taking the head clean off another one of them. Catching a scion waddling out from behind one of the buildings, Shepard took another sharp breath before charging straight into it. Even though the towering amalgam of husks weighed half a ton, her impact sent it keeling over.

This close the commander didn’t even bother with guns. Drawing her combat knife from its scabbard, she rammed the blade into the creature’s grey flesh. She ran the razor edge along the length of its bloated body sack, spilling a mess of gore. Eventually the gurgling screams abated and the scion stopped twitching. With her right arm covered in sickly blue fluid, Shepard levelled her rifle. Exhausting an entire thermal clip into one of the Collector assassins, she brought down its barrier. With a whip of her arm, she pulled it into the air. The beam from its particle rifle tore a glowing scar into the dirt before a concussive shot from Garrus blew the thing into bloody chunks.

Husks charged forward, moving like a grey river toward the squad. Another line of shockwaves flew from Shepard’s arm, sending the creatures flying. She carved swathes into their advancing line, while Tali tore apart any that got too close to pieces with her shotgun. The squad were holding the line, if just barely. Mordin stayed crouched within Miranda’s barrier, doing his best to patch up Kasumi’s leg. Jacob’s biotics were weakening, and thermal clips were dwindling. Then Shepard saw a dark cloud rushing toward them from the Collector ship. Seeker swarms.

“Normandy, you receiving?” Shepard called out over the radio. She prayed for a response.

“Joker here. Whatever you guys did down there I’m getting your signal again.”

“I’ve got seeker swarms inbound from the enemy ship. I need an incendiary strike on them!”

“Roger, hold tight!”

Thankful for her luck, Shepard looked up to catch the Normandy, wreathed in plasma. In seconds the glowing aura died off, and several missiles came screaming toward the swarm. By now the swirling mass of insects were directly overhead, bearing down on them. The sky grew dark, and the droning of countless wings joined the din of cannon fire and battle. She braced herself, watching the smoke trails streaking those last few moments into the target. Then all at once, several white clouds dominated the sky above. At first it looked like snow, gently floating there. A few of the seekers flew down through it, covered in the sticky film.

Then came the ignition. A roar filled the air, drowning out all other sounds. The odd shade was replaced by a blazing orange firestorm. From one side of the sky to the other, there was nothing but flame. The wind rushed up from below, yearning to fill the void. Grass whipped back and forth and the combatants were thrown off balance. Taking the initiative, Shepard picked up the nearly empty grenade launcher resting near Kasumi. With a look to Miranda, the two of them engaged. Dropping the barrier, Miranda fired multiple warp fields into the enemy numbers. Barriers failed under the inferno, and the commander fired the last few grenades. Four shots, four explosions, with nearly a dozen Collectors reduced to blood and carapace. After several seconds saturated in orange light, the fire subsided. The sky was black with smog, and thousands of tiny shapes began to fall. Raining down all around them were the charred remains of the swarm, pattering on the ground before crumbling to ash.

“Good effect on target!” Shepard sang her pilot’s praises.

“Roger that commander, we’ll circle around and… shit! I’ve got multiple fighters launching from the Collector ship!”

Shepard turned to see them, strange spherical craft darting up from inside the rocky bow. In seconds they were flying toward the Normandy. Chasing her as she broke hard for space. Even as the squadron disappeared into the sky, Shepard saw red beams of light flashing toward her ship. With the pressure taken off, Miranda’s barrier fell permanently. She and the others went on the offensive. What remained of the Collectors seemed to be retreating. Something wasn’t right. While Shepard’s squad had made quick work of the husks and scions, they’d barely cut down a third of the bugs’ numbers. It was only when Shepard focused her attention past the chaos of the battle; that she realised why. There were pods on the far side of the battlefield, dozens of them. The furthest were already floating away, escorted by Collectors. Despite her exhaustion from the fighting, the commander’s blood boiled. She wouldn’t let these monsters slink off now. With the enemy falling back, she surged forward, hot on their heels. She raised her rifle, bringing down one of the Collectors as it took to the wing. The enemy fell, but the pod flew on. She pushed her legs harder; she had to stop this!

“Enough.”

Before she knew what was happening, Shepard crashed to the ground. She felt sick to her stomach, and the air was ripped from her lungs. She could feel it, raking its claws along her skull. The sound came howling up from inside her, a deep metallic growl. Her vision began to blur and streak, catching glimpses of her team as they rushed to help her. She sucked in breaths through her helmet filters, trying to get a lungful.

“I am assuming direct control.”

It hit her again like a blast wave, trying to pull the woman back to the ground. Pushing through the tar-thick air, Shepard was on her feet again. Looking toward the remaining Collectors, she saw one of them twisting and contorting. From a distance, it seemed as if the creature was about to fall over dead, when suddenly its body was ablaze. The gaps between the exoskeleton began to glow: First the red of flesh, then bright blazing gold. As the ambience of the world returned, so did the garbled screeching of the burning Collector. Soon her teammates had arrived to her side, all of them tense from the disturbing scene.

They watched on in dumbstruck awe, all except one. Tali’s eyes were fixed on the thing, but her shining gaze bore a look of recognition. Finally the spasms stopped, and the burning Collector settled on its feet again. The typical jerky movements of its brothers were now completely absent. Instead, this new horror stalked confidently towards them. Shepard felt her composure return, switching to her shotgun. Bolstered by her act, the others readied their weapons. Then something new appeared. An enormous chimera of flesh and metal came down from the sky. Standing on four spider-like legs, its dark grey carapace was little more than a set of jaws filled with human heads. With a shriek it settled overhead, floating above the burning Collector.

“Praetorian. Bring me their bodies.” The voice commanded.

A pair of particle beams shot from the praetorian’s eyes, carving into the soil as they homed in on their target. With a biotic push, Shepard sent Tali flying off her feet. The engineer crashed down behind a nearby crate, out of the monster’s line of sight. Before it could acquire another target, Shepard sprinted forward to engage. Firing blast after blast form her shotgun, the incendiary pellets burned into its armour. Letting out another piercing cry, it knocked her aside with a kick. But the commander didn’t relent. She was back up, charging into the praetorian at biotic speed. The impact threw it off balance, just in time for Miranda and Jacob to attack. More blue beams fizzed through the air, striking against the dark-haired woman’s hasty barrier bubble. Taking advantage of protected allies, Zaeed tossed a bundle of grenades. The explosions completely obscured the creature; only monstrous screams were heard. Content that at least one threat had been contained; the commander turned her attention to the other new foe.

At first the burning Collector simply watched, as if it was studying them. It seemed unconcerned, almost bored by the whole ordeal. Racking her shotgun, Shepard put the bastard in her crosshairs. A breath, then a flex and she charged. The world around her stretched on forever, her mass effect field bending her form into a biotic missile. Crossing the distance in the blink of an eye, the commander collided right into this new threat. But this time, the enemy did not falter. She fell back, as though she’d run up against a brick wall. Down for less than a second, Shepard rolled to her feet, weapon in hand. The burning Collector turned to her, unafraid.

“Shepard.” The voice spoke her name with disgust.

She felt the monster’s notice tunnel through her. Pulling the trigger she sent four fiery shots into it, burning pellets clashing against the enemy’s barrier. The Collector raised its hand, unleashing a powerful warp field. It struck her before she could move, racking the woman’s body with agonising pain. She could feel it eating away at her, sapping her strength. Before she could ready a counter, another biotic attack struck her. Lifting the commander off her feet, the blast sent her crashing into a cargo crate, denting the metal. Shepard’s chest heaved; she could taste blood in the back of her mouth. Heavy footsteps stalked toward her, and she felt a clawed hand around her neck. With incredible strength, the Collector lifted her into the air.

“Weak. Slow. You have failed, human.”

As if on cue, a blinding flash erupted in the distance. Struggling against the Collector’s grasp, Shepard turned her head to see it. The towering behemoth of stone and metal slowly lifted off the ground, climbing on a massive pillar of fire. White-hot fury filled the woman’s mind; she couldn’t let these things get away with the colonists! She felt the hand tighten around her neck, focusing the pain and panic into her biotics. Soon her body crackled and flickered with blue light. Her fists shot forward, punching the burning Collector as hard as she could. The blows shook her enemy, and its grip faltered. But those eyes… those golden flames never wavered. She could feel them peering inside her, looking beyond her armour and flesh.

Gritting her teeth, she kicked and punched and kneed her attacker, watching its barrier flicker and fail. The Collector slammed her back against the crate, leaving the woman dazed. But before it could attack again, a volley of rifle fire ripped up its right side. Shocked, Shepard glanced back toward the squad; all of them were still battling the praetorian. Another human, a marine was running towards her. Dressed in spec-ops black, he unleashed a biotic push, knocking the Collector back. It turned to face him, enraged at the interruption. With a moment to breathe, Shepard tried the radio. After a few seconds of static, the signal came in clear.

“Normandy!” She croaked, trying to catch her breath. “The Collector ship is airborne, I need you to intercept and disable it!”

“Shepard I’m barely keeping up with these little bastards. If I pull a pursuit trajectory, the Normandy’s a sitting duck!” The pilot’s concern was pushed aside.

“That’s a direct order Jeff! Bring down that ship!”

Joker’s reply was drowned out by a shriek from the praetorian. Tali had emerged from cover, hitting the monster with an energy drain. She scrambled away, barely avoiding its stomping foot. Single-minded desire overcame the commander, and she ran to help Tali. But before she could get halfway there, the sound of wing-beats filled her ears. The burning Collector came down on top of her, pinning the woman to the ground. Claws slashed at her armour, leaving gouges in the ceramic of her gauntlets. The enemy’s weight crushed down on her chest, searing pain flooding her mind. She struck back with all her might, desperately fighting off the dizziness. Sharp claws sliced against her helmet. She felt them pierce into her breather mask, raking along her face. With a wheezing gasp, she struck back with a biotic punch, freeing herself. Shepard’s ribs ached, stifling her actions as she stumbled forward. She felt a human hand grip hers, pulling her up.

“You okay?”

The Alliance soldier’s brown eyes caught hers. There was an instant of pure realisation. Shepard recognised that voice anywhere, and she felt Kaidan’s grip slacken with shock. But there was barely any time to process before the Collector attacked again. Circling above them, the winged alien threw down powerful energy blasts. Together the two soldiers returned fire, alternating their own biotic strikes. Without serious injury, the lieutenant moved faster, landing his shots and trying to distract it. With barely any support weapons left, all Shepard could do cycle clips and keep firing. Finally growing tired with the battle, the burning Collector dove straight for Kaidan.

Despite the lieutenant’s prowess, the commander realised this monster meant to kill him. Banishing all distractions, Shepard drew in an unsteady, shuddering breath. She waited, watching the thing descend. Kaidan’s bullets chipped away the last of its barrier just milliseconds from impact. She focused and flexed her body. One final biotic charge impacted the Collector side-on, sending both of them tumbling across the battlefield. Shepard pushed away, hurrying to get to her feet as the burning creature slashed at her; long claws ripping her chest-plate clean off. Another blast struck her unprotected chest above her breasts, knocking her down again. Before she knew it, the Collector was on her.

“Pitiful.”

Shepard felt the Collector’s claws pierce into her gut. The pain was overwhelming, her vision flickered and her ears rang. She let out a harrowed cry, the sheer agony drawing it out of her. She tried to fight back, but her arms felt like lead. Another clawed hand secured around her neck, slowly suffocating her. The vice squeezed tighter and tighter, soon all she could hear was her own heartbeat, struggling through her carotid artery. Something moved in her peripheral vision. A shape was approaching, running toward her. She wanted to call out, to warn them. Whatever this thing was, it was deadlier than she could’ve imagined. The dim shape focused into a woman, covered head to toe in purple silks. Ice surged through her veins, wrenching her back to awareness.

Tali fired a blast of pellets into the burning Collector, drawing its attention. Shepard felt the claws release her. Blood poured from her wound, but the pain had vanished. Instead she just saw Tali, and the whole world slowed as those claws drew closer to her suit. Shepard’s biotics flared, and with a screaming effort she lunged forward. Gripping the Collector’s spiny arm she wrenched it to the ground, tearing the limb from its socket. She rolled on top of it, staring down into those burning eyes. With blood gushing from her belly, she returned the beating. Shepard’s fists struck with an audible crunch, each blow disfiguring the Collector’s head.

“This changes nothing.” The voice jeered.

She ignored it, bringing her fists down again and again. Hard shell cracked and buckled under the onslaught, rage and pain driving her blows deeper and deeper. Blood began to trickle over her eyes, beneath the helmet. She ignored it, punching blindly. Finally her fists found the burning hot flesh beneath, and then solid ground. Pulling her helmet free, Shepard looked down to see the Collector’s head reduced to a smear of brown blood and gore. Then it was over. She struggled to breathe, slumping forward.

Her stomach burned, blood oozing from the puncture wounds. Shepard watched as the alien’s body laid still, those glowing golden eyes snuffed out. Heat and colour left the corpse, and it blew apart like leaves in the autumn wind. But the words remained, mocking her. They burrowed deep inside, taking root with an awful certainty. On her hands and knees, Shepard’s body shook from stress and exhaustion. Just a few seconds more, and she thought she was going to pass out. But the feeling of slender, gloved hands on her brought the woman back to awareness.

Tali’s lilting voice filled her ears. Through the daze, she could hear Tali calling her name. Gently Tali lifted her up onto her haunches, holding her steady. Shepard saw her shining eyes widen in horror, a gasp escaping her helmet. Tali’s hands trembled as she held her, calling out to the others for help. Shepard tried to straighten up, anything to relieve the fear in her eyes. Tali stared down at Shepard’s wounds, transfixed. Neither of them heard the sound of footsteps approaching.

“Move!” Shepard bristled with anger as Miranda shoved Shepard’s friend aside, her hands clasping instruments from Mordin’s doctor’s bag. Her icy-blue eyes met Shepard’s for a moment, conveying both relief and anger. Shepard felt the hard metal nozzle of a sprayer touch her flesh, before a torrent of freezing cold poured into her guts. The commander cursed herself, yelping in pain as the medi-gel foamed and filled the gaps left by Collector claws. She saw Tali standing there, her hands wringing as she looked on. She was shifting on her toes, unable to look away. Suddenly movement caught Shepard’s eye, looking past Miranda’s dark head of hair.

“Get away from her!” Kaidan’s voice froze the three of them. Dressed in her Cerberus armour, Miranda raised her hands.

“She needs another dose, if I don’t-”

“Now!” A shot rang out, landing at her feet. Letting out a frustrated snort, she stood up and moved away. Kaidan stepped closer, taking in the sight of his lost friend with awe and trepidation.

“H-how…” He stuttered, collecting himself. “…You’re alive?”

“Not for much longer if I don’t seal that wound.” Miranda interjected.

“Shut up!” Kaidan pointed his rifle at her head. “I’m not letting you touch her!”

“Please, Kaidan.” Tali stepped toward him. “She needs help.”

“Tali?” Shaking off the surprise, his voice softened. “All right. But you do it.”

The quarian nodded, clearly nervous. Walking over to Miranda, she gingerly took the medi-gel sprayer from her. The Cerberus agent remained still, imparting a quick direction on how to apply it. Kaidan’s eyes darted between Shepard and Miranda, his finger resting just above the trigger of his gun. Looking down at Shepard’s wound, Tali’s breath shortened again. Shining eyes closed and she calmed herself. Her hands stilled, returning to their effortless natural precision. Carefully she inserted the instrument into the oozing gouge, her finger resting on the button.

“Sorry.” She said quietly to Shepard, locking eyes with her as she pressed it. Another burst of freezing foam made the commander grit her teeth, forcing her way through the pain as she kept Tali’s gaze. After a few seconds the material billowed out of the wound, and began sealing her up. Shepard gave her friend a nod of thanks, getting a sigh of relief in return. Tali helped her to her feet, getting under Shepard’s shoulder to keep her upright. Finally Shepard and Kaidan stood eye-to-eye.

“Kaidan…” Shepard managed through strained breaths. “You can put the gun down.” She gestured to Miranda.

“What?” The old lieutenant balked. “But she’s… what the hell is going on here, Shepard?”

Kaidan only grew more agitated as the other members of the squad approached, especially at the sight of another Cerberus uniform. Uncertainty was clear on his face as he scanned the team, before catching the sight of a familiar turian. Garrus came to Shepard’s side, taking the strain off Tali. He gave the lieutenant a nod of acknowledgment, but kept his reptilian eyes on the man’s rifle. Realising he was outnumbered, Kaidan finally lowered the weapon, an annoyed Miranda moving back toward her squad-mates. Raising her voice, the commander ordered them to check the pods that were left behind. The blatant request for privacy was quickly heeded, and the team dispersed. Leaving the four friends alone.

“It’s been a while, Alenko.” Shepard started, standing on her own.

“Is that all you have to say?” Kaidan’s expression shredded the woman’s attempt at pleasantries. “You let me think you were dead, for two years. I spent months trying to get past what happened! Not a day went by I didn’t ask myself what I could’ve done to change it.”

He stepped closer to Shepard, face flushed with emotion.

“Do you have any idea what it’s like? What it did to us? I watched Liara fall apart at your funeral. I haven’t heard from her since: Tali, Garrus? They nearly got themselves killed trying to rescue you!”

“Yeah… I’ve some idea.” Shepard’s answer was tinged with bitterness. Garrus and Tali shifted uncomfortably beside her. “I’m sorry Kaidan. I never wanted to put you through that. Any of you.”

The old lieutenant’s expression softened, anger and resentment giving way to his typical calm demeanour. Shepard watched as he forced back the bile in his throat, clearly unsatisfied with the answer. The commander felt a pang of guilt, but it couldn’t travel far past the current circumstances. Her mind was still focused on the Collectors, and the eerie sensation of not exactly “winning” this battle. Still, it was good to see her old friend again. Shepard held on to that, for what it was worth.

“…Alright.” Kaidan nodded, blowing out a sigh. “Tell me what happened."

“After the attack on the Normandy, I was in bad shape. Beyond saving. I spent the last two years in a coma, only woke up a few weeks ago.”

“Woke up? Where, why didn’t I hear about it?”

“Because… it was in a Cerberus facility.” Shepard’s answer sent the man’s eyes wide.

“What?” Kaidan struggled to comprehend it. “But… why? Why would Cerberus save your life, how could they save your life?”

“They’re a lot better organised, and funded, than you know.” Shepard shook her head. “As for why, that’s the reason I’m here. These things that attacked Horizon, they’re Collectors.”

“I know. Intel checks out, never seen one in person before today though.”

“They’ve been responsible for all the recent abductions, including Freedom’s Progress and Ferris Fields. Cerberus wants to put a stop to it, and they wanted me to head the mission.”

The realisation crystallised on Kaidan’s face. The air between them shifted, and whatever shared warmth was there completely evaporated. He unconsciously stepped back, glancing between the three of them.

“No…” He said to himself, still reeling. “…You can’t be serious.”

“I don’t like it either, but right now I’m out of options.”

“Out of options?” Kaidan repeated, raising his voice. “This is Cerberus we’re talking about!” He looked to Garrus and Tali. “Is this… are you with them too?”

“Shepard’s right.” Garrus spoke up. “Whatever the Collectors are up to, it’s big. We need to stop whatever they’re planning, without getting bogged down in politics again.”

“So you threw your lot in with terrorists, instead?” Kaidan retorted, eyeing the turian. “Did you just forget what we saw together? The labs filled with husks, Alliance transports sabotaged with rachni eggs?”

“We haven’t forgotten, Kaidan.” Tali’s voice drew his attention. “Shepard doesn’t trust Cerberus, none of us do. That’s why we’re here, to keep an eye on them while we deal with the Collectors.”

“What if that’s what they want?” Kaidan suggested. “Shepard and her old team, allied with Cerberus to protect the Terminus clonies. What if they’re just using you for propaganda? To sway more people to their cause?”

“So what if they are?” Shepard cut in, annoyed. “I don’t have the luxury of choosing bedfellows. I needed a ship and crew, and they provided. If it weren’t for Cerberus, I never would’ve known Horizon was the next target.”

“And you don’t find that suspicious? Cerberus just so happens to know who’s getting attacked, so you can swoop in and save them?”

“What are you getting at, Kaidan?”

“I’m getting at Akuze. They led your squad into a thresher nest just to study them!”

“I don’t need a fucking history lesson Kaidan, I was there!” Shepard’s hackles were up.

“You don’t see the similarities? You said so yourself, Cerberus tipped you off about Horizon.” Kaidan’s lips pursed. “What if they set this all up?”

The idea sent something cold crawling up the commander’s spine. Her mind flared at the possibility, only adding to her anger. After everything they’d gone through trying to save these people… for it to have been a setup…

“Why?” Shepard asked. “Why would they lead both us and the Collectors to this colony?”

“Maybe to study them up close.” Kaidan suggested. “Gathering data on their technology or weapons? Maybe to boost their image, saving colonists from an alien threat?”

He went quiet for a moment, weighing up his next words. Shepard could see it gnawing at him.

“Or maybe… they set it up to manipulate you, to convince you to work with them?”

“…Manipulate me?” Shepard chewed on the words, her tone sharpening. “Is that what you think is happening here?”

“I’m just saying it’s-”

“Look around!” Shepard cut in. “We just lost thousands of people! The threat is real, Kaidan!”

“I’m not saying the threat isn’t real. I just don’t trust that Cerberus is-”

“Is what? Actually focused on the enemy? Because as far as I can tell, we just saw them get away with half a colony! What the hell is the Alliance doing about this?”

“It’s not that simple Shepard, you know that.”

Kaidan’s answer incensed the commander. After what she’d learned on the Citadel, what Garrus had told her? Hearing this kind of platitude, especially from on of her old crew, it was the last straw. After everything they’d been through together, after stealing the Normandy and risking a court marshal, she wouldn’t be fed this bullshit again. Not from him.

“I’ll tell you what I know. In the two years I’ve been gone, six entire colonies have been abducted. Six! How many people do you think that is? But instead of bolstering our patrol fleets, the Alliance spent it’s time guarding the Citadel. The most well defended place in the galaxy. They sat on their god damned hands and watched as those colonies were picked off!”

Kaidan remained silent, his face tightening.

“You wanna know what they did have time for?” Shepard glared at him. “Tearing apart my reputation, smearing me as delusional! Neutering Anderson’s position on the council, pulling their support from an investigation into the Reapers!”

Shepard could see the anger building in her old friend, but at this point she didn’t care.

“They’ve got all the time in the world to send our warships around council space as fucking parade floats! But between the lies, the politics and screwing over the people who saved them from Sovereign, they just can’t find a minute to actually help.” Shepard spat out the last sentence. “Not that simple… what a joke.”

“Is that what this is about, Shepard?” Kaidan asked through clenched teeth. “Is this personal?”

“You’re damn right it’s fucking personal!” The commander roared. “The Alliance turned its back on these people! At least Cerberus is doing something!”

Shepard’s outburst ended in dead air. Rage and indignation clung tightly to her, staring down her old lieutenant: The look of dismissal on his face, like he was talking to a child. It made the commander angrier than she’d ever felt.

“God… do you hear yourself?” Kaidan asked. “You’re willing to work with Cerberus, to overlook all the monstrous things they’ve done, just to get back at the Alliance?”

“And what the hell have you done?” Shepard pointed a finger at him. “All your expertise, and you’re out here doing public relations! Setting up defences that don’t work! Is that the problem Alenko, am I not playing poster girl for the right team?”

Shepard stepped forward, suppressing a wince beneath her scowl.

“So self-righteous… You wanna judge me, go ahead. At least I haven’t forgotten who the real enemy is.”

“No…” Kaidan agreed; fists clenched. “…You’ve just forgotten everything else.”

He shook his head, looking away. “What happened to you? What happened to the woman I followed into hell, the woman who brought down Saren without stooping to his level?”

Shepard bared her teeth, barely containing her rage at the accusation. She wanted to bite the man’s head off. But instead she kept her voice low; leaning in close so there was nowhere to escape.

“She’s dead.”

They were just two words, but they chilled the air. Something in the way Shepard spoke them, in the tone of her voice and the slightest twitch at the corner of her mouth. All of it left Kaidan completely silent. The commander kept her blue eyes fixed on him. She watched for that flicker of vulnerability again, something to tell her the message had gotten through. But there was nothing there now. Instead Kaidan simply looked resigned… tired. Something deep inside the commander stirred and twisted, trying desperately to force an apology out of her. It saw the window closing, looked on helplessly as time ran out. Whether it was better judgement, or some feeling of obligation, or even just the embers of her old life, it wasn’t enough. She buried it under her rage, letting the fire burn inside her. She held her stone-like expression, letting nothing past. Kaidan stood there sheepishly, perhaps fighting back his own feelings. But right now, there just wasn’t any more to say. He turned and left, without another word.

Shepard put her finger to her earpiece. She called for the shuttle, barely hearing the words she spoke. Her mind wanted to be somewhere else; it wanted to go after her friend. But she kept her thoughts in the moment, blocking it out. What was done was done. Garrus and Tali stayed by her side, neither of them made a sound. For the first time since they’d all been together again, she felt completely alone. The satisfaction was already rotting, and as Shepard cast her eyes up to the sky, watching for the Kodiak, she couldn’t help but notice it: The Collector ship’s absence. The thing she’d been fighting for hours to reach, the key to saving this colony. It had felt like the hardest fight of her life. The wind still whipped raw against the deep burn in her back, sending horrid tingles through her. Her insides felt sore, pressed up against the medi-gel foam. She noticed the wheeze in her breath, and the weight of standing upright. The image of Kasumi’s leg, torn open by a particle beam flashed in her mind. She’d seen the others, beaten and bloodied during the fighting. All of them were battered, and at the centre of it all, was the sheer, terrible absence of their goal.

Her skin itched as the Kodiak settled on the ground. She was inside in an instant, grunting in pain as the others quickly followed. She saw Tali’s eyes on her, watching carefully. Tali bounced a little on her toes, like she was going to flee if Shepard met her gaze. Or perhaps it was something else, anticipation maybe? Shepard dismissed it, moving quickly over to the shuttle’s advanced communicator. She couldn’t stand being left in the dark any longer. Locking on to the Normandy’s transponder, she linked them up as they rocketed skyward.

“Normandy, come in.” She spoke into the headset. “Normandy, status? Is the Collector ship disabled?”

There was no answer. The itch was getting worse, and Shepard’s mind began to race. Everything they’d been fighting for was slipping away. Suddenly her Omni-tool received a message, from EDI.

[- EMERGENCY BURST UPDATE. NORMANDY STATUS: CRITICAL DAMAGE. PERSONELL INJURED. PURSUIT ABORTED: COLLECTOR SHIP SIGNAL LOST AT FTL. AREA OF OPERATIONS PACIFIED. ALLIANCE COMMS TRAFFIC DETECTED. IMMEDIATE WITHDRAWL RECCOMMENDED. -]

Shepard felt numb. After all she’d done to stop it… She’d failed those poor people, the people she’d sacrificed in the hope of rescuing them all. Their cries came back to her, making the woman grit her teeth. Her heart was pounding, threatening to drown out the ambient sounds and chatter of the worn-out team around her. The whole thing sunk in, word by word. Critical damage, crew injured. Her mouth went dry. Pressure was building, more of it every second. This wasn’t supposed to happen; she’d damn well vowed it wouldn’t. Not another Omega, not another Haestrom. Not another Jack. She’d sworn things would be different. That they would get better. Her hands were shaking, flickering biotic energy escaping them.

There was a sound behind her.

In an instant her fist struck the shuttle window, cracking the view-screen. The loud crunch filled the cabin, killing the chatter. Shepard glanced at her torn glove; it was all over before she’d even noticed. She turned around to find Tali standing there, just inches away. Tali was frozen stiff, her shining eyes wide with terror. All at once, the bottom fell out from under the commander. Crimson leaked down her fingers, dripping to the floor. Everyone had flinched at the sound, looking at her. But they might as well have not existed at all. She felt a cold sweat beading on her skin, completely unsure of what to do. Her chest ached, and her face was flushed with colour. She wanted this moment to end. But it didn’t. It held her captive, in shame and regret. When the long, agonising moment did pass, it was followed by something even worse. The others had gathered at the functioning window, their faces pressed up against it. Parting Garrus and Miranda, Shepard looked outside.

She saw the Normandy… burning.
