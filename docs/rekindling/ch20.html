Chapter 20: A Madman's Legacy

It still bothered her, even inside the safety of the Kodiak. She could feel it out there, cold and empty. Threatening to swallow her up again. They’d been flying for nearly ten minutes now, leaving the Normandy far behind, beyond the horizon. The Blue Suns fleet had finally reached its destination: A planet in the Imir system, Korlus. Scans indicated it was little more than a lifeless rock, barely habitable to the handful of settlements on its surface. Tourist sites on the extranet described it as “the galaxy’s recycling centre”. A flowery way of saying the entire planet was one enormous dump: More specifically, a graveyard for starships. With a staggering murder rate and absolutely no redeeming qualities, it was the kind of place where only two kinds of people ended up: The poor bastards who were born there, and the other kind. The latter was what Shepard and her team were here for.

The shuttle pilot’s voice chimed over the PA system. She had the mercenary fleet dead ahead, holding in orbit over the objective. The sensors of their frigates would pick them up soon; it was time to drop into atmosphere. Shepard gave the go ahead, barking the order through her breather helmet. With her features mostly obscured, the commander had taken the opportunity to close her eyes during the trip. Instead of watching, she listened as her team made ready around her. The beeping confirmation of Omni-tools, the smooth clack of armour and shield harnesses. One after another, breather helmets were fitted to everyone. Even Kasumi, the eccentric thief had forsaken her usual outfit for a customised Cerberus soft-suit. Airtight and sealed, though repainted black for her preference. Shepard’s orders had been clear for this mission. The planet was a dump, and no doubt the air was toxic. She didn’t want Chakwas dealing with lung infections for the next week.

“Eyes open, Shep.” Kasumi’s voice was low, coinciding perfectly with the shaking rattle of the cabin as it passed through the cloud layer. She patted a hand on Shepard’s arm, and the commander’s eyes opened. Before, Kasumi’s perceptiveness would’ve bothered her. But now it was welcome. The endless yawning void was gone, replaced instead by a hazy daytime vista.

Korlus was an ugly planet. Looking through the cabin window, every inch of every mile was covered in debris: A landscape of artificial peaks and garbage-strewn plains: Lakes of oil and mechanical fluids. The air was thick with smog, and the sheer heat of the place shimmered off the endless metal surfaces. There must have been thousands of wrecks here, maybe hundreds of thousands: The remains covered entire swaths of land, as far as the eye could see. Ships of every make and model, some of them probably decades old. Every now and then a splash of colour would pop from the overpowering pallet of rust and neglect: Company colours or a flashy paintjob. But the harsh sun was relentless. Nearly every identifying mark left upon the junk heap had been bleached clean. High above, the clouds that hung in the air had clearly never borne a drop of rain. The commander’s nose screwed up in disgust. She could only imagine the fetid cocktail of chemicals that dwelled within the choking atmosphere of a place like this.

Taking advantage of the shuttle’s steadying approach, Shepard let go of the overhead rail. Turning her back on the window, she looked over the ground team. Nearly everyone was present, except for Mordin. Though the professor was an adept fighter, his expertise was still required in the lab. What the salarian’s absence cost in manpower, it more than made up for in development efficiency. Plus, the old genius didn’t seem to mind. A welcome arrangement, as the commander found her hands growing fuller every mission. Garrus and Zaeed had picked a corner of the cabin, checking each other’s rifles and swapping ideas for how to approach the Suns. No doubt starting a new betting pool for kills too.

Miranda and Jacob had stuck close to each other, conversing quietly. Despite her usual subtlety, Shepard’s second in command was obviously drilling her fellow Cerberus agent over tactics. Jacob’s pained expression said it all. But he listened intently. Aside from Shepard, Miranda was easily the best tactician. Shepard had spent nearly an hour with her just before the mission began, going over what the Normandy’s scans had revealed about their target.

That just left Tali and Kasumi: The two tech specialists. By the time Shepard had turned around, the thief was already in Tali’s ear again, chatting casually. The quarian listened and nodded, even having a chuckle now and then. But Shepard caught Tali’s shining eyes glancing toward her more than once. Tali could feel it too. They were closing in. When the commander made her move forward, the quarian immediately responded. Giving Kasumi a gentle nudge, she gave Shepard her full attention, followed seconds later by everyone else. In the cramped interior of the shuttle, all eyes were on her.

“Alright.” Shepard tapped her Omni-tool, projecting a hologram in front of her. “Our radio intercepts confirm Okeer is located at this base, here.” The image shifted to an overhead mock-up of a large star-port. “From what we can tell from the scans, their main structure is protected by an outer wall. The whole thing is built into one of the wrecks, and bristling with triple A. So getting in from above is a no go, not without clearance.”

“So we go through the wall.” Garrus suggested, eyeing the hologram.

“Exactly.” Shepard nodded. “But standard munitions aren’t gonna cut it. We’ll need the Normandy to kick in the gates. But there’s a problem.” She switched the image, highlighting three towers. “The base is protected by anti-orbital guns, and obscured by a jammer. Not only that, but the fleet we’ve been tracking is sitting right on top of it. Three frigates and a cruiser, in geo-sync orbit. No way for the Normandy to get in, take the shot and get out without being noticed, and destroyed.”

“Right. So what’s the plan for all that, Shepard?” Zaaed croaked, dialling in his rifle. With a swipe, the view shifted a little, focusing on a smaller structure nearby.

“The Blue Suns have got an outpost about ten klicks from the main base: Probably another landing zone for supplies. Imagery shows vehicles stationed there. So we take a merc rover, and use it as cover to get in close. After that, we split into three teams: One for each of the guns, and the last for the jammer itself. We secure the guns, and then we reconfigure them to target the Blue Suns fleet. On my go we bring down the enemy ships, kill the jammer and open up the way for the Normandy. Once we’re inside, we grab Okeer and the shuttle picks us up.”

Shepard watched as nods of affirmation were given.

“Now, we don’t know if the Okeer is here by choice, but we all know the Suns. When they know something’s up, things will get ugly. So we keep under the radar until the anti-orbitals are taken care of. Understood?”

“These objectives are pretty spread out, Shepard.” Garrus noted, looking over the map on his own Omni-tool. “The jammer team will be closest, but they’ll be going in without backup until the others get there.”

“It’s a risk we have to take. Every minute we wait to regroup, the mercs have to kill Okeer and cut their losses.” Shepard could see the tension in her friend’s mandibles. She didn’t like it either, but both of them knew it was the right call.

After a few minutes the shuttle began to shudder and decelerate. Shepard made sure everyone checked their suits and weapons a final time before the large cabin doors opened. Even behind the auto-polarising visor of her helmet, she found herself squinting in the harsh sunlight. The offensive smog was drawn in through a calming breath, filtered out by military-grade scrubbers. With her feet firmly on the ground, the woman blinked her eyes and gripped her rifle. The others filed in behind her, familiar footfalls to her left and right. The shuttle rose into the air behind them, keeping low as it skirted over the landscape of jagged metal wreckage.

The ground team had been dropped in a sort of canyon; somewhere the Kodiak wouldn’t be spotted as it approached. The looming metal shards above were unnerving, like the bones of old monsters. Moving with purpose, the squad began to work their way through. They filed in closer together, double and sometimes single file to avoid rubbing up against the countless puncture hazards that lined the canyon walls. Each time Tali fell in beside her, Shepard found herself checking her over, searching for even the smallest tear. Naturally she’d butted the old turian out for the number two spot.

Soon the landscape opened up, revealing a large open plain of scrap. The main objective jutted up in the background, the corpse of a giant ship infested by mercenaries. But not too far ahead, lay the outpost. Carefully making their way up onto a ridge, the squad tried to get a look beyond the walls. From the elevated position, they could make out enemy patrols and defences plain as day. Personnel numbers were light, not even a full platoon stationed there. From the look of it, most of the vehicles were automated rovers. As the commander scanned the outpost, she found what she was looking for: A few fighting vehicles, painted in blue and white.

Half sliding down to ground level again, the squad approached the compound. Garrus and Zaeed had stayed behind, lining up shots on the wall patrols. Everyone kept low, suppressors fitted to their weapons. It was a game of stop and go, ears strained to the radio as either the turian or his gruff human companion guided them closer. Thanks to lacklustre training and thin numbers, getting to the walls of the outpost was a relatively straightforward affair. However the obstacle itself was another matter. Four meters of sheer vertical, with guards stationed above. Circling around the perimeter, the main entrance was spotted. A similarly sized gate; mechanically locked from the inside. No way to hack in. But even behind the visor of her environment suit, Kasumi’s grin was obvious.

With a quiet affirmative from the commander, Miranda and the thief lined up against the wall. For a moment nobody moved, waiting for the all clear. A minute went by, punctuated by the clank of boots on the rampart above. The lazy sounds drifted away, followed by a rumbling green light from Garrus. Kasumi flickered and vanished. Using her biotics, Miranda lifted the woman up and onto the walkway. She re-joined the squad at the gate, the silent workings of their infiltrator impossible to ascertain. Shepard gave Tali a look. With a final tap of her Omni-tool, a short-range hack disabled the local radios. At the same time, the magnetic lock on the gates clunked open.

“Everyone ready?” Shepard asked, flicking her rifle to burst fire. The answers came in unison, followed shortly by the sniper duo. Miranda and Jacob moved to the far side of the gate, while Tali stuck by Shepard’s side. She gave the commander a final look, priming her pistol.

“Go!”

The gates creaked and slid open, getting the attention of a few guards standing nearby. They moved to investigate, when they heard a pair of loud cracks behind them. To their shock, two of the patrolling troopers tumbled off the wall, crumpling to the ground in front of them. One of the men went to sound the alarm, when the courtyard disappeared behind a flash and a deafening bang. The whole thing was over in minutes. By the time the guards in the courtyard had responded, bursts of rifle fire and the blue flare of biotics had all but wiped them out. The stragglers on the walls attempted to line up the intruders, before crack after whistling crack put them on the ground, helmets torn open by high-calibre rounds. Shepard had learned a long time ago that numbers counted for very little against skill and experience. A team of three could bring down an entire base, and she had twice that.

While waiting for the two snipers to catch up, Shepard had the squad search the outpost. The tents outside were ransacked, stripped for supplies and weapons. Spare thermal clips were always a priority, along with grenades and other heavy weapons. She stuck her head into the outpost’s small communication relay. Tali was already hard at work, fingers flying across her Omni-tool as she accessed the base’s local network. For a moment Shepard simply admired the engineer at work. Her eyes scanned over constant new lines of code, slowly but surely worming her way in. Not only that, but Shepard had forgotten just what a god shot she was. No hesitation, clean and professional. The next step of the mission solidified in her brain when Tali finally gained access.

“Alright.” She let out a held breath. “I’ve got control of comms. Only basic level protocols, but it should be enough to keep nearby troopers silent without filling the airways with static.”

“Good work.” Shepard couldn’t resist the compliment. “Anything from inside the base?”

“No.” She grunted angrily. “It must be on a separate network. “But the fleet has been in contact. They mentioned Okeer’s name.” She looked up at Shepard with a shrug. “Something about production issues?”

“Sounds like we should get moving.” Shepard concluded, helping the quarian to her feet.

The two of them emerged in time for Garrus and Zaeed to fall in. The two snipers were chatting amongst themselves, keeping one eye on the mission as they measured kills. Upon spotting the commander, the turian made his way over.

“What’s the count?” He asked expectantly, sidling up next to them as the squad searched for a vehicle.

“Four.” Shepard thumbed to herself. “Three.” She turned the thumb on Tali.

“For now.” Tali added, eyeing the turian.

“Very impressive.” Garrus nodded, unable to contain his mocking tone. “…Eight.” He threw the number in, grinning behind his visor.

“I don’t even think they should count.” Shepard argued. “Targets waddling around on the walls; hardly seems fair.”

“Agreed.” Tali nodded. Their friend looked genuinely upset.

“So you’re gonna conspire against me now?” He scoffed. “It’s not my fault you managed rookie numbers against a bunch of…” Garrus trailed off.

The three of them had rounded a corner of the outpost, coming upon a small fleet of vehicles. The rest of the squad were right behind them, confused by the sudden stop. Jacob and Miranda leaned out past the trio, trying to get a look at what was so interesting. Zaeed didn’t even pretend to care. To them and anyone else, it appeared like they’d found exactly what they needed. Just past a pair of cargo rovers, their prize was sitting. Fully fuelled on its parking pad, with an excited Kasumi perched on top, her feet knocking against the armour. She leaned back against the cannon, throwing her arms wide with anticipation. Though helmets obscured their features, both Garrus and Tali cringed. Shepard by stark contrast, looked upon the thing with hungry eyes.

“Oh no…” Tali eked out, getting a solemn nod from the turian next to her.

By the time everyone was piled inside the Blue Suns Mako, it was far too late. Shepard had put herself firmly in the driver’s seat, ignoring the glares from her old friends as they embarked after her. Garrus took the turret, desperate to find something to keep his mind busy. Tali resigned herself to her fate, slumping down in the technician’s chair next to the driver. Miranda sat just behind them, followed by the others. With seven people loaded up, the Infantry Fighting Vehicle wasn’t quite at its full capacity. Though as the small entry hatch closed, the reality of how snug the thing was began to dawn on everyone.

Shepard didn’t care; to her this was another belated reunion: An old friend who’d lost her way, gotten mixed up with the wrong crowd. After a few seconds of reluctant hacking from the quarian next to her, operator privileges were overridden. Pressing the starter button, the armoured vehicle shuddered and roared to life. She let herself get lost for a moment, squeezing her gloved hands around the Mako’s driving wheel. Her foot rested on the accelerator, pressing down just a little to make the engine growl. Letting out an easy sigh, the commander focused again. There would be time for reunions later.

The armoured vehicle reversed sharply on its oversized wheels, heavy external suspension axels accounting for even the smallest bumps. The six wheels all moved independently, tightly turning the IFV before it rumbled forward again. By the time it had left the outpost, everyone had become painfully aware of what Tali and Garrus already knew. Shepard was an… enthusiastic driver. With her pedal to the metal, the heavy machine took off at speed down the wide dirt path that had been carved out by countless trips.

Inside the cramped interior, several sets of eyes frantically searched for seatbelts, only to find nothing but a good grip would keep them rooted to their bucket seats. The only belts on board belonged to the gunner, the driver, and the technician. The quarian and the turian had known just where to be, hoarding the knowledge from the others. But as the vehicle settled in for a short drive towards the base proper, things slowly stopped shaking and bouncing. Shepard watched the makeshift road, staying ready in case an auto-rover came their way or God forbid, an actual threat. The mood seemed to lighten a bit, and quiet talking filled the cabin of the Mako. She kept quiet, looking out through the one-way ballistic glass.

As the vehicle rounded the bend, the main base came into full view. At a distance, the layout seemed mostly similar, though on a much larger scale. It was a fortress, with high walls stretching all around the front. They anchored into the massive wreck the compound was nestled against. It towered over the base, covering it in shadow. The tops of the walls were lined with smaller anti-air guns. Not the kind of thing that could threaten the Normandy, but they could blow a shuttle out of the sky. The improvised road ran all the way up to the main gate: An enormous, heavy set of doors. Her guess was correct, no way anything less than a ship’s gun was going to punch through that.

The commander’s eyes followed a split in the road. A few hundred meters to either side of the base were smaller camps, tucked in around two huge mass accelerator cannons. The guns themselves were nearly twenty meters long each. Mounted onto heavy-duty gimbals, they could probably acquire just about any target in the sky. Planetary cannons were always the most dangerous. Without the size and power restrictions of a starship, they could be virtually any size. Scaling up the recoil resistance made these weapons deadlier than an entire nuclear arsenal. One shot could completely vaporise a dreadnought. Hell, a big enough gun could glass an entire planet.

“So… why are we looking for Doctor Okeer?” Tali’s question pulled Shepard back into the driver’s seat. She looked over to the quarian, catching that inquisitive glint in her eyes.

“According to the dossier, he’s gotten his hands on Collector technology. Only one way he could’ve done that, right?” Shepard answered with a shrug. “I’m hoping he’ll have some insight on them.”

Tali nodded, tweaking something on the Mako’s computer.

“Plus he’s… y’know, a krogan.” Shepard added with a smile. Tali giggled and nodded again.

“Wrex will be furious when he finds out.” The quarian warned. “A jealous krogan… Keelah.”

“I won’t tell if you won’t.” Shepard gave her a conspiratorial wink, before adjusting the speaker on her own helmet.

“Alright everyone, we’re coming up on the objectives. We’ll be pairing up for this, one techie and one biotic. Team one, Jacob and Kasumi.”

“Yes ma’am!” The Cerberus agent affirmed with practiced professionalism. Kasumi gave a thumbs-up, though she seemed jittery afterwards.

“Team two, Garrus and Miranda.” Shepard braced herself for the inevitable.

“What!” The two of them spoke up at once.

“Consider it a trust building exercise. Which means if you screw things up, I’m leaving your asses here.” Shepard’s tone was serious. The two forced partners glared at each other.

“Who am I with?” Zaeed asked, getting the question in first.

“You stick with the Mako. After we blow the door, you grab teams one and two, then haul ass to the base.”

“So sit on my arse then?” he clarified with a groan. “…Fine with me.”

By the time Shepard looked over at her soon-to-be teammate, Tali was already tilting her helmet at Shepard incredulously. She couldn’t see Shepard’s whole face, but the commander was grinning regardless.

“That was mean.” Tali chided.

“I can send you with Miranda, if you want?” Shepard offered, watching the engineer’s eyes widen in horror.

“Forget I said anything.” She quickly conceded.

The low chatter fell away the closer the Mako got to the base. So far nobody had seemed to notice them, even as the vehicle rolled up on the first objective. The side doors opened and Team One slunk out, quickly moving to some nearby cover. After a quick status confirmation from Jacob, the IFV set off again. By now Shepard had relinquished the driver’s seat to Zaeed, keeping her disappointment to herself. The theatrical sighs of relief from her blue and purple squad-mates only added to the upset. With their cover holding, the grizzled merc drove alone while Tali joined the commander. She’d sat down beside Shepard, taking in the awkward scene across from them. Garrus and Miranda kept a seat apart, the Cerberus officer going over the infiltration plan. Shepard wasn’t sure if Miranda could read turians like she could, but the sniper made no effort to hide his distaste for her. When Miranda prodded him with an eventual “are you listening?”, it was only the commander’s stern look that kept Garrus from snapping back at her. Shepard knew she’d be paying for it later.

Things grew tense as the Mako began to slow again. This was it, their stop. The cabin rocked a little as the vehicle came to a halt. She was up, rifle in hand. With a tap of a button, the armoured side doors opened and she clambered out with her engineer behind her. They were only a few dozen meters from the camp’s perimeter. Finding some cover amongst the mess of cargo crates and spare equipment, Shepard scanned the area ahead. The camp was smaller than the outpost, with only tents and a towable boxy control centre hooked up to the jamming tower. A few armoured rovers were parked nearby, and there were troopers all over the place. She glanced upward; the whole place was blanketed in shadow. Up close, the old wreck looked more like a mountain. Tali was on her Omni-tool, no doubt taking the moment to shore up her hack. It took the lightest brush of her shoulder before her silenced pistol was drawn.

They took it slow. Half crouched, half crawling, the two of them slowly snuck through the camp. All around the idle chatter of the enemy mixed with the crunching of boots on rusty dirt. All three teams had muted their helmets, relaying updates through internal radio. It always felt weird; having someone in your ear while a guard passed by so close you could reach out and touch them. Things were going smoothly so far, even if Vakarian and Lawson could use another lesson in keeping chatter to a minimum. By comparison, Tali had been nearly silent. She’d set up a paired channel, just for Shepard and her. The quarian’s callouts were quick and precise, allowing for as much quiet as possible: Clean and professional, always watching Shepard.

When a pair of guards had simply refused to move away from the control box, Shepard found Tali already waiting for her go ahead, boot-knife in hand. The commander pulled her own blade from its chest scabbard, and the two of them struck in unison. She grabbed the closer human, while Tali took the turian. Within seconds the mercs were dead, shuffled into the small interior. Tali took no time getting to work, hacking in to the anti-orbital systems. A minute ticked by while the two of them waited on confirmation from the other teams. Then two minutes, three minutes. Shepard gripped her rifle, eyes peeled on the doorway. They couldn’t stay hidden for long. Finally the radio crackled with success, first Garrus, then Kasumi. The guns were hacked and awaiting command. Shepard shot a thumbs-up to the engineer, and Tali sent the signal.

Even at a distance, she could hear the immense hydraulics and motors hissing and grumbling to life. With the VI’s re-tasked the quarian joined Shepard outside again. The pair watched on in awed silence as the enormous twenty metre cannons began to slowly swivel into position. Suddenly the airways filled with Blue Suns chatter: Frantic requests for updates, with the blaring of alarms in the background. The guns locked on to their targets, and fired. The ground shook as their helmets adjusted audio intake from the deafening boom. Hypersonic rounds the size of basketballs screamed up into the sky, moving so fast they looked like beams of light.

Again and again the ground shook, as the anti-orbital weapons shifted to new targets. One by one, the radio channels from above fell silent. The sky lit up with a series of blinding flashes, as fusion reactors and antimatter fuel ignited into thermonuclear fireballs. The impressive show was short lived, as guards began swarming towards them. Guns drawn, the two of them returned fire. Moving and shooting they made their way towards the base. Explosions reported from the other camps, it seemed like all three teams were fighting now. Putting some distance between them and their pursuers, Shepard radioed the Normandy.

“Joker, I need an airstrike on the base’s main gate!”

“Rodger that commander.” The pilot came through calm and collected. “You’ve got some fans behind you, want them fragged too?”

“I’d appreciate it.” Shepard smiled.

Hunkering down, the wait was barely a minute. A volley of missiles came down from the clouds, impacting with pinpoint accuracy behind them. The camp was little more than a smouldering crater. The pair watched as two more volleys came down, giving some relief to the others. Then she emerged, a winged angel dressed in white, black and gold. The Normandy swooped in low, her impressive size utterly captivating for a moment. Underneath the belly of her sleek fuselage, the main gun glowed and crackled. Then another ear-splitting blast punched through the air. The shot was perfect: A heavy slug vaporised against the base’s main gates, melting them into slag. Without missing a beat, the heavy frigate broke off, soaring overhead as she pushed back up into the stratosphere. In the wake of the airstrike, Shepard heard an absent-minded “Keelah” escape her quarian friend. She silently agreed; the whole thing had been one hell of a spectacle.

Inside the perimeter wall, the base itself was a large central building, multiple stories high. After hacking past an armoured front door, Tali followed her commander inside. It was unnerving. Whatever this place was, it certainly wasn’t a typical barracks. Power fluctuated off and on, with seemingly no way to stabilise it. In the intermittent darkness she gripped her shotgun tightly, pointing its flashlight into every side room they came across. Shepard took point ahead of her, keeping her attention on any movement. All around them the sounds of gunfire rang out. The Blue Suns were buzzing everywhere like a swarm of stinging insects. But what were they shooting at? The other teams would still be en route, unless the Mako was taking pot shots from outside. Maybe the mercenaries weren’t as coherent as she thought, and they were turning on each other now that things had gone sideways. Whatever the case, she didn’t like it. The only thing that helped was having Shepard with her. If she was stuck with only one person for backup, she was glad it was Shepard.

The hallway opened up into what looked like some kind of observation room. Not really military, almost… medical. Her train of thought was stifled by a gasp. Under the flickering lights were the bodies of a squad of Blue Suns troopers: Humans, turians and batarians, all torn limb from limb. The walls were spattered with blood, blue and crimson mixing together amongst the severed limbs and splayed out entrails. The whole scene disgusted her. Tali shut off her olfactory filters, looking over at the commander to shift her focus elsewhere. Shepard looked just as shocked as she was. The commander knelt down, examining the bodies. Turning away, Tali examined the room some more. There was a second level above them, with a catwalk. The more she noticed, the more this place felt like some kind of pit. What the hell was going on here? The quarian felt antsy, desperate to break the silence.

“What do you think happened?” She asked, helmet to helmet.

“I have no idea.” Shepard admitted, getting to her feet. “We hit them pretty hard, but this looks like…”

Something clanged against the floor, hushing the both of them. Tali’s eyes were wide as she spun around to investigate. Something huge scrabbled to its feet from a darkened corner of the room. At first she had no idea what she was looking at, beyond the sheer size of the thing. But as the creature stalked towards them, the realisation shook her: It was a krogan. Covered in blood and completely naked, the plated alien had her in its sights. She levelled her shotgun, firing two quick blasts as it charged. The pellets sunk into the hard bony plates on its chest and head, merely pissing it off. The engineer moved to sidestep the attack, slipping on the wet floor. She regained her balance in time to be bowled over by the massive enemy.

She crashed down on the hard surface, winded and groaning in pain. For a moment she laid there dazed while the krogan advanced on her. A spike of terror shot through the young woman before the room became a loud chorus of rifle fire. Shepard raced forward, dumping an entire thermal clip into the alien. Her avenger found its mark, destroying the krogan’s right eye and getting the beast’s attention. Changing targets, it charged her. Cycling the clip too late, the commander took the attack head on, losing her weapon. She stopped the krogan in its tracks, redirecting its momentum into the wall before landing two biotic punches into its side.

Tali got to her feet, shaking off the impact. Blood rage filled the aggressive alien, and it clamped its jaws down on Shepard’s arm. Shepard yelled in pain, crushing teeth slowly cracking the ceramic gauntlet open. Tali felt a surge of adrenaline as her friend held the monster off. Shepard wrenched her arm free, pulling away from the krogan. That was all Tali needed. Instinct overtook her, and Tali leapt towards it. Curling her powerful legs in mid-air; she delivered a devastating double kick. Spring-boarding off the alien, she sent it tumbling off its feet.

Before her brain could process the error of her action, she felt Shepard’s arms around her. She knocked Shepard to the ground, the fall softened by the commander’s body. For a second she laid there on top of Shepard, an arm around her waist. The two of them panted from the ordeal, before the krogan stirred again. Grabbing her rifle one handed, the commander fired a volley into the monster’s head plate. Bullets chipped away at the bony amour, until flesh peeked out. Without a word between them, Tali levelled her shotgun, finishing off the krogan with a single blast. It slumped over dead, a grisly canyon where its skull had been.

“…Nice kick.” Shepard managed the compliment between ragged breaths.

“Nice catch.” She returned to sentiment, with a strained chuckle. Tali felt Shepard’s chest beneath her as the commander laughed. The moment didn’t last long, the sounds of snarling and stomping echoed up from the hallway. More naked krogan appeared.

“Oh shit.” Shepard’s observation had the two of them upright again. “We need a way out of here!” She looked to Tali before firing at the overwhelming threat.

Tali sent out her drone to buy some time. Frantically searching the room, the pit analogy became more apparent every second. They were trapped, with only the one hallway in and out. Then the quarian’s eyes lifted, spotting their salvation. She did the math in her head, bending her digitigrade legs. She sprang into the air, clearing twice her own height and grabbing onto the catwalk above. Not wasting a second, Tali rolled onto her belly holding out a hand for Shepard. Her yell got the woman’s attention, and the human jumped as high as she could, hand outstretched.

Three fingers caught five, and the quarian grabbed on tight. The krogan were closing in, yelling and growling beneath them. Digging her toes into the metal walkway, she summoned every ounce of strength she had. Powerful legs slowly dragged Shepard up and onto the platform, the commander’s other hand grabbing the guardrail to help her. Wrapping her arms around Shepard’s chest, Tali pulled her up and over, falling backward as Shepard came down next to her. Below, the angered aliens were already turning on each other, violent head-butts breaking up the feasting on what was left of the corpses.

Not wanting to tempt fate, the pair kept moving. Whatever was going on, these crazed krogan were everywhere. The upper floors seemed to be in better condition, with lights mostly working. But unfortunately that also meant more Blue Suns. In such close quarters, it was impossible to avoid fights for long. Tali kept shoulder-to-shoulder with Shepard as they made their way up. Using the tight hallways to eliminate flanking, the two of them carved through the enemy squads. The commander’s rifle peppered the enemy with incendiary rounds, while Tali filled them with electrified pellets from her shotgun. Despite their prowess together, things were getting desperate. They couldn’t afford to get pinned down, as the roaming groups of krogan could burst in at any time, turning the battle into a bloodbath. Moving into what was now clearly a series of labs, tracers flashed overhead as the team snuck around a nearby brawl. Somewhere up above, the thunderous rattling of an YMIR’s heavy gun was laying waste to something. Tali didn’t look forward to dealing with it.

The discoveries grew only more horrifying as they went. Gestation tanks littered the higher levels. Most of them were empty, explaining the horde of aliens they’d been fighting through. But others were still occupied, some of them in the early stages of gestation. The engineer felt sick to her stomach seeing the contents inside: Some of them were just babies. None of it made sense to her. Why would the Blue Suns be lab-growing krogan? Clearly they didn’t cooperate; it looked like this small horde had done more damage than Shepard’s attack. She supposed it explained Okeer’s presence, but there had to be more going on. Whatever the case, clearly the whole thing had been a massive failure. Her thoughts turned more cynical as they passed through a trashed dissection room. These poor krogan had been taken apart, their organs harvested and catalogued: Hearts, livers, lungs and testicles. It wouldn’t have surprised her if this had been the reason, cashing on the organ market. Exactly the kind of despicable behaviour she’d expect from scum like the Suns.

The other teams had finally made it to the base, radioing in with the same shock and horror. Tali relaxed a little, knowing the squad could take care of whatever was left. Instead she focused on Shepard, who was growing tenser by the minute as they moved in on what had to be the main lab. There was an eerie quiet up here, most of the fighting going on multiple stories below. Stacking up on the door, Shepard gave her the nod. The hack barely took any time at all, and they were in moments later. The pair walked into what looked like a large observation room, with more gestation tanks lining the wall.

The quarian’s eyes were immediately on the huge robot at the far side of the room. The YMIR was an intimidating foe, even when it was dead. The machine was propped against the wall, covered in a swarm of dead attackers. Luckily, the fight had dealt with both threats at once. Her relief was broken up by Shepard’s quick strides over to one of the tanks. Crumpled beside it was an old, scarred krogan, sitting in a pool of blood. Nearby was a woman in Blue Suns armour, a large knife driven right through her chest plate. Tali could feel her heart sinking, as the commander looked the body over.

“Shit…” Shepard said quietly to herself, her head falling. “God damn it.”

“Is that…?” Tali asked the question already knowing the answer.

“It’s Okeer.” Shepard nodded, getting to her feet again. “…All this way, for nothing.”

She didn’t know what to say. A part of her wanted to make some quip, or find another reason to take it in stride. But the engineer knew why Shepard was so upset. This krogan could’ve known about the Collectors, where they were, what they wanted with humans. He’d been the most recent contact they’d had. Now that was all gone, wasted. Tali kept quiet, letting her friend have a moment to process. Instead, she studied the tank Okeer was lying beside. There were bloody handprints on the control panel, decoupling it for transport.

Inside the tank was another krogan, larger than the others she’d seen. This one was already dressed in armour, with a kind of care paid to it that clearly wasn’t present on the monsters below. Her eyes caught a blinking light on the nearby video screen. She tapped it, watching what must’ve been Okeer’s last moments alive. He used Shepard’s name, clearly knowing who had come for him. But soon his speech turned to rambling about purity and legacy, the “perfect krogan”. She glanced back at the soldier in the tank. What could possibly make this single soldier so special? The last part caught her attention, and she turned to Shepard.

“Did you hear any of that?” Tali asked, offering a concerned tone.

“Something about a perfect krogan?” Shepard shrugged, joining her by the tank. “No accounting for taste, I guess.” She forced some levity.

“Okeer knew we were coming. He used the technology the Collectors gave him to make this.” She explained, gesturing to the tank. “Maybe we should take it with us, for analysis?” The human looked at the krogan behind the glass, eyes narrowed.

“Good call.” Shepard nodded in agreement. “At least we might get something out of this. I’ll radio the Normandy.”

Tali gave Shepard a smile, watching as the commander’s own blue eyes returned it to her. Looking down at the dead doctor, she felt her shoulders fall. First Haestrom, then Purgatory. Now they’d come across another dead end. No matter the mission, it always seemed like something went wrong. Playing day care for a criminal, hoping to pull some data out of this clone. She still hadn’t asked Garrus about his scars, but the way he glanced at Shepard heightened her suspicions. Of course, none of it compared to that awful day. Being rescued from certain death should’ve been “old-hat” for her, as Shepard would say. But it didn’t feel so easy anymore, having things go wrong. It weighed on Shepard, and she hated it. They needed a win, without a catch. Hopefully wherever they went next, things would go better. Gingerly, Tali put a hand on her belly. She’d never seen Shepard move so fast before, at least not without biotics. All to break her fall? The idea put a smile back on her face. The quarian turned to thank Shepard properly, before the words died in her mouth.

“What’s wrong?” She asked, eyes widening at the sheer rage in Shepard’s eyes.

“It’s Jack.” Shepard hissed the words through clenched teeth. “She’s put someone in the med-bay.”
