Chapter 3: Reunion

The Nualli made a final bank in the frigid upper atmosphere of Freedom’s Progress. After a series of long lazy S shaped turns back and forth toward their landing zone, the quarian stealth ship had finally bled enough speed to begin its descent. Levelling out its delta wings, the Nualli dipped into the clouds. Even with the expensive momentum dampeners she’d paid for out of pocket, Tali gripped a handrail as she made her way toward the bridge.

The scout ship jostled back and forth in the ever-thickening air. An atypical approach for a modern spacecraft, utilising the atmosphere for drag had allowed the engineer-turned-captain to squeeze the most out of the IES and Tantalus core. At their rate of descent, the Nualli could stay in stealth the whole way down. Turning to the side as she entered through the tight doorway, Tali took hold of a console near Meeko’s seat.

“Anything from the scanners?” She asked her pilot.

“Well, the colony seems intact. Whatever was blocking comms isn’t broadcasting anymore. No signs of heavy combat, no smoke trails or impact craters. As far as I can tell there was no fighting at all.” Meeko gave a shrug.

“Is there anyone down there?”

“Thermal’s scattered due to the bad weather. I think I picked up a heat signature from one of the earlier sweeps, but it was below your typical range for organics.”

“That could be a good thing, maybe you caught Veetor?”

“Maybe… we’ll be overhead in a few minutes, I’ll have a better answer for you once my sensors are close up.”

Tali gave the woman an affirmative pat on the shoulder before pulling down a screen. Unable to help herself, she had engineering’s critical data streamed directly to the bridge. While gliding the thrusters were sitting at zero output, cold from the air outside. Flight surfaces were all right, none had frozen up or been damaged from the wind. Scrolling a finger down the screen, she moved the readouts to the core and IES systems. The internal heat sinks were all sitting uncomfortably close to maximum tolerance, while the core itself was wavering a little as it steadily lowered the ship’s mass the closer they got to the surface. Glancing out the bridge windows did little to relieve the tension. It was pitch black out there, with thick snowy clouds obscuring everything.

Her lip curled unconsciously at the view; ever since Noveria, she hated the cold.

“Approaching the settlement.” Meeko tiled her helmet toward Tali, never quite looking away from her readouts.

“Alright, let’s power up.” Tali gave the order.

The ship jostled again as her two main thrusters roared to life, followed instantly by a pair of smaller vertical rockets near the nose. Seconds later the Nualli’s venting segments opened, hull plating slid to expose the ship’s heat sinks to the cold air. With the help of an atmosphere they began cooling fast. Freed up from its stealth responsibilities, the Tantalus core reconfigured its mass effect field to countering the ship’s weight. Finally the powerful thrusters began to angle their exhaust nozzles downward, causing the scout vessel to hover as it coasted the last few hundred meters to the settlement. Now completely out of stealth mode, the Nualli’s sensors beamed to life, sweeping over the ramshackle town with full penetrating scans.

“Got something here!” Meeko piped up as Tali quickly joined her.

“Is it the kid?” Prazza’s voice came from behind the two women as he lingered in the bridge’s doorway. Tali beckoned the lieutenant over and soon all three of them were watching closely.

“There!” Meeko pointed at the screen. “Far side of the settlement, near the loading bay. That heat signature’s gotta be an organic.”

“Can you get us closer?” Tali asked.

“Just a second.” Meeko grunted as she tapped away at the Nualli’s haptic holograms. The ninety-meter ship slowly drifted past the abandoned buildings, coming to a stop over the bay. The infrared cameras on board flickered to life, showing the bay in grey-scale. Amidst the uniformly dark cargo containers and buildings, the unmistakeable silhouette of a quarian blazed a bright white.

“Keelah…” Meeko said to herself. “He’s alive.”

“Well done Meeko!” Tali congratulated her pilot with a firm squeeze of her shoulders. “Can we set down there and get him?”

“Hmm… no, I don’t think so. Too cluttered. But, there’s a landing pad not far from the bay.”

“Alright, we’ll land and go on foot.” Tali gave the go ahead and the Nualli shifted toward the pad.

“How do you want to play it once we’re ground-side?” Prazza asked.

“Carefully.” Tali answered. “Veetor’s probably terrified, he could be unpredictable. We’ll take the whole squad, but no shooting unless I say so. There could be humans down there.”

“But what if-”

“No, Prazza! I don’t want to tell the admirals we’re at war with the Alliance because one of your men got trigger happy, understood?” Tali’s firm tone halted any further argument, but she could see the lieutenant’s eyes narrow.

“You’re the boss, captain. I’ll get my guys ready.”

“Good, meet you on the ramp in two minutes.” Prazza gave her a nod as he slipped out the doorway. Tali turned back to her pilot again.

“Still an asshole…” Meeko remarked. Tali sighed in agreement.

“Anything else out there?”

“I sent a scouting drone up when we arrived. Right now I’m getting a lot of cold buildings, but no life signs.”

“…I don’t like it.” Tali muttered.

“Me neither. Watch your back out there.”

Meeko’s warning was sitting in Tali’s mind as she made her way past the bunkroom to the Nualli’s main stairwell. Something about this whole place felt off. She hopped down the last few stairs and headed toward Prazza and his marines, all clustered near the cargo ramp. Grabbing her shotgun and pistol from her locker, she gave her Omni-tool a quick tap and her kinetic barriers hummed into life. Armed and shielded, she gave the nod to a marine standing near the ramp’s release handle. With a tug the ship’s belly began to open, the wailing wind invading the previously quiet space.

“Alright everyone!” Tali’s voice carried clearly through the helmet radios of the marines. “We’ve confirmed Veetor’s location in a loading bay about three hundred meters from here. Since the main cargo doors are closed, we’ll circle around through the administration buildings and get in through the side entrance.”

The marines all nodded in affirmative.

“Now, we didn’t pick up anyone else on sensors, but this is a human colony. If we encounter locals, you do not fire your weapons unless I say so. If we’re fired upon, we’ll fall back and find another way in.”

“So we’re supposed to get shot in the ass and play diplomat afterward?” One of the marines complained.

“Shut it Rallo!” Prazza’s voice filled the radio. “Captain Zorah says no shooting, we don’t shoot.” The marine shifted on his toes but didn’t argue.

“Let’s bring this pilgrim home!” Tali ordered as the ramp clunked down on the landing pad.

“Keelah Se’Lai!”

Tali and Prazza exited first, stepping off the ramp into the snowy cold. Soon the rest of the squad joined them, six quarians in total for the mission. The team quickly crossed the open ground between the landing pad and the admin buildings, all of them eager to get out of the wind. The young engineer pondered how even after three hundred years, they’re damn suits couldn’t keep out the chill. Stacking up on the door, Tali took point. Grabbing her shotgun from its mag-mount in the small of her back, she flicked her wrist and the weapon unfolded to its full size. Flicking the safety off and giving it a priming pump, she signalled Prazza to ready his rifle. With a gun on the door, Tali waved her Omni-tool across the red lock hologram. Blinking to an orange colour as she hacked in, barely a second later it flashed green and the automatic door slid open.

The interior was dark, only a single light at the other end was left on. Switching on their flashlights, the squad cautiously entered. The typically samey look of the mass-produced structure was broken up by office décor, with the uniquely bland look of government bureaucracy permeating it. The uniformly coloured chairs were wheeled away from their desks, with a couple having been knocked over. The desks themselves looked untouched, with data-pads and paperwork left half-finished.

From a quick look up, Tali ascertained the building’s lights must have been left on a while. The bulbs had burned out from use. Not usually a problem for modern lighting, but the cost cutting of colony setup lent itself to unreliable amenities. She remembered the squalid conditions of Zhu’s Hope; the image of their water pump brought an unconscious shake of her head. The squad pushed on, sweeping through the connecting office before being back outside again. They kept their heads on a swivel as they crossed open ground. Years of training and experience had taught the marines to always watch for snipers.

“Tali?” Meeko’s mature voice filled the engineer’s helmet.

“I’m here.”

“We’ve got a ship inbound, headed right for us.”

That got her attention.

“Any specifics?” Tali asked.

“It’s a small shuttle, probably a Kodiak.”

“You think it’s the Alliance?”

“That’d be my guess ma’am.”

“…Great. Thanks for the heads-up Meeko. I’ll radio you when we have Veetor, out.” Tali beckoned Prazza over.

“What’s going on?” Prazza asked, warily looking around.

“We’ve got Alliance coming. Meeko just spotted a shuttle heading our way.”

“Keelah… what do you want to do?”

“For now we continue the mission. We’ll get Veetor safe, then we can link up with the human team.” Prazza gave Tali a nod as they moved into the next building.

The squad found themselves in a worker’s house. The lights flickered, holding on better than the previous pre-fab. Tali took point as they split up to sweep each room, looking for survivors… or hostiles. With one of Prazza’s team at her back she moved into a small bedroom. Her flashlight bathed the unnerving scene in cold white. This was a child’s room. Along the neat, fabricated walls were scribbles made in crayon. Pieces of paper had been taped on the wardrobe, crude drawings at hip-height for the quarians. The bed was messy and unmade, a colourful blanket left hanging off the mattress.

There were toys scattered on the floor, some placed into groups and others knocked over. A small glow caught Tali’s eye. Moving to the bedside table, she picked up a small, framed picture. The holographic image showed a little human boy with blonde hair and missing teeth, wrapped in the arms of his similarly blonde mother. She felt a pit in her stomach.

“What the hell happened here?” The marine wondered to herself, plucking one of the boy’s toys for a closer look.

“They were taken.” Tali answered, her expression hardening. “…Batarians.”

“I don’t think so.” The marine replied, placing the toy gently back where she found it. “ If it was slavers, where’s the looting?”

“Hmm?” Tali grunted, only half-listening.

“I used to chat with this Alliance vet on the extranet.” The marine explained. “He told me about slave raids he’d seen, and the colonies were always torched. Bodies left in the open, or mutilated as a warning. Four-eyed bastards would’ve taken everything not nailed down, yet everything’s untouched?”

“It… is strange.” Tali admitted.

The squad regrouped in the living room, moving through to the back door. Prazza and the others found the same things; house keys, wallets, little keepsakes anyone could just shove in their pocket at a moment’s notice were left behind. The feeling of unease was starting to set in. Where was everyone?

The quarians headed out into a small street. Heading in the direction of the loading bay, they kept close to the buildings. Tali’s eyes snapped from the rooftops to the alleys ahead of them. The closer they got, the slower the pace. After spending enough time in combat, every soldier develops that little feeling in the back of their mind: A sense that lets them know when something’s wrong; when they’re being watched.

Suddenly, movement caught Tali’s attention. Shifting her gaze to the hazy sky, she strained to make out something hanging in the air above them. It was small, and maybe fifty meters ahead. Something was blinking on it, like… running lights?

The dark sky lit up with blue muzzle flashes, the flying thing spitting a volley of shots right at her.

“Contact!” Tali yelled over the radio as her shields flickered from the bullets. Within seconds she was in cover, with her squad scattering behind her. Pulling her pistol, she peeked around the corner of a building to get a bead on their attacker. One of the marines fired a flare forward, into the night sky. The spindly silhouettes of gun drones broke up the brilliant red glare. A squadron of three darted through the air, speeding toward them. Rifle fire chattered from behind her, the other marines trying to bring the threat down. Aiming her pistol, Tali waited until a burst of gunfire hit the lead drone’s shield. When the barrier fizzled away, she placed two solid shots through the airborne turret, sending it crashing to the ground. More silhouettes appeared. Shambling out from the loading bay area came a small army of LOKI robots, their head-displays glowing a hostile crimson. Soon a barrage of gunfire came down the street. Tali ran over to Prazza, her shields taking a hammering before she slid into cover next to him.

“There’s dozens of them!” He yelled over the gunfire.

“We’ll fall back to the admin buildings!” Tali ordered.

Prazza gave a nod and the two of them coordinated the squad’s fire. Well-placed rifle bursts took down a few LOKIs while Tali overloaded another gun drone. With a break in the attack, the quarians began to retreat, moving from cover to cover. Eventually the squad put a few blocks of distance between them and the pursuing robots. Aside from the airborne drones, the other machines struggled to keep up. With the scope of the enemy now clear, they moved into a small pre-fab. Locking the doors, everyone took a moment to breathe.

Tali gave the order to check suits, followed by a tense minute of marines meticulously going over each other for unregistered punctures. When the all clear was given, Tali propped herself against the wall. Activating her Omni-tool, she checked the drone footage Meeko was streaming to her. To the young woman’s frustration, past footage was all that was left. The Nualli’s drone had been shot down shortly after the attack had happened. Leaving Prazza and his squad in the main room; Tali moved to a smaller storage closet. Somewhere she could think.

She looked over the footage. The drone had gotten a good look at the settlement, giving her a map of the area to work with. They were still about two hundred meters from the target, pushed back by the bots. The small warren of worker housing offered a secondary entry point, but it would take longer. Tali grimaced at the idea of leaving Veetor out there a second more than necessary. Thankfully the drone had managed to capture most of the enemy numbers and positions.

The locations wouldn’t be accurate for long, but the colony had a compliment of at least a hundred and fifty LOKI robots, along with over twenty gun-drones. The footage showed the majority of them in and around the loading bay, with a handful scattered through the settlement proper. A direct assault through the side entrance was too risky, even with superior tactics she’d be risking all their lives. She considered sneaking in, taking a couple marines to infiltrate the loading bay and hack the bots. A better idea, but it’d get ugly if they ran into trouble. Tali weighed her options, looking over the map again. All of a sudden Prazza yelled out behind her.

“Stop right there!”

Wheeling back into the main room, Tali strode through her squad. The marines had all gathered together, their rifles pointed firmly at a squad of three human soldiers.

“Prazza! Weapons down this instant!” Focusing her attention on the lieutenant, she gripped the barrel of his rifle, forcing it away from the visitors. Before Prazza could react, the human leader spoke.

“Lawson, Taylor! Guns down!”

Tali stopped dead. She felt a shiver run up her spine.

She slowly turned to face the human squad leader. Shepard’s attention was on her squad mates, but she was still facing Tali. She stood taller than the others, her shoulders broad. Her armour was coloured a deep charcoal grey, Tali’s eyes widening as she saw the crimson stripe running up Shepard’s right arm. There was an emblem on her chest, a letter and a number she would never forget: N7. Even behind the mask of her breather helmet, the familiar shine of blue eyes was there. For a moment Tali couldn’t say anything, she didn’t want to. To be here, on the precipice of the impossible was almost too seductive to give up. For the first time in eight months, she dared to say Shepard’s name.

“…S-Shepard?”

Tali saw the woman flinch. In an instant Shepard’s blue eyes were on hers. She saw her own realisation in Shepard’s wide-eyed stare. Shepard stumbled back, her rifle clattering to the floor.

“Oh my God…” Her words were barely more than a whisper. “…Tali.”

Her mouth went dry, whole body trembling as Shepard grabbed her helmet. Tali’s comrades aimed their weapons, causing Shepard to pause for a moment. Slowly she removed the obscuring piece of gear, it fell from her own shaking hands. Tali’s heart was in her throat, her feet carrying her step by agonising step toward Shepard. Her eyes shone wider than they ever had, looking at the woman she’d lost so long ago. She reached up a hand to Shepard’s face, unable to suppress her shivering as her gloved fingers finally brushed against Shepard’s skin. Shepard didn’t move at all, held captive by the same force that drove the quarian girl to make sure this was real.

“You’re alive…” Tali uttered the words in disbelief.

In that moment her legs gave out, and she felt Shepard’s arms around her. Shepard pulled her close, holding her upright. She buried her helmet into Shepard’s chest, sobbing quietly as she clung to her. The two squads looked on in shock, completely unsure what to do. But to Tali, they’d all but vanished. She felt sick to her stomach, her mind blazing with a million questions. She squeezed as tight as she could, digging her fingers into Shepard’s armour. She couldn’t speak, no matter how hard she tried. All she could do was cry, and hold her friend as close to her as possible. Never in all her life had anything felt so terrible… and wonderful. Two years of guilt and shame poured out of her, endless nights of that one final moment, hoping against hope that she could change the outcome. Tali felt Shepard’s hand stroking her back; the touch of someone she thought would forever be a memory. She drew strength from the embrace, finding her feet again. Slowly pulling away, she stood at full height and looked Shepard in the eye.

“I didn’t know if you made it.” Shepard’s voice shook as she spoke, tears still sliding down her face. “I was so scared you-” Her words fell away as Tali rested a hand on Shepard’s cheek. Gently she brushed away the remaining tears from Shepard’s cheeks; a smile crept onto her commander’s face. Right in that moment, seeing Shepard smile again after so long. It filled Tali with a sensation so far beyond hope, so far beyond joy she couldn’t hope to articulate it. Even after everything she’d seen and done. This moment… felt like a miracle.

Soon the cold room surrounded them again, and the two friends found themselves back in a tense standoff.

“Prazza! I gave you an order!” Tali turned to her lieutenant, her voice steely.

“This is bullshit Tali, they’re Cerberus!” Prazza pointed an accusing finger at Shepard’s squad. Paying attention to the man and woman at her commander’s side for the first time, she realised he was right. Both were clad in black and yellow armour, with the terrorist’s emblem emblazoned on their chests.

“Wha… what the hell’s going on, Shepard?” She asked; her eyes fixed on the commander. Her shoulders sunk as she rubbed a hand over her face.

“...After the attack on the Normandy, Cerberus found me in the wreckage. I was badly injured, they spent the last two years patching me up.”

“Why didn’t you contact me? Shepard if I knew you were alive-”

“I know; you’d have come for me.” She nodded. “I was in a coma… I’ve only been awake for a few hours.”

“Keelah… I’m sorry.” Tali offered her a pained look.

She turned her attention to the Cerberus agents, her eyes narrowing. “What the hell do you want with Shepard?” She demanded.

“We’re investigating attacks on human colonies, the Commander's helping us search this settlement.” The dark-skinned man, Taylor, replied.

“The quarians don’t need to know that!” The woman, Lawson, snapped.

“We don’t need another fight!” Taylor retorted.

“Agreed.” Shepard spoke up, ending the chatter. “Look Tali. I don’t like it either, but tens of thousands of people have been taken. The Alliance is too beat up to investigate and the council have been as helpful as last time. So for now, I’ve agreed to work with Cerberus. At least until I know what’s going on.” Tali felt a knot in her stomach, but she wasn’t going to abandon her friend a second time.

“You’re right Shepard. I don’t like this.” She said pointedly before turning to her lieutenant. “Alright Prazza, truce for the time being.”

“What? You’re kidding!” Prazza started.

“No I’m not. I’m in command of this mission, if you wont follow orders you can wait on the ship!” Prazza bristled, but nodded in compliance.

“What’s the plan?” He asked.

“Take your team back the way we came, try lure the enemy out. I’ll go with Shepard and draw some of the bots from your position. We’ll spread them out, then I can slip into the loading bay and hack their controls. Veetor should still be there, so we’ll pick him up after it’s safe.”

“Copy. Let’s go, guys.” Prazza roused his marines to leave.

“Veetor? What’s a quarian doing here?” Shepard asked.

“He was here on pilgrimage. He wanted to help a small settlement.”

“Sounds like a good kid.”

“Yeah… I hope we’re not too late.” Tali said, worried.

“Thanks for the backup, by the way.” Shepard said warmly.

“I’m not leaving you alone with these bosh’tets!” Tali replied. “Besides if they try something, I’ll shoot them.”

“Is that right?” Lawson scoffed, crossing her arms.

“Yes. It is.” Tali snarled back.

The squad of four broke cover from the building. Tali took point with Shepard, leading her through the winding streets and alleys of the town. After a couple of blocks, they ran into resistance. A large group of the humanoid LOKI robots were loitering in the open, just down the road from them. The two friends moved up first, getting into cover either side of the street. With the Cerberus agents behind them, Shepard gave the signal. Pulling her pistol, the quarian leaned out of cover. Her high-calibre sidearm tore through the shields of the first bot, before ripping the metal frame apart. Shepard followed suit, firing bursts from her assault rifle. Tali was surprised to see her commander’s legendary accuracy falter as several shots went wide, but barely a second later two more machines collapsed onto the snow. From overhead a pair of gun-drones hovered into view, taking pot shots at them from above. Arming her Omni-tool, the engineer sent an overload to them, catching both in the sparking blast. Seconds later Shepard and Taylor loosed their biotics, smashing the drones down onto the rooftops.

The skirmish was attracting more machines, this time FENRIS robots. Sharing a knowing glance, both Tali and Shepard pulled their shotguns. The two Cerberus agents were busy engaging LOKIs behind them when the dog-like machines were finally upon the two old friends. Working in concert they broke cover, leading the single-minded bots on a chase. With careful footwork soon both pursers was broadside caught in crossfire. Shotgun blasts barked over the winds and the rotund little robots were ripped open. Despite the situation, Tali was having fun. Fighting side-by-side with her old commander again, it was like they’d never been apart. She’d seen a fire fight on nearly every mission for the admirals, but with Shepard it was different. Together they had a rhythm, something inexplicable that guided her steps, told her where to aim and when to shoot. Battle became a dance with her… and she’d forgotten how much she missed it.

With the last of the sizeable horde of LOKI bots scrapped, the squad made haste for the loading bay. Carefully bypassing enemies here and there, resistance was indeed starting to thin out. Stopping to catch their breath, Tali noticed the divots in Shepard’s armour. She’d been hit during the fight. Tali felt anxiety in her belly, resisting the urge to ask her if she needed a break. Being a quarian, Tali had always been a little faster than her human friend. She could easily outrun any human, but her amped nervous system offered better reaction times as well. While she certainly had no love of combat, these qualities had helped her excel once she’d gotten past her hesitation.

But despite her species, Shepard had always been an incredible soldier. She matched any physical advantage Tali had with pure skill and experience, the kind of person who always had a fight under control. But this time… Tali had noticed the difference. Shepard was slower: Slower to react and heavy on her feet. Too often enemy fire had bitten into her barriers, and judging from the damage, her armour too. The respect she’d earned from Tali wouldn’t be shaken by one sloppy performance. But all the same, they could use some backup.

“Prazza, this is Tali. We’re pushing up parallel to your position, I want you to head east and link up with us.”

Nothing but static came back, a glance to her Omni-tool showed the radio connection was fine.

“Prazza, you out there?” Tali asked impatiently. There was another round of static. Just before she turned to Shepard her lieutenant answered.

“This was a mistake.” Prazza said grimly.

“What are you talking about? Just head this way and we’ll hit the loading bay together.”

“No. You’ve been compromised.”

“I’m what!” Tali yelled into her helmet.

“I’m taking Veetor out of here. My men are already at the bay doors. Don’t interfere, or they’ll shoot to kill.”

Before Tali could answer, the signal went dead in her ear. She came to a stop, anger boiling out of her.

“What’s wrong?” Shepard was beside her, keenly aware of her shift in mood.

“Prazza’s mutinied. His bosh’tets are going to grab Veetor and leave us here!” Tali spat, utterly furious.

“Reliable as always.” Lawson chimed in. Tali grit her teeth until the urge to punch her subsided.

“Knock that shit off!” Shepard bit back. “Can we beat them there?” She asked Tali.

“If we’re quick we can follow Prazza’s team through the side entrance. But…” She didn’t want to say it.

“It might not come to that.” Shepard offered. “Let’s just get there first.”

“Right.” She felt a little better already.

Moving double time through the streets, running and gunning with the remaining LOKI robots, the squad hustled toward the loading bay. They could hear gunfire up ahead, the chatter of the marines rifles, along with something bigger. Coming to a stop at the entrance, Tali’s eyes went wide at the scene. Prazza and his men were pinned down, with three massive YMIR robots firing at them. The three-meter tall machines were as wide as battle tanks, with a heavy machinegun on one bulky arm and a rocket launcher on the other. Standing side-by-side, they were blocking access to the warehouse on the far side of the bay.

Instinctively she moved to help them, but Shepard grabbed her arm. She turned to pull away, but behind the helmet Shepard’s eyes pleaded her not to go. Tali fell back into cover; well aware she didn’t have a real plan to deal with the heavy machines. The four odd squad mates could only watch helplessly as the lightly armed strike team were cut down one by one. As the last of Prazza’s team fell, the YMIRs turned their attention to the new threat. A hail of bullets began chewing into the concrete cover the team were huddled behind.

“Now what?” Lawson yelled over the din. Shepard looked over the barricade for a second, her barriers flashing from impacts as she tucked herself down again.

“Tali, got any ideas?” She leaned in as she asked her.

She knew between the three of them, even with biotics it wasn’t enough. No one had come prepared for heavy armour, and those things would shake off anything except… Tali had an epiphany.

“Meeko, you there?” She yelled.

“I’m here, Keelah what’s going on out there?”

“We’re pinned down by three YMIRs in the loading bay. I need an airstrike!”

“Copy, I’ll be up and away in a few seconds. Keep your heads down!”

Relieved, Tali turned back to Shepard. “I’ve got fire support incoming!”

“What about the kid?” Shepard asked.

“He should be fine inside the warehouse, Meeko’s a great shot!”

“Then we’ve got a problem.” Taylor interjected.

Both Shepard and Tali peeked over the cover; the sight of a quarian in the open greeted them.

“Shit.” Shepard said before being drowned out by the roar of the Nualli’s engines.

“Meeko, hold off on the shot!” Tali’s plea came too late as she saw the ship’s bow glowing with electric charge.

“I’ll get him.” Shepard said; her eyes focused on Veetor.

“What? Shepard that’s suicide!” Tali protested.

Before she could grab Shepard, the commander blazed with blue light. For a moment she could only look on, utterly stunned by the display of biotic power. There was a flash, knocking the quarian back, and then Shepard was gone. At the same moment the Nualli fired her main gun. The teeth-rattling boom was instantly followed by a quake from the hypersonic impact. The ship bucked backward before stabilising again, hovering back toward the landing pad. Meeko’s voice confirmed the shot, but Tali was too dazed to register it. She slowly got to her feet, looking over the carnage that the airstrike had wrought. All three YMIR robots had been destroyed. The Nualli had targeted the centre one, atomising it with the mass accelerator. The other two on its flanks had been ripped apart from the shockwave. The wreckage of the machines had coalesced in a small crater left behind from the impact. Everything was still, and the snow now fell silently without the wind to guide it. On the far side of the loading bay, the warehouse still stood. It was mostly unharmed, with only some debris embedded in the wall. Tali’s eyes followed the smattering of damage on the warehouse down to the entrance door… then went wide with horror.

“Shepard!”

Leaving the Cerberus agents behind, Tali bolted toward her friend. Shepard was lying on her side outside the door, her back to Tali. The quarian’s powerful legs cleared the loading bay in seconds; soon she was only meters away from her. Her chest felt tight when she noticed the small ring of debris around Shepard, she could only guess her barrier must have taken the brunt of the blast. But she could see Shepard’s pale skin peeking out through gashes in her armour, with red blood seeping from wounds. Falling by her side, Tali made another incredible discovery. Clutched in Shepard’s arms, was Veetor, unharmed. Gingerly tugging at Shepard’s shoulder, the engineer nearly passed out when she heard her groan in response. Releasing the pilgrim from her grasp, Shepard rolled onto her back, wincing from the cold ground. The pilgrim had similarly recovered, sitting up in silence, staring at the utter devastation where only seconds ago he’d been standing.

“You okay, Veetor?” Shepard asked, her voice straining. The quarian turned to look at her.

“Y-yes.” He stuttered, shaking all over. “How did… I-I was…” Shepard slapped a hand on his knee.

“Didn’t mean to scare you.” She chuckled.

“Are you alright, Shepard?” Tali asked, hovering over her.

“Sore… but I’m fine.” Shepard smiled up at her as she took Tali’s hand. By the time she was on her feet, the others had caught up. All together again, the commander turned her attention to the pilgrim. “I don’t suppose you could help me?”

Veetor led the squad into the warehouse. He explained, amidst a stream of apologies, that he had hacked the colony’s security robots. After some reassuring from Shepard, he started to recount what happened. He explained that at first, the settlement had spotted a large ship in orbit, unlike any they’d ever seen before. Already a nervous type, Veetor decided to signal for help, his broadcast escaping only moments before communication was completely blacked out. Scared, he’d fled to the loading bay.

Watching from the warehouse, he saw the enormous vessel landing, a cloud of swarming insects coming out of it. He sealed the door and watched from the security cameras as the insects descended upon the human colonists. The humans froze in place, just from being touched by one. In a matter of minutes the entire settlement had been pacified, the swarms scouring the area for any humans hiding. Then the monsters came. Veetor activated the video screens, and Tali felt a chill run up her back.

“I’ve seen these things before.” She uttered, watching in disgust as they hauled the victims into the pods.

“What, when?” Shepard asked, her eyes on Tali.

“About eight months ago. I was part of a mission to attack a pirate ship. We pulled the flight recorder for intel, and I saw the batarian slavers start filling their ship with humans. Then they were boarded by… these things.” She explained. “There were quarians on board, an asari too. And they just walked right past them, like they weren’t even there.”

“What are they?”

“I don’t know.” Tali shrugged.

“They’re Collectors.” Lawson spoke up. “Rumour has it they come from beyond the Omega 4 Relay. You could count on one hand how many people have seen them in person.”

“Collectors… well, at least we’ve got our culprit.” Shepard mused. She turned to Lawson, her expression more serious. “But that doesn’t mean they’re working for the Reapers.”

“Shepard?” Tali’s voice brought Shepard’s attention back. “There’s something else. The pirate ship, they picked up a Reaper artefact a few weeks before they met up with them. They changed their behaviour, their hunting routes. I think… the artefact told them to meet these things.” She said, a look on unease in her eyes. Shepard looked back at the footage, rubbing her brow.

“…Shit.” Letting out a sigh, she turned to the pilgrim. “Veetor, is there anything else you can tell us?”

“Uh… no, I don’t think so. B-but I studied them! Took scans with my Omni-tool; strange readings, electromagnetic, dark energy.” Veetor held out his glowing gauntlet.

“We need to get this back to the Illusive Man, let’s grab the quarian and get out of here.” Lawson’s order immediately raised Tali’s hackles.

“What the hell are you talking about?” She yelled. “Veetor is injured, I’m not letting you take him to some prison to be tortured!”

“Neither am I Tali, don’t worry.” Shepard raised a hand to calm her. “Veetor goes with Tali to the Flotilla, understood?”

“…Fine.” Lawson scowled.

“Tali, could you have Veetor’s data sent to Cerberus?” Shepard asked.

“They won’t like it, but I’ll get it to you Shepard.” Tali promised.

“Sounds good. I’ll help you get these bodies ready for pickup.”

Tali was more than happy to accept her friend’s offer. After some relatively easy hacking, the colony’s machines were back under control. In just a few minutes, the engineer had written up a custom program for the LOKI robots, bringing a few of them back to the loading bay. Together with Shepard, she began to seal away the marines in body bags, forbidding the Cerberus agents from touching any quarian body. With an immediate mood too grim for small talk, the two of them carried out the process in silence and eventually all five marines had been tended to.

With a quick tap of her Omni-tool, a squad of unarmed robots gathered around the bodies. Two machines to each corpse, their typically clumsy movements were reworked into a delicate and reverent procession, carrying them toward the now open main loading bay doors. With the large exit cleared, it was only a few minutes to the ship. Tali radioed ahead to Meeko, ordering the pilot out of her chair to greet them. When she asked why, Tali promised her it would be worth it. Upon finally laying eyes on the Nualli, Shepard let out an impressed whistle.

“That’s quite a ship.” She remarked, paying extra attention to the impressive main gun sticking out of the bow.

“You like it? I… built her myself.” Tali admitted bashfully. Shepard turned to her, mouth failing open.

“Are you serious?”

“Well I… I mean not all by myself. I had help from lots of engineers, and the admirals funded it, but…” Tali had to look away from her friend’s beaming smile.

“That… is… incredible.” She said with awe, sidling up beside her.

“Hah, thanks.” She almost giggled, quickly getting a hold of herself. After all the time she’d spent building an homage to the Normandy, she never imagined she’d get to show Shepard her own design.

“Could I… maybe, get a tour?” She asked with a grin.

“Of course you can!” Tali answered, maybe too quickly. “Keelah, it’s my ship. I get to say who comes aboard… which means your new friends can wait outside.” She added with a nudge to Shepard’s shoulder. Shepard laughed as they headed up the ramp.

“Captain, there a reason I’m down here… Keelah, what happened?” Meeko asked, looking past Tali’s guest to the bodies being brought aboard.

“Prazza and his men mutinied.” Tali answered darkly. The pilot responded with a khelish curse that Shepard couldn’t translate.

“So, who’s the human?” Meeko asked, pointing to the commander.

“This is… Commander Shepard.” Tali said the words with a lingering disbelief. Meeko’s half-lidded eyes bulged brightly in her helmet.

“Shepard… like, your dead friend Shepard?” She asked, taking it in.

“Meeko!” Tali chided.

“Sorry, sorry. It’s an honour to meet you, ma’am.” The pilot stood tall, taking a step forward with her hand raised in greeting. “Meeko’Rala vas Neema, I’m the sucker they chose to fly Tali’s little experiment.”

“She’s not too much trouble I hope.” Shepard asked with a chuckle.

“No ma’am, the Nualli is one of the finest ships I’ve ever flown.”

“I meant Tali.” She clarified, grinning. Meeko was shockingly caught off guard.

“Hardly. Tali’Zorah is a brave woman and a brilliant technician. We all owe her our lives, and the admirals owe her a lot more than that.” The mature pilot answered reverently. Tali for her part, groaned.

“Thank you, Meeko. I’m quickly realising this was a mistake.” She tugged at Shepard’s arm to continue.

“A pleasure to meet you Meeko, I’m glad Tali’s got an ace at the stick.” Shepard bowed her head as she passed Meeko.

“Pleasure’s all mine ma’am.” She returned the gesture. As the two friends made their way toward the stairs, Tali gave a shudder.

“I like her.” Shepard said quietly, returning the nudge to Tali’s shoulder.

“Keelah…” Tali shook her head, thoroughly embarrassed from the pilot’s compliment.

The tour of the Nualli was a relatively short affair. Being barely longer than ninety meters meant the trip from bow to stern could be done in a minute, but Shepard was diligent in her questions. No matter how small the question, Tali was quick to give an enthusiastic answer. She talked bout the construction of the ship, about Hal helping her all along the way. Digressing for a few minutes, she talked about her protégé’s injuries and how he’d only gone off to pilgrimage a couple weeks ago. Pausing in the mess room, Shepard said her hellos to some of the crew who were up. All of them gave the woman the same look Meeko had given, wholly unprepared to meet a legend during mealtime. Tali felt a little uncomfortable, having her crewmates gawk at her friend, but Shepard played it off well. Heading toward engineering, she could see Shepard bracing for what would no doubt be the most talking all tour. Greeting her replacement engineers before shooing them off, Tali and the commander took a long look at the drive core.

“There’s something familiar about this.” Shepard said, her hand on her chin.

“It’s a Tantalus.” Tali said, proud as could be. Shepard’s eyes widened.

“So those were IES heat sinks I spotted in the cargo bay. But… how?”

“Mostly from memory, I had to trial-and-error my way through a few things.” Tali answered, her hands behind her back. “Welcome aboard the Migrant Fleet’s first stealth ship.”

“Tali’Zorah nar Rayya… I may just have to arrest you for copyright infringement.” Shepard chuckled, shaking her head. Tali giggled.

“It’s… actually vas Neema now.” She corrected Shepard. Shepard turned to her, a warm smile on her own face.

“Congratulations.” Shepard said, shaking her hand. Soon enough the bodies had all been loaded and Meeko had found her way back to the pilot’s chair. Without any excuses left, Shepard and Tali headed back down the cargo ramp. Pausing at the bottom, the pair prepared themselves for goodbyes.

“Tali?” Shepard spoke first, holding her attention completely. “I’ve got… no right to ask you, but whatever Cerberus has gotten me into, I could sure use your help.”

Tali deflated, her change in posture sending Shepard’s eyes to the ground.

“I’m sorry, I shouldn’t have…”

“No!” Tali interrupted. “…No, it’s alright.” She took a breath.

“I want to, Shepard. The idea of leaving you in the hands of these bastards… but I can’t. I’ve got a mission too, something big. I can’t ask someone else to take my place, you know?”

“Yeah… I know.” Shepard nodded. “Anything you can tell me about it? Where you’ll be, how long?”

“Shepard…” Tali shook her head.

“It’s just… I never thought I’d see you again. I guess I’m nervous.” Shepard tried to hide the fear in her voice. Tali’s stomach knotted just hearing it.

“Alright.” She relented. “I can’t say much, not with Cerberus around, but it’s in geth space.”

“Thanks, Tali.” Shepard said, relieved. Trying to ease her friend’s mind a little more, Tali pointed to Shepard’s wrist.

“New Omni-tool?” She asked, bringing up her own.

“Yeah, woke up with it… which is a little creepy.” Shepard answered, her own glowing to life.

“Here.” Tali tapped her tool, selecting a full-breach program and sending it to Shepard. Within seconds the hand-written code completely shredded the Cerberus cyber-security. “This should stop them spying on you.”

Another tap and the two tools began to sync up. “Now we can keep in contact. You can send me Cerberus base locations, and I can blow them up.” She said with a forced chuckle. “Just like old times.”

“I’ll message you whenever I can.” Shepard said, putting a hand on Tali’s shoulder. Unable to resist, she pulled Shepard into a hug.

“Promise to come back from your mission in one piece.” Shepard pleaded, squeezing her tight.

“I promise.” She said solemnly.

Their embrace ended painfully as she headed up onto the ramp. “Good luck, Shepard.”

It had taken every last shred of self-control Tali had not to leave with her; she’d never wanted anything more in her life. In just a single moment, it felt like everything had changed. She headed back to the bridge in a daze; unable to process the sheer magnitude of what had just transpired. It felt like Shepard had been the missing piece, as though the niggling fears that had scratched at the back of her mind were finally brought into focus.

After what felt like coming up for air, now she couldn’t help but feel more trapped than ever. The galaxy was under threat again; a new enemy had risen to carry out the Reapers’ sinister work. And just like before, her closest friend was caught in the centre of it. Tali knew her mission; she knew Project Haestrom couldn’t be ignored.

But after that was done, she also knew what she had to do next, her father be damned.
