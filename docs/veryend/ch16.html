Chapter 13

The Citadel had so many secret tunnels and chambers only the Keepers knew how to access. Was the master control room among them?

Momentarily, Shepard smiled. Again, she slid one hand down her face, wiping away her tears, then sighed. “Anyways. After the Illusive Man killed himself, I was finally free of his control. On the nearby control panel, I opened the Citadel’s arms. And while they did, I could hardly stand. I was just so…so tired, so damned tired, as if I hadn’t slept in days. There was no hope he’d survive.” She gulped. “But I’ll never forget the last things he told me.”

She scooted closer to Shepard, holding her hand with both of hers. She looked deeply into her eyes. “What were they?”

Again, Jane smiled momentarily. “He said that I’d make a good mother, and that he was proud of me.”

She let out a weak laugh, then smiled. “And he was right.”

“Thanks,” Jane said, squeezing her hand. She sighed. “After that, I blacked out. I don’t know for how long, but eventually, the Catalyst woke me up.”

She tilted her head and couldn’t help but notice how Shepard said the word ‘Catalyst’, with so much acid and contempt. “The Catalyst? So wait, the whole time, the Catalyst was some kind of intelligence?”

Shepard looked away, then let out a bitter, sarcastic laugh. “The whole time, an AI in the Citadel had been controlling the Reapers and nobody noticed. When it woke me up, its ‘avatar’ took the form of a child I watched die during the Reaper invasion of Earth. And I just barely managed to restrain myself from doing something rash, from doing something I would only regret.”

“Sounds like it was trying to emotionally manipulate you.”

“It was,” she almost shouted. She huffed through her nose, then slid her hand down her face. “That arrogant, self-righteous little shit only wanted to make its ‘reasons’ for the Reaper Cycles easier to accept.”

Her pulse climbed. Her breathing quickened. Ancestors, she was finally going to learn the true reasons for so many genocides. “What were they?”

“It told me,” Jane said, “that it was created by the Leviathans to solve a problem. Apparently, the Leviathans’ countless thrall races kept destroying themselves in conflicts just like the Morning War, cutting off their ‘tribute’ to their masters. And apparently, the Leviathans originally intended for the Catalyst and the Reapers to be ‘peacemakers’ between organics and synthetics.”

Keelah…

Had her people been repeating a pattern that had gone for countless eons? She gulped. If not for Jane, we never would have broken it. “And how did that turn out?”

“Exactly how you’d expect,” she said bitterly. “No matter how hard the Catalyst tried, it couldn’t stop synthetics and organics from killing each other.” She sighed. “So in its sick, twisted fucking head, the Calayst concluded that the only way to stop synthetics from taking over the galaxy was to regularly cull space-faring races before they could even make them, to enact genocide after genocide in–”

Jane nodded, and a brief silence ensued.

Ancestors, what had been like to learn such a terrible, cosmic truth about organics and synthetics? It couldn’t have been easy. “So did the Catalyst do next?”

“It told me that, just by making it this far, its solution wouldn’t work anymore,” Jane said, “that I’ve altered the variables and presented…new possibilities.”

“New possibilities?”

Jane pursed her lips. She closed her eye, then sighed. “It gave me three choices, three ways I could end the war.”

“And what were they?”

“The first choice,” Jane said, “was an offer to Control the Reapers.”

She gasped, her eyes wide, her mouth open. “An offer to WHAT?”

“It said my corporeal form would be atomized,” Jane said, “and that my mind would become the new guiding intelligence behind the Reapers.”

She paled. She couldn’t speak, nor move. Keelah, just the thought of that scenario chilled her insides to ice and made her sick. Control the Reapers? And prove that the Illusive man was right?!

And Jane…

If she had learned that Shepard’s body had been atomized, and that a cold, emotionless imitation of her mind was controlling the Reapers, then Felz’elt would have burned away her sanity. Yes, upon hearing such news, she would have put the barrel of her shotgun in her mouth and pulled the trigger without hesitation.

Shepard squeezed her hand, and momentarily Tali smiled. I’m supposed to be comforting you, not the other way around.

She exhaled. “What was the second choice?”

Again, Shepard let out a bitter, sarcastic laugh. “The second choice was something called Synthesis.”

“Synthesis?”

“Again, my body would have been atomized,” she said. “But this time, my mind would have been wiped from existence. And somehow, the Crucible blast would have used my DNA to make every organic part synthetic, and every synthetic part organic, connecting them all in this…galaxy-spanning hive mind.”

She looked at the ground, her eyes wide. Ancestors, how was something so fantastical and ridiculous even possible? It had to have been a trap, a ruse for something else. But what?

And Jane…

If Shepard had been naive enough to believe the Catalyst’s lie, and had been totally erased from existence to doom everyone to a dystopian nightmare Tali couldn’t imagine, then Felz’elt would have ravaged her mind and scorched away her sanity until nothing remained. Likely, she would have become too insane to even commit suicide.

“Yeah,” Jane said, “it’s as bad and crazy as it sounds, and that fucking god-child believed that this was the ideal choice, that somehow synthesis would just magically fix everything.”

She looked at Shepard once more. If the Catalyst had presented her lifemate with such insane, unthinkable choices, then what could possibly be the last one? “And the last one?”

Jane looked away, staring at the ground. In Jane’s eyes, she found only fear…

And shame.

Such terrible shame, the kind that ravaged one’s self-esteem until only scraps remained.

What are you ashamed of? Undoubtedly, Shepard was responsible for EDI and the Geth’s demise. Undoubtedly, they deserved to see the end of the war, the end of the Reapers. But surely, their sacrifice had to be necessary.

Right?

“The last choice…” she said. She gulped, then pursed her lips. She slid her hand down her face and wiped away her tears. “The last choice was…”

“Jane,” she said. She leaned forward and put one hand on Shepard’s cheek, staring deeply into her eyes. “Whatever it was, you don’t need to feel ashamed to tell me. You’re my lifemate, my other half. So there’s nothing you can do that would ever stop me from loving you.”

She gasped, her eyes wide, her mouth open. “Everything?”

“Yes,” she said. “Everything. EDI, the Geth, the Reapers, and every AI in the galaxy. At the cost of some damage to the relays.”

“Oh…” she said, putting one hand on her vocalizer. “Jane, I…I can’t imagine…”

“They trusted me…” she said, “...and I betrayed them.” She pinched the bridge of her nose, then let out a few quiet sobs. She sniffled, then wiped her tears and exhaled. Once more, she looked at Tali. “And it wasn’t just them.”

“There were more?”

“So much more,” she almost shouted. She opened up her omni-tool and input a few commands. Within seconds, Tali’s omni-tool beeped, and in her inbox, she found some files, some reports on the status of three uncontacted races. Quickly, she skimmed through them, only to be left utterly speechless.

Is this what you’ve been carrying, Jane? A cold, hollow sensation racked her chest, and she couldn’t help but shed a few tears. Keelah, no wonder her lifemate had been in so much pain. No matter what, she’d do anything possible to help Jane cope with it, to help her forgive herself and realize that she deserved to be happy.

“I’m a monster, Tali,” she said, her voice choked with emotion, “one of the worst in galactic history. And everyone keeps praising me – calling me a god damn hero!” Grimacing, she wiped her face of tears. “I told…” She sniffled. “I told the Coalition leaders that I did it for the ‘greater good’ and because it was necessary. But I didn’t give them a complete answer – the main reason why I did it.”

She tightened her grip on Shepard’s hand. “And what was the main reason?”

“I…I did it because…because…” Again, she pinched the bridge of her nose, then burst into a brief fit of choking sobs. She sniffled, then took a deep breath. “I did it because I couldn’t bear the thought of you suffering alone in some dystopian hell. Because I’d sooner see everything burn than let the Reapers get away with it!”

She broke down into a sobbing mess.

Without hesitation, Tali wrapped her arms around her lifemate and embraced her in a warm, tight hug. She also burst into tears, and for what felt like hours, Jane clung to her with desperate tightness, pouring out the emotions she’d kept buried since their days on the SR-1.

Eventually, Jane’s sobs lessened, and she calmed down. When she did, Tali gently pulled away, then held Jane’s face with both hands. With one hand, she began stroking her cheek, wiping away her tears.

Ancestors, what could she tell Shepard to make her guilt at least bearable? Think, you bosh’tet. Think.

Maybe her lifemate would feel better about her decision if she knew how much worse the others were. “Now, to be honest, I can’t thank you enough for refusing to choose synthesis or control.
